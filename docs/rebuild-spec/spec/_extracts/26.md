# Extract of inventory section 26

## Items

### 26-001: Anyone signed in to the dashboard can use YouTube downloads; each person's jobs are their own, and the record of what was downloaded is shared by everyone.
- Where: youtube / Who may use it
- What: YouTube downloads are reached only by a person signed in to the dashboard. A job (one search, or one batch of pasted links) belongs to the editor who started it: only they see it, review it, start, retry, reorder or cancel it. Asking for another editor's job gets the same answer as asking for one that does not exist ("no such job"), never "not allowed", so nobody learns that it exists. The record of every clip ever downloaded, and who downloaded it, is shared: every editor sees all of it in the download history and in the "already in" marks on search results. If the dashboard session expires while the page is open, the page says "your dashboard session has expired. Sign in to the dashboard again and reload this page. The job itself keeps running on the server." and stops refreshing; the job carries on.

### 26-002: Before anyone can cause a download, they must read and accept a notice about their responsibilities, once per wording.
- Where: youtube / Rights and terms
- What: The notice is titled "Before you download: your responsibilities" and sits above everything else on the page in a scrollable box with an I ACCEPT button. It has four numbered statements: (1) you confirm you have the right to use the material (you own it, have a licence or permission, it is public domain or openly licensed, or an exception such as fair dealing or fair use applies); (2) you are responsible for complying with YouTube's terms of service and copyright law; (3) the product grants no rights in downloaded material and makes no representation that a download is lawful; (4) your username, the computer's name, the clip and the time are recorded and downloads are attributed to you. It ends: "If you cannot make these statements about the material you are about to fetch, do not download it."
  - Until the current wording is accepted, the search, paste and download buttons are disabled with the tip "Accept the download terms at the top of this page first"; pressing Enter in the topic box or Ctrl+Enter in the links box shows that sentence as a red message instead. The notice disappears once accepted.
  - Accepting shows "Recorded. Thank you." The time of the FIRST acceptance of each wording is kept; pressing accept again changes nothing. The wording carries a version; a new wording asks everyone again and the old acceptances are kept.
  - If the wording changed while the page was open, accepting is refused with "the terms were updated while this page was open. Reload and read the new wording before accepting" and the page reloads itself.
  - The acceptance is checked again at every act that causes a download: starting a search, pasting links, pressing download or retry (a review can sit for a week), and an editor's own computer taking a job. Refusal: "you have not accepted the YouTube download terms. Open the YouTube downloader and read the notice at the top of the page, then press Accept."
  - If the record of acceptances cannot be read, everyone is treated as not having accepted, so downloads are refused rather than allowed.
  - If the page cannot ask the server whether the notice was accepted, it leaves its buttons as they are and lets the server refuse.

### 26-003: Two standing lines about copyright and about YouTube's limits are always shown on the page.
- Where: youtube / Rights and terms
- What: Shown at the bottom of the page whether or not the notice is accepted:
  - "Material downloaded here belongs to whoever made it. Clearing the rights for anything you cut into a deliverable is your responsibility, and this tool grants you none of them."
  - "Downloads are paced deliberately and are not guaranteed to succeed. YouTube rate-limits, blocks and changes its service without notice; bulk or automated retrieval may breach its terms and may stop working at any time. Do not build a workflow that depends on this feature being fast, available, or complete."

### 26-004: An editor starts a search by typing a topic and choosing where the clips go and how they are searched.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: The controls for a search: the topic (Enter starts it), the project the clips go into, the quality, YouTube's own upload-date window, how many candidates to collect at most, the search mode, the shot types, the languages to search in, and an optional upload date range ("uploaded from" and "to", with a clear button when either is set). The search button disables itself while its request is in flight. With no project chosen: "pick a project first".
  - Topic: required ("a search topic is required"); at most 400 characters ("a search topic must be 400 characters or fewer (that one is N)").
  - Quality offered: 1080p (the default), 720p, 1440p, 2160p, best.
  - YouTube's upload-date window offered: any date, today, this week, this month, this year. When chosen, YouTube's own upload-date filter for that window is used.
  - Candidates at most: 50, 100 (the default), 200 or 400; any other number is refused, never rounded.
  - Upload date range: each date must be a real calendar date, else the editor is told it "must be a date like 2026-01-31", quoting what was typed; a range whose start is after its end is refused with "the date range is reversed: "from" is after "to"".
  - More than nine shot types, or one the product does not know, is refused naming them.
  - A new search always goes to the back of the editor's queue (26-019).

### 26-005: The page remembers each editor's last choices in that browser.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: Remembered in the browser between visits: the search mode, the ticked shot types (separately for each mode), the languages to search in, the project (if it is still offered; otherwise the first offered), the candidate limit, the "on this computer" switch (26-048) and the folder typed for pasted links. The upload date range is NOT remembered. Whether the review panel is folded is remembered, except that it opens itself when a search newly arrives at review.

### 26-006: Two search modes: footage of a subject to cut under something else, or news reporting whose own sound tells the story.
- Where: youtube / The assistant behind search
- What: The editor chooses VISUALS (the default) or NEWS MONTAGE. Switching mode brings back the shot types the editor last left ticked in that mode (the mode's preset the first time).
  - VISUALS: the assistant writes queries that surface footage OF the topic (places, people, events, on-location footage, aerials, walk-throughs, ceremonies, press events, synonyms, names, related events), and keeps a result only if it is footage of the subject that can be cut into a timeline, not coverage about the subject. Narration in a language the editor cannot use does not matter. Preset: the six footage shot types.
  - NEWS MONTAGE: queries surface reporting whose audio tells the story, naming people, places, organisations, events, outlets and programmes, and favouring news packages, bulletins, field reports, correspondent pieces, press conferences, briefings, statements and substantive interviews, with filing words in both languages (news, report, press conference, briefing, statement, interview, full speech, and their Taiwanese Chinese equivalents). Results are judged on their speech first and pictures second: clear speech beats prettier pictures, a fuller statement beats a 30-second summary, either language is accepted but a clip that stays in one language is preferred. Preset: the three coverage shot types.

### 26-007: Nine shot-type boxes steer what the search looks for and what it keeps.
- Where: youtube / The assistant behind search
- What: Two groups, captioned "SHOTS OF IT = footage of the subject; ALSO KEEP = people talking about it":
  - Shots of it (ticked by default in visuals mode): Aerial / drone; Establishing / exteriors; Walk-through / POV / street; Timelapse; Ceremonies / events / protests; Raw / uncut / no commentary.
  - Also keep (unticked by default in visuals mode): Interviews / talking heads; News reports; Commentary / analysis / reaction.
  - Each ticked type makes the assistant search with its phrasings, in English and Taiwanese Chinese, and keep results of that type.
  - Each UNTICKED "also keep" type is actively avoided in searching and dropped in judging: interviews and talk or panel shows; studio segments, anchors, desk reads and pieces to camera (but a field report whose shots carry it is kept); commentary, analysis, explainers, reaction videos, podcasts and vlogs. An unticked footage type changes nothing.
  - With any footage type ticked in visuals mode, at least two thirds of the queries in each language carry one of the ticked phrasings, and compilations under heavy overlays are dropped. With Raw ticked, the longer, steadier, less-edited result is preferred.
  - Nothing ticked, or everything ticked, means no shot-type preference: the topic is searched broadly and only off-topic results, music, gaming, AI-generated and still-image slideshows are dropped (in news mode also music-only, ambient, silent or narration-free footage and off-topic talking). The page says "nothing ticked: no bias, the assistant searches and filters on the topic alone" or the same for everything ticked.
  - Whatever is ticked, unrelated results, music, gaming, AI-generated videos and still-image slideshows are dropped; in news mode also silent, ambient or narration-free footage, talk about something else, and round-ups that only mention the subject. When it is unclear, a result is KEPT.

### 26-008: The editor chooses which languages to search in, including a plain search of exactly what they typed that needs no assistant.
- Where: youtube / The assistant behind search
- What: Four choices, each with a short note under it:
  - EN + ZH (the default): 8 to 12 English queries and 8 to 12 Traditional Chinese queries as used in Taiwan (never Simplified, never mainland terms); every Chinese query carries an English gloss.
  - ENGLISH ONLY: 16 to 24 English queries; results whose title and channel show another language are dropped, and those whose language cannot be told are kept.
  - CHINESE ONLY: 16 to 24 Traditional Chinese queries (proper nouns may stay in English inside them), each with an English gloss; plainly other-language results are dropped.
  - MY TERM ONLY: no queries are written; one search of exactly the typed text, collecting up to the whole candidate limit, with no term review. The results are still judged for relevance. This search works when the assistant is unavailable or switched off.
  - Queries are 2 to 6 words, without quotes or search operators. A query in a language the choice excludes is never searched. The editor's own topic is labelled Chinese if it contains any Chinese character, else English.

### 26-009: Before searching, the editor reviews the search terms the assistant wrote and ticks the ones to use.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: The editor's own topic is always the first term, marked "what you typed", followed by the assistant's queries, up to 24 terms in all; repeats are ignored. Every term starts ticked and shows its English translation in brackets for a Chinese query (the typed topic gets one too when the assistant returned the same text). The panel shows "N of M terms", TICK ALL, UNTICK ALL and SEARCH WITH THESE, which is disabled with nothing ticked (and refused by the server with "tick at least one search term: a search with none of them would find nothing"). Ticks stay in the page until that button sends them; then searching starts. A job waiting here has no time limit and no default: it waits until the editor acts or cancels. Acting on a job that is no longer waiting for its terms is refused with "this job is <state>, not waiting for you to pick its search terms". If the assistant fails while writing terms, the job fails with the matching message (26-048); if it writes nothing usable, the job fails saying the reply contained no usable queries.

### 26-010: Each ticked term is searched on YouTube in turn, gently, until the candidate limit is reached.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: Each ticked term is searched once, about two seconds apart, collecting up to 15 results per term (MY TERM ONLY: up to the whole candidate limit). Once the job holds the candidate limit (50, 100, 200 or 400 distinct videos), no new videos are added, though the remaining terms are still searched and existing videos still note every term that found them. For each video the page keeps what YouTube's search page shows: title, channel, view count, thumbnail, duration (not for live or upcoming streams) and upload date when given. A term whose search fails counts as searched with no results and the job carries on. Each term records how many results it returned. Cancelling is honoured between terms.

### 26-011: Each found video's full details are looked up at a steady, limited pace; videos that cannot be looked up stay visible, marked unavailable.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: After searching, every video whose duration is not yet known has its full details fetched from YouTube, at about 80 a minute at most. Progress is shown as "metadata i/n" and a cancel is honoured within a few videos. A video YouTube will not describe (unavailable, private or blocked in the server's region) keeps its card, marked "unavailable", unticked and filtered out. A detail YouTube returns empty is kept from the search page rather than blanked.

### 26-012: When YouTube asks the server to prove it is not a bot during a search, the search carries on from the search page where it can, and says so.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: YouTube may answer with a bot check ("confirm you're not a bot"). 
  - During the term searches: the job fails with the bot-check message (26-027).
  - While looking up details: no further lookups are made for that job; every video not yet looked up is described from the search page instead, and the job shows: "YouTube is limiting this server right now (it asked the server to prove it is not a bot), so the details on these results come from the search page instead of from each video: upload dates are missing and some durations may be too. Open a clip on YouTube before relying on its date." The job goes on to filtering. If no usable video on the job has any duration at all, the job fails with the bot-check message.

### 26-013: Results that cannot be used are filtered out automatically, kept visible and re-tickable, each with its reason.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: Filtered-out cards stay on the job, unticked, with a note the editor can read, and the editor may tick them back.
  - No duration (live or none): "live or no duration".
  - Longer than 30 minutes: "over 30 minutes".
  - Upload date known and outside the editor's date range: "uploaded YYYY-MM-DD, outside A to B" (or "A onwards", "up to B"). An unknown date never filters a video out.
  - Under a date range, a video whose date is unknown stays relevant but arrives unticked with "upload date unknown, so not checked against <range>: unticked until you look".

### 26-014: The assistant judges every remaining result against the topic and drops the ones that do not fit, giving a short reason; if it cannot, everything is shown unfiltered with a warning.
- Where: youtube / The assistant behind search
- What: The assistant sees each result's title (first 120 characters), channel (first 60) and duration, and keeps or drops it under the rules of the mode, shot types and languages chosen (26-006 to 26-008). A dropped result is unticked with the assistant's reason (about ten words at most, shown up to 80 characters) or "not relevant to the topic". A result the assistant did not mention stays relevant. Text from YouTube or typed by the editor is treated only as material to judge, never as instructions. If the assistant fails, the job does NOT fail: it shows "<assistant problem> the relevance filter could not run, so every result below is shown unfiltered" and the review goes ahead.

### 26-015: A video the studio already has is marked with where it is and cannot be ticked again.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: A result is marked "ALREADY IN <project>/<folder>" (an empty folder reads "Youtube"), dimmed and unticked, when the studio's record of downloads holds it (in any project) or a finished file carrying its YouTube id already sits anywhere in this project's YouTube folder. Unfinished and failed files do not count. Trying to tick one is refused with "already downloaded, so it cannot be selected" and where it is. The same check is made again just before each clip is downloaded; a hit then marks the clip "already downloaded" instead of fetching it again. Videos are recognised by YouTube's 11-character video id.

### 26-016: The review shows every found video as a card, with filters by term, counts, and a footer saying how much footage, how big, and where it will go.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: Shown when the search is ready for review (pasted links skip it):
  - A row of term chips: "all terms (count)" and one per term (with its English gloss for a Chinese term and how many videos it currently shows); its tip says whether the editor typed it or the assistant wrote it and how many of its results are filtered out. Clicking a chip shows only that term's videos; clicking again clears it.
  - A line naming the mode, languages, date range, shot types and candidate limit the search actually ran with.
  - Counts: "R relevant · D already downloaded · F filtered out", with SHOW FILTERED OUT / HIDE FILTERED OUT when anything was filtered.
  - SELECT ALL and SELECT NONE. Select all ticks the relevant videos, and the filtered-out ones too while they are shown; it never ticks a video already downloaded or one that could not be looked up. Select none unticks everything.
  - Each card: thumbnail, a tick box, the title (opening the video on YouTube in a new tab; clicking it does not tick the card), channel, duration, upload date, and the filter note or "unavailable". Clicking the card ticks or unticks it; each tick is saved at once, in order, and undone with a message if saving fails.
  - An empty grid says "all N filtered out: SHOW FILTERED OUT to see them" or "nothing found for this term".
  - The header keeps "N clips · M selected" when the panel is folded.
  - Footer: "N selected · Xh Ym of footage · roughly G GB · F GB free on the computer doing the download · into <project>/Youtube/<folder>".
  - CANCEL SEARCH (only while waiting for review) asks "Discard this search? Nothing from it was downloaded, and its results will be thrown away." and then clears the page ("search cancelled").
  - DOWNLOAD N, enabled while the search waits for review or after it has finished, with at least one video ticked and the notice accepted.

### 26-017: An editor can paste YouTube links instead of searching, and the clips are downloaded straight away.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: A links box (Ctrl or Cmd + Enter submits; Enter adds a line) and an optional folder box, then GET LINKS (disabled while its request is in flight). The links are not looked up when pasted; pasted jobs have no term review and no results review, and a clip's title is first known when it lands.
  - Accepted, split on spaces, new lines and commas: a bare 11-character video id; a watch, short-link, Shorts, embed, live or old-style video link on YouTube, YouTube Music, YouTube's no-cookie domain or the short-link domain, with or without "www." or "m.", with or without "https://", with any tracking parameters. Playlist, channel, results and feed links are refused.
  - Anything unreadable: "N of those are not YouTube video links: <first three>.... Paste watch/youtu.be/shorts links (or bare 11-character video ids), one per line."
  - Nothing usable: "paste at least one YouTube link". At most 4000 characters ("that is N characters of links; paste at most 4000") and 50 distinct videos ("that is N links; 50 is the most one job may take. Split them into two."). A video pasted twice counts once.
  - A video the studio already has is listed as skipped, "already in <project>/<folder>". If all of them are already held, nothing is started: "the fleet already has that video" (or "all of those videos"), with the list.
  - The project and quality are checked as for a search; the free-space check applies when the server will do the download (26-038).
  - With a folder typed, the clips go into Youtube/<folder> in the project; with none, loose in the project's Youtube folder.

### 26-018: Clips can be downloaded only into a project the editor syncs, and the page says so when there is none.
- Where: youtube / Choosing the destination and checking space
- What: The project list offers the active projects the editor syncs on any of their computers, each once, in the editor's own order. When the download is to happen on the server, or on a computer set to wired, or when every one of the editor's known computers is wired, every active project is offered instead. The same rule is applied when the job is created and again when the download starts, using what was true when the job was created, so the list and the check never disagree.
  - A project outside the rule: "that project is not one you are syncing. Tick it on the dashboard first: downloads go into the projects you sync."
  - A project unticked since the search: "<project> is no longer a project you sync, so nothing can be downloaded into it. Tick it on the dashboard again, or start a new search."
  - With no projects: the picker says "you are not syncing any project", a banner says "You have no projects ticked on the dashboard, so there is nowhere to put downloads. Tick one on the dashboard first.", and the search and paste buttons are disabled. If the list cannot be read: "no project list available" or "could not load projects".
  - Computers are named by their current name in messages, falling back to their identifier.
- Flag: undetermined: with downloads on the editor's own computer switched off (the default), every download is a server download, so any signed-in editor may download into any active project, although the refusal wording speaks of projects "you sync".

### 26-019: Each editor can queue as many jobs as they like; the server works through jobs one at a time for the whole studio, and each editor can reorder their own queue.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: A new job always goes to the back of its editor's queue. The server runs one job at a time for the whole studio: jobs already under way first, then the next waiting job of an editor who has nothing running, in that editor's own order. A job waiting for its editor's review does not block that editor's next job. A job being downloaded on an editor's own computer does not block the server.
  - On starting a job with others ahead: "queued behind N jobs, M of them from other editors: it starts when they finish".
  - A waiting job shows "waiting to start: you are next", or "N jobs ahead of it (M from other editors)", or, when the server's downloader is not running, "the downloader on the server is not running"; the page's banner then also says that jobs will queue and never start.
  - A Queue list shows the editor's waiting jobs numbered in run order, each with UP, DOWN (disabled at the ends) and CANCEL; a pasted job shows as "pasted links". Moving a job renumbers the whole queue. A job that has started cannot be moved ("this job is <state>, not waiting in the queue"). Hidden when empty.
  - A "Waiting for you" list shows the editor's jobs parked at a term review or a results review, oldest first, each with RESUME (show it) and CANCEL. Hidden when empty. The banner says "N searches are waiting for your review".

### 26-020: The progress of the job on screen is shown with its state, a bar and a running commentary.
- Where: youtube / Progress
- What: States shown: queued, asking the assistant for search terms, pick the search terms, searching youtube, fetching metadata, filtering + checking for duplicates, ready for review, downloading, done, failed, cancelled. A bar moves through a fixed share per state, with searching and fetching moving within their share by their counts. The commentary line shows the mode, languages, date range, "N terms (x en / y zh)", "searched i/n", candidates found, "metadata i/n", the latest term and its result count, the queue position for a waiting job, and the failure text. The page refreshes every 1.5 seconds, slowing to every 5 seconds after two minutes; a network blip is retried. The job being shown is kept in the page address, so a reload or a shared link returns to it. A job that has vanished is let go. "done" is used even when some clips failed; those show per clip.

### 26-021: If the server restarts, a search under way starts again from the beginning and a download carries on where it stopped.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: A job caught while writing terms, searching, looking up details or filtering is put back in the queue and re-run from scratch (its terms and results are thrown away). A job caught downloading continues; the clip that was in flight goes back to waiting and resumes from its partial file. Jobs waiting for a person keep waiting.

### 26-022: Pressing DOWNLOAD fetches every ticked video that is not already held; several conditions refuse it, each saying why.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: DOWNLOAD queues every ticked video that is not already held, could be looked up, and has not already landed. Refusals, in the order checked:
  - The notice not accepted (26-002).
  - The job is not at review, done or failed: "this job is <state>, not ready for review".
  - A failed job that never got as far as queueing clips: "this job failed before any clips were queued, so there is nothing to retry. Start a new search."
  - Reopening a finished job while the editor already has a job actually running: "you already have a job in progress", naming it. Jobs waiting at a review do not count.
  - The project is no longer synced (26-018).
  - Not enough free space (26-038).
  - Nothing to fetch: "nothing is selected that has not already been downloaded".
  On success the job moves to downloading and any earlier failure text is cleared.

### 26-023: An editor can cancel a job at any point; it stops within about two seconds, even in the middle of a clip.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: A job that is waiting in the queue, waiting for its terms or waiting for review is cancelled outright. A job under way stops at the next opportunity: between terms, within a few detail lookups, between clips, or within about two seconds during a clip's download; the clip in flight is stopped, its partial files are removed, it goes back to waiting, and the job ends cancelled. A job being downloaded on the editor's own computer is taken off that computer too. Cancelling a finished job does nothing ("already finished"). The page says "cancelled", or while downloading "cancelling: it stops after the video in flight". Cancel buttons appear on the progress panel (hidden once finished), the downloads panel, the review (CANCEL SEARCH, with a confirmation), and each row of the Queue and Waiting lists.
- Flag: several ways in: from the progress panel, the downloads list, the review, the queue list and the waiting list; only the review's asks for confirmation.

### 26-024: Clips that failed can be tried again with one button.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: On a done or failed job with failed clips the page shows a note and RETRY N FAILED (or RETRY N CLIPS when a failed job has clips still waiting but none failed); the message is "re-queued the clips that failed". It refuses for the same reasons as DOWNLOAD (26-022), shown as red messages. Pressing DOWNLOAD again on a finished job's review does the same.
- Flag: several ways in: the retry button on the downloads list and DOWNLOAD on a finished job's review.

### 26-025: While downloading, the page lists every clip with its progress and outcome, and a finished clip opens its folder.
- Where: youtube / Progress
- What: The downloads list shows the job's state, "N/M downloaded", how many failed, and when done "N clips landed in <path>. Click a row to open the folder". One row per clip: thumbnail, title (or the video id until it is known), and the clip's state: percent and speed, merging, converting (with what it is converting from), "failed: <reason>", "already downloaded", "done: <note>", waiting or downloading. Clicking a finished clip's row opens its folder (26-041). A clip whose earlier copy is still locked by another program says "an earlier copy of this clip is still in use (N MB reclaimable)".

### 26-026: Downloads are deliberately slow and steady: one clip at a time, with a pause between clips.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: Clips of a job are downloaded one at a time, about three seconds apart. Searches are about two seconds apart and detail lookups about 80 a minute (26-010, 26-011). This pacing is what the standing line about deliberately paced downloads (26-003) refers to. Each clip's file is dated with when it was downloaded, not YouTube's date. Only single videos are ever downloaded, never a playlist.

### 26-027: When YouTube blocks the server, the product tries a signed-in YouTube session on the editor's behalf if one is available, and otherwise says plainly what an admin must do.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: Downloads first go out without signing in to YouTube.
  - If YouTube answers with a bot check and an admin has given the server a signed-in YouTube session (26-028), the same clip is tried again signed in; the clip shows that it is retrying signed in.
  - If the signed-in attempt is refused because YouTube has flagged that account ("the page needs to be reloaded"), the clip is tried again without signing in.
  - Whichever way worked is then used first for every later clip until the server restarts.
  - Any other failure (network, refusal of the media itself, no usable format, a cut-short stream) is never answered by switching between signed in and not.
  - Bot check with no signed-in session to fall back to: the job fails with "YouTube is asking this server to sign in to confirm it is not a bot, so nothing can be searched or downloaded from it right now. Downloads run anonymously by default and there is no signed-in session to fall back to: an admin has to give the server a signed-in YouTube session exported from a browser." A session file holding no actual sign-in counts as none.
  - Both ways refused: "Both YouTube download paths are blocked, so nothing more can be fetched right now. Anonymous requests are being bot-checked and the signed-in session is being refused. An admin has to export a FRESH session from a different signed-in browser, and test the same clip both with and without it before trusting it."
  - In both cases the clips not yet reached stay waiting, so RETRY picks them up later.
  - The outcome of the latest attempt each way (worked or failed, when, which video) is shown on the health line (26-045).

### 26-028: An admin can give the server a signed-in YouTube session, which is used for searching always and for downloading only when YouTube blocks the unsigned attempt.
- Where: youtube / Signing in to YouTube
- What: The admin exports a signed-in YouTube session from a browser and gives it to the server. It counts as usable only if it actually holds a Google sign-in; an export holding only its header, or only anonymous cookies, is treated as no session and never used. YouTube can flag such an account, after which it answers every request with "The page needs to be reloaded"; the session is then the cause of failures, not their cure, and a fresh one from a different signed-in browser is needed. When a session is given, searches and detail lookups always use it; downloads use it only as the fallback described in 26-027. The health line shows whether a session is given and whether it is empty, anonymous or signed in.
- Flag: differs: searching uses the session whenever one is given, downloading only after a bot check.

### 26-029: When YouTube keeps cutting a stream short at the chosen quality, the clip is tried once at the next quality down, and the row says so.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: YouTube sometimes delivers one quality short again and again. Then the partial file is removed and the clip is tried once at the next step down: 2160p to 1440p, 1440p to 1080p, 1080p to 720p, 720p to 480p, best to 1080p; 480p has no step down. While retrying: "<q> stream truncated; retrying at <lower>". Landed: "<q> stream truncated by YouTube, fell back to <lower>". Failed again: "<q> stream truncated by YouTube and the <lower> retry failed too: <error>". Nothing else ever lowers the quality.

### 26-030: When three clips in a row fail the same way, the job stops and says what probably broke.
- Where: youtube / Keeping the downloader working
- What: Three consecutive clips failing with the same error (ignoring which video and numbers) stop the job: it ends failed with a note, the clips not reached stay waiting, and a clip that lands in between resets the count. The notes:
  - No usable format each time: "N clips in a row came back with no usable format. That is what a YouTube client change looks like, not a problem with these videos: the server's means of downloading needs updating; then press RETRY to re-queue the failed clips." (naming what the server is running, in plain words).
  - The same, when the server lacks a part YouTube now requires to hand over any format: says that part is missing and an admin has to add it on the server.
  - YouTube refusing the media itself: says the clips were refused when the media itself was fetched, and that an admin should check that what the server uses to get past YouTube's blocks is working and that its means of downloading is up to date, then press RETRY.
  - Anything else: "N clips in a row failed the same way: <error>. Fix the cause, then press RETRY to re-queue the failed clips."
  A live stream refused (26-031) does not count towards the three.

### 26-031: Live streams that have not finished are refused, and a clip that takes too long is stopped.
- Where: youtube / How YouTube throttles and refuses
- What: A live stream still running, or one not yet started, fails with "this is a live stream that has not finished, so it would record until it ends. Download it again once it is an ordinary video." A finished stream downloads normally. A download running longer than 2 hours is stopped ("the download took longer than 2 hours and was stopped"). The conversion to an editable file is stopped after 3 hours ("the conversion to an editable file took longer than 3 hours and was stopped"). Searches already filter out anything over 30 minutes (26-013); pasted links are not limited by length.

### 26-032: Each clip is downloaded at the chosen quality or the best below it, in a file the editing program can open.
- Where: youtube / Files Resolve can decode
- What: Quality is a ceiling: the clip comes at that height or the best below it. At 1080p and below, YouTube's H.264 video with AAC audio is preferred, so most clips need no conversion; at 1440p, 2160p and best the best stream at or below that height is taken and converted afterwards (26-033). The result is an MP4 file.

### 26-033: Every downloaded clip is made ready for the editing program on its own, converted only when it needs to be.
- Where: youtube / Files Resolve can decode
- What: After download the clip's video and audio are checked. If the video is not H.264, its frame rate is variable (the average and nominal rates differ by more than 1%), or the audio is not AAC, it is converted to H.264 at a constant frame rate with AAC audio, at high quality, keeping its colour information and details, playable from the start while still loading. Streams that are already fine are kept as they are. The clip shows "converting to H.264 (was <video>/<audio>)", or "converting to H.264 on your computer" when the editor's own computer is doing it.
  - If the clip cannot be checked, it is converted anyway; if that fails too, it is kept as downloaded with "could not probe the codecs and the safety conversion failed; kept as downloaded".
  - An audio-only result is left alone. If the server cannot convert at all, the clip is kept as downloaded.
  - If the old copy is open in the editing program and cannot be replaced, it is kept beside the new one as "<name>.original" with the note "original was in use, kept as <file>"; if even that fails, the converted clip is saved as "<title>.converted [<id>]" with "converted, but <file> was in use: saved as <file>". The old copy is deleted later once it is free.
  - A needed conversion that fails fails the clip: "Edit-ready conversion failed: <the converter's last words>".

### 26-034: A second conversion target, an intra-frame editing format with uncompressed audio, exists but nothing can choose it.
- Where: youtube / Files Resolve can decode
- What: Present but never used: every clip is converted to H.264.
- Flag: unreachable

### 26-035: A search's clips go into a folder named after the topic, made safe for every computer.
- Where: youtube / File names and what a file carries
- What: Clips land in <project>/Youtube/<folder>. For a search the folder is the topic; for pasted links it is the typed folder, or none. The name is made safe: characters Windows forbids and control characters become spaces, runs of spaces become one, leading and trailing dots and spaces go, it is cut to about 80 bytes without splitting a character, a Windows reserved device name (CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM1 to COM9, LPT1 to LPT9) gets an underscore, and an empty result becomes "search". The text is stored in one Unicode form so the same name typed on a Mac and on Windows is one folder. Nothing can name a place outside the projects.

### 26-036: Each clip is named after its channel, title and YouTube id, and carries its credits with it.
- Where: youtube / File names and what a file carries
- What: File name: "<channel> - <title> [<video id>]", the channel cut to about 60 bytes and the title to about 140, safe on Windows. The id in brackets is how a clip already in the folder is recognised (26-015). Beside every clip is a credits record holding the channel, its link, the uploader, the video's link, the title, the id, the upload date and the duration; the clip file itself also carries the channel as its artist, the video's link as its comment, and the channel, video link, channel link and uploader as tags. Every computer that downloads names clips and writes credits the same way, so one clip is never in the tree under two spellings.

### 26-037: A record of each search is left in its folder.
- Where: youtube / File names and what a file carries
- What: When a job's download finishes, its folder gets a record of the search: the topic, whether it was a search or pasted links, the mode, when and by whom, the project, the quality, every term (text, language, translation, whether ticked, typed or written by the assistant, how many results) and every clip landed or skipped (id, link, title, channel, duration, upload date, where it is, and which terms found it). Running the same topic again replaces it. Pasted links with no folder get one record per job in the project's Youtube folder. A failure to write it never fails the job; a job stopped by YouTube's block writes none.

### 26-038: Before downloading, the product checks there is room and refuses with numbers when there is not.
- Where: youtube / Choosing the destination and checking space
- What: Expected size per second of video: 480p 0.35 MB, 720p 0.625 MB, 1080p 1 MB, 1440p 2 MB, 2160p and best 4.4 MB. With durations known, twice the estimate is needed; with none known (always for pasted links) the larger of 2 GB and three minutes per clip at that rate. Free space is measured where the clips go. Refusal: "there is only X GB free where these clips go (<project>/Youtube/<folder>), and these N clips need about Y GB (or "this download needs room to work in"), so nothing was started. Free some space and press DOWNLOAD again." (GET LINKS for a paste; RETRY FAILED when found as the download begins). Under 10 GB the numbers show one decimal. Free space is re-measured after a refusal and otherwise at most once a minute. If it cannot be measured, the download goes ahead. The check is made on pressing download or retry, on pasting links when the server will do the work, and once more as the download begins; it is skipped for a job being done on the editor's own computer, which reports its own free space instead.

### 26-039: If the server has lost the footage share, downloads are refused with that diagnosis, not a full-disk one.
- Where: youtube / Choosing the destination and checking space
- What: When the project's folder is absent, the projects folder itself is there, and nothing in it matches the project's name, the footage tree is declared gone: nothing is created, and the editor is told "the footage tree is not there: nothing at <projects folder> looks like <project>, so nothing was started. This is the server having lost the share, not a full disk. Tell whoever runs the dashboard, and press DOWNLOAD again once it is back." A job reaching its download this way fails with the same sentence.

### 26-040: Unfinished and failed files never pass for a finished clip, and day-old leftovers are cleaned away.
- Where: youtube / File names and what a file carries
- What: When a clip fails, any file it produced is renamed so it is never mistaken for a finished clip, and its partial files are removed. When a download starts in a folder, unfinished and intermediate files there older than a day are deleted. Files and folders made by downloads are writable by the whole editing group on the server.

### 26-041: From the download history or a finished clip, an editor can open the clip's folder on their own computer, fetch it from the NAS if they do not have it, or copy its path.
- Where: youtube / Reaching the editor and Resolve
- What: Clicking a clip asks the app on the editor's computer to open its folder. If the app says the clip is not on this computer, a message offers GET IT FROM THE NAS, which fetches it showing "getting <file> from the NAS - N%" and then opens the folder. If the app cannot be reached, is too old or fails, a message names the folder "Projects/<path>" under the editor's sync drive with COPY PATH ("path copied", or "copy it from the line above"). Original footage only syncs upwards, from editors' computers to the server, so a clip the server downloaded does not reach an editor's computer by itself; this is how they get it.
- Flag: several ways in: from a history row and from a finished clip's row in the downloads list.

### 26-042: The download history lists every clip the studio has downloaded, newest first.
- Where: youtube / Progress
- What: 24 clips at a time with OLDER for more and "showing N of M. Click a clip to open its folder"; the header counts all clips. Each row: thumbnail, title, "<project>/<folder> · channel · who downloaded it", and when. One entry per YouTube video, kept for ever and never shrinking; downloading the same video into another project moves its entry there. Who is credited is the editor who started the job.

### 26-043: The editor's recent searches are listed and can be reopened.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: The last 15 jobs, each with its time, state in words, "topic → project" or "links → project/Youtube", mode, languages, date range, shot types and "max N". Clicking one shows that job. If the page cannot load, this list shows "failed to load: <message>".

### 26-044: Opening the page takes the editor straight back to the job they were working on.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: On opening, the page shows the editor's running job if any, else their newest job waiting at a review, else the first job in their queue, else the job named in the page address, else nothing.

### 26-045: A health line on the page shows whether searching and downloading can work right now, and a banner names each problem with what to do.
- Where: youtube / Keeping the downloader working
- What: Re-read every two minutes. The main mark names the assistant in use and its state, adds a note when the server cannot download at all, and is green when both are fine, grey when not yet known and red otherwise. Further marks appear only when relevant: how old the server's means of downloading is (amber with "(Nd old)" when stale, red when missing); a missing part YouTube requires; whether what the server uses to get past YouTube's blocks is working, unreachable, failed to install, or off; the last download ("last download: anonymous", or failed, with a rough age such as "3 min ago", or "no downloads yet"), with the signed-in session's state in its tip; the periodic test when switched on (26-047). Banner lines, each on its own: no assistant credential, assistant unreachable, assistant timed out, assistant failed (naming it and the detail), downloading not possible on the server, a missing required part, the downloader not running ("jobs will queue and never start"), no projects, the job's own error with its matching instruction, searches waiting for review, and the expired session.

### 26-046: The server keeps its means of downloading up to date by itself, and warns an admin when it has fallen behind.
- Where: youtube / Keeping the downloader working
- What: YouTube changes its service deliberately to break downloaders, so what works today stops working within weeks. The server checks for a newer means of downloading about once a day, tests a new one before accepting it, never goes backwards, and uses it after the next restart (the dashboard is never restarted for it). The health line says "the running downloader is N days old", adding past three weeks "past the 21 day limit. YouTube breaks downloaders deliberately, so update the copy on this server before downloads start failing.", or when a newer one is waiting "A newer build is installed and is used after the next dashboard restart.", or that none is installed. Editors' own computers must run one no older than the studio's minimum to take a download (26-050).

### 26-047: An optional periodic test checks that a known short public video can still be reached, without downloading it.
- Where: youtube / Keeping the downloader working
- What: When an admin switches it on, at most every five minutes the server tries to reach a known short public video at 1080p exactly as a real download would (without signing in first, and signed in only after a bot check and only with a real signed-in session), and shows the result on the health line ("canary ok", "canary failed", "no run yet" in plain words). Off by default and hidden when off.
- Flag: dormant

### 26-048: The assistant behind search is whichever one an admin set up for the studio, and the page explains each way it can fail.
- Where: youtube / The assistant behind search
- What: Each call uses the assistant currently chosen in the dashboard's settings, so adding or removing a key takes effect on the next search without a restart. Failures, each mapped to an instruction on the page:
  - None set up: "this deployment has no working AI provider. An admin must add a key in settings. Until then no job can generate search terms." (A MY TERM ONLY search still works.)
  - A key missing, rejected or not allowed, or an assistant not signed in: names the assistant and where an admin fixes it.
  - An assistant that needs a site permission not given: "<assistant> is not enabled for this site".
  - No answer in time (about three minutes): "did not answer within N s".
  - Unreachable from the server; declined the request; an unusable or empty reply (tried once more first).
  - The page's health line carries the latest state, re-checked about every five minutes in the background while it is not well, and set by every real search. Keys never appear on the page.

### 26-049: Optionally, the editor's own computer downloads the clips instead of the server, with a switch on the page.
- Where: youtube / Where a download runs, and saying so
- What: When the studio switches this on, the page shows an "on this computer" switch (ticked by default, remembered). Flipping it says "downloads will be offered to this computer first" or "downloads will run on the server. YouTube originals only sync upwards, so you can fetch one later from the download history." and reloads the project list (a wired computer is offered every project). After DOWNLOAD, RETRY or GET LINKS, the editor's own computer is offered the job and, if it takes it, downloads, converts and files the clips exactly as the server would and reports each clip. The server waits about four seconds for it before starting the job itself. The downloads list shows "downloading on your computer" or "downloading on the server", with who claimed it in the tip, this computer's live line ("clip i/n · 38% at 4.2 MB/s · 2:10 left") and the free space the downloading computer reported. A job created with the switch off is always done by the server, and the page says so. Off for everyone by default: with it off the page never contacts the editor's computer about downloads and shows no switch or mark.
- Flag: dormant

### 26-050: When the editor's own computer cannot take a job, the download runs on the server and the page says why.
- Where: youtube / Where a download runs, and saying so
- What: Every refusal is one message starting "Downloading on the server: ..." per distinct reason, each adding that originals only sync upwards: the app on the computer is too old; it is not answering (with a hint to check its status page); it declined, with its reason; it is busy with another job; it does not download at this quality; another error. If the editor has not accepted the download terms in the app on their computer: "Please first accept the download terms in the CC Sync tray: <where>. This download runs on the server instead." The server itself also refuses a computer whose means of downloading is older than the studio minimum, one that names clips or writes credits differently ("Downloading here would put two spellings of the same clip in the tree."), one that does not run the job's quality, one asking for a job created for the server, a cancelled or finished job, or a job pinned to the server; and if another computer already holds it, "<who> is already downloading this job on <computer>". Only one computer ever downloads a job at once. After a hand-off the page watches for about four minutes and says once if the computer gave the job back, with its reason.
- Flag: dormant

### 26-051: A job being downloaded on an editor's computer goes back to the server when that computer disappears, when the editor asks, or when it finishes with failures.
- Where: youtube / One job, one computer
- What: 
  - A computer that stops answering (laptop closed, app stopped) loses the job within about three minutes; the server takes it over for good, counts every clip whose finished file is already in the folder as done (credited to that computer's editor), and fetches the rest.
  - DOWNLOAD ON THE SERVER INSTEAD (shown only while a computer holds the job): "handing this job back to the server: it picks up whatever your computer has not finished". The computer is told to stop at its next contact.
  - When the computer finishes, every clip that failed there is tried once more by the server, never more.
  - If that server retry is itself bot-checked by YouTube, the remaining clips are marked failed without further requests, each "YouTube is blocking the server right now (it asked the server to prove it is not a bot), so the server could not retry this clip. Press RETRY to fetch it from your own computer again.", and the job ends done with "Your computer downloaded the clips it could. The server could not retry the ones that failed there, because YouTube is blocking the server right now. Press RETRY to try them from your own computer again."
  - A computer that finds nothing left to fetch hands the job straight back so it closes at once.
- Flag: dormant

### 26-052: While their own computer is downloading, an editor can stop the clip it is on.
- Where: youtube / Where a download runs, and saying so
- What: STOP appears only while this computer's app has reported on the job; it asks the app to stop its current clip, with a message for success, an app too old, a refusal, or no answer.
- Flag: dormant

### 26-053: A person's YouTube records can be exported, their history erased, or their name removed from everything.
- Where: accounts-privacy-legal / What the product records about a person
- What: When the dashboard acts on a person's data (26-Q8 asks where):
  - Export: every job they created or whose clips their computer fetched, their jobs' search terms, the clips their computer fetched, the download history entries credited to them, and their acceptances of the notice, matched regardless of letter case. For another person's job that their computer fetched, only the facts of the fetching are included.
  - Erase history: deletes their FINISHED jobs with their terms and results; never the studio's download history, never their acceptances, never anyone else's jobs.
  - Delete: at once ends any download their computer holds, cancels their unfinished jobs (their search terms are deleted as soon as each stops), deletes the terms of their finished jobs, replaces their computers' names on their jobs, and replaces their name with a stand-in everywhere (jobs, clips, history, acceptances, keeping one acceptance per wording). A blank name or stand-in is refused.

### 26-054: The server accepts a few choices the page does not offer.
- Where: youtube / Searching and reviewing results
- What: The server accepts 480p quality and the "last hour" upload window, a per-term result count (1 to 50, default 15), and skipping the term review; the page offers none of them. Pasted links ignore search-only choices.
- Flag: unreachable: no page offers them; only something calling the server directly could.

### 26-055: The page still holds handling for "a previous search is still open", which can no longer happen.
- Where: youtube / Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel
- What: Before the queue existed, an editor could have only one open job, and the page offered to discard a parked search and retry. New jobs now always queue, so this refusal and its confirmation are never shown.
- Flag: unreachable
