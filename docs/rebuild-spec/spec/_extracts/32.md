### 32-013: The script export is the words of the cut, as spoken, laid out to read, in Markdown or plain text.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports

" < > |` and control characters) become `_`; trailing dots and spaces are removed; at most 150 characters (counted in UTF-16 units, never splitting a pair).
  - Markdown starts with `# <name>`.
  - A section or plain heading becomes `## <title>` (Markdown) or the title underlined with `=` (text). The heading's kind decides this, never its colour.
  - A `◆` note heading or a note row becomes `> ◆ <text>` (Markdown) / `-- <text> --` (text); an "unmatched" note row is prefixed "unmatched: ".
  - Each cut: a header line with the speaker in bold, then a long dash character, then the record timecode, with a hard line break (Markdown), or `Speaker <timecode>` (text). The timecode is where the cut sits on the timeline a send would build.
  - Then the cut's words as spoken: when a cut has lines in a non-Latin script, those are kept and the Latin gloss lines beside them dropped; an all-Latin cut drops only plainly English lines beside non-English ones. Lines that would read as Markdown structure (`# `, `> `, `- `, `1. `, `===`) are escaped.
  - Then the cut's note: `> 📝 <note>` or `   [note: <note>]`.
  - A gap's note comes before the cut after it: `> 📝 gap: <note>` or `   [gap note: <note>]`, taken from the gap rows of the cut list.
  - A cut with no words is left out and counted.
  - The loss list always says "the words as spoken only: translations are not in the script", plus "<n> cut(s) with no words are left out", plus a sentence when gap notes could not be read (the download still happens).

### 32-015: Imported timeline files are recognised and read by these rules.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports
- What:
  - The kind of file is told from its contents first (OpenTimelineIO's own marking; a Final Cut Pro XML root; an EDL `TITLE:` line or event line), only then from its extension (`.otio`, `.fcpxml`/`.xml`, `.edl`).
  - Limits: 16 MB of text; at most 20 000 clips or events; XML nested at most 256 deep. A file with no readable clips is refused ("this <format> has no clips the editor can read").
  - Clip names are matched to the cut list's clips ignoring a media extension (`.mov .mp4 .mxf .wav .ogg .m4a .mkv .braw .avi .aif .aiff`), repeated whitespace, letter case and Unicode form.
  - Source times are converted from the clip's own timecode to seconds from the media's first frame, using the clip's start timecode from the cut list (falling back to the file's own available range or asset start when the cut list does not know the clip).
  - OpenTimelineIO: the track carrying the editor's own metadata wins, then the first audio track with clips, then any track with clips; warning "this timeline has <n> tracks: only <name> is read" when several have clips. Gaps advance time; transitions are read as straight cuts (warning); other nested items are skipped (warning); clips without a source range are skipped (warning). The editor's own metadata (cut id, person, colour, gain, fades, note) round-trips exactly. Marker colours map: BLUE to Blue, CYAN to Cyan, GREEN to Green, YELLOW to Yellow, RED to Red, PINK to Pink, PURPLE to Purple, MAGENTA to Fuchsia, ORANGE to Sand, WHITE to Cream, BLACK to Cocoa.
  - Final Cut Pro XML (versions 1.8 to 1.11): spine items gap, asset-clip, clip (with nested audio or video reference) and mc-clip; transitions read as straight cuts; other spine items skipped with a warning; fades read from volume adjustments; markers on gaps and clips (the editor's own kind and colour written in the marker note is honoured); notes read. Clip volume is NOT read back (warning: the export baked levelling into it). Markers after the last clip cannot be carried, so the cut list's own trailing headings are kept.
  - CMX3600 EDL: `TITLE:`, `FCM: DROP FRAME`, events, `* FROM CLIP NAME:`, `* COMMENT:` (notes), Resolve marker comments (`|C:ResolveColor<colour> |M:<title> |D:<n>`). Dissolves, wipes and keys are read as straight cuts (warning). Audio and video events with the same record span count once. `BL` (black) events are gaps. An EDL starting at 00:00:00:00 rather than the cut list's start is counted from its first event's hour. No markers in the file: warning "this EDL carries no markers: the sections stay where they were".
  - The Resolve timeline, read from the project library: each card's clip, source in and out, timeline position and its own clip colour (only when the item has one), and the timeline's markers.
- Exact: yes

### 32-016: An import is matched to the cut list cut by cut so that only real changes are proposed and nothing is lost.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Bringing Resolve's changes back into the cut
- What:
  - Cuts are matched by the editor's own cut id when the file carries it (an OpenTimelineIO file from this editor), then by clip name and source span in three passes: exact, contained, overlapping; ties go to the nearest in order, then the earliest. Never by a Resolve clip id.
  - A cut id that names a shelf cut brings that cut back from the shelf ("back from the shelf").
  - An edge within half a frame of the cut list's value keeps the cut list's value; for a clip at a different rate than the timeline the tolerance is one and a half frames. So an untouched cut never comes back "trimmed".
  - A matched cut keeps what only the cut list knows: person, reference, words, highlights, note (unless the timeline states one), manual gain (unless the file carries gain), fades (unless stated), colour (unless stated).
  - An unclaimed clip inside a matched neighbour's span is the second half of a blade: it copies that cut's person, words and note under a new id.
  - A clip starting before its media is skipped with a warning. A changed cut shorter than the minimum cut length is lengthened to it; an untouched older cut under one frame keeps its span. Fades longer than the cut are shortened to fit.
  - Black before a cut becomes a gap row (an existing gap keeps its identity); gaps under the minimum gap length are dropped.
  - Sections: when the timeline has markers, they ARE the sections. A marker whose id or title the cut list knows keeps the cut list's kind and identity (a colour change is kept as a recolour, but the kind's default export colour does not count as one); an unknown marker becomes a `## §` section, or a `## ◆` note when Green or Red; untitled markers are ignored unless they are note rows. Each marker stands in front of the first clip that has not ended by it. A file with no markers keeps every heading in front of the cut it stood in front of.
  - A cut that is not on the timeline is moved to the shelf under the staged category "Not on the timeline", never deleted.
  - The cut list records "<UTC time> from <timeline name>" as when it was last updated from a timeline.
  - The whole proposal is checked to apply as one step before anything is shown. A proposal whose only change is that record counts as "in step". Warning "the cut order after accepting may differ from the timeline's: check the preview" when the result's order differs.
  - Preview row kinds: Trimmed ("in <a> to <b>, out <c> to <d>"), Moved ("was cut <n>", judged by the longest run left in order), Gap, Colour ("colour <a> to <b>", "speaker" when none), Fade, Gain, New ("new on the timeline: <in> to <out>"), Staged ("not on the timeline: staged under 'Not on the timeline'"), Marker ("new heading/note '<title>'" or "'<title>' is not on the timeline: removed"), and an unshown Same count.
- Exact: yes

### 32-018: The editor never connects to Resolve's scripting while Resolve is still starting.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Talking to Resolve: restarts, dialogs and speed
- What: A fact about Resolve: a scripting client that connects in the short window while Resolve is starting, after its script service appears but before Resolve has registered with it, kills scripting for that whole Resolve session on every client on the computer, until Resolve and every client are restarted in order. So before every connection attempt the editor checks, without connecting, that Resolve's script service is listening and that Resolve itself is attached to it; it connects only then, or when that check cannot be made at all. Otherwise it waits about 3 s and checks again, never touching Resolve meanwhile, and shows "Resolve is starting: waiting before connecting". One connection is kept while it lasts; a lost one is dropped and the check is made again before reconnecting. The link to Resolve is let go after a minute with nothing asked.
