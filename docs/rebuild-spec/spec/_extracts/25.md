# Extract of inventory section 25

## Items

### 25-001: A person finds music by describing the sound they want in their own words.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: The music page has one search box (its hint reads "describe the vibe… tense driving synth pulse, warm nostalgic piano, hopeful build for a montage"). Pressing Enter or the search button runs the search; Enter is the only key shortcut on the page. An empty box shows the library instead (browsing). A choice beside the box sets how a track is matched: "any moment" (the default: a track is scored by its best single 10-second stretch, taken from up to twelve stretches spread evenly across the track, ignoring the very start and end) or "whole track" (scored by the track as a whole). Changing that choice re-runs the search only when the box has text. A search returns at most 60 tracks, in order of how well they match, each with a match figure from 0 to 100 that is relative to this result set only (the best hit shows 100, the weakest 0, a single hit 0). Filters are applied to those 60 after they are chosen, so a filter can only thin the results, never bring in further tracks. A very long description is cut short; only its first several hundred words count.

### 25-002: Eight example descriptions run a search with one click.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: Under the search box are eight ready-made searches, each of which runs at once: "tense driving synth pulse", "warm nostalgic piano", "hopeful build for a montage payoff", "sparse ominous drone under an interview", "triumphant orchestral finale", "playful quirky plucked strings", "traditional east asian strings", "gritty industrial percussion".

### 25-003: One clear control resets the whole search.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: "clear" empties the search box, deselects any tag, resets every feel slider to "off", unticks "include tracks with no BPM or length", empties the tempo and length boxes, and lists the whole library again.

### 25-004: The page shows whether the editor's Resolve is reachable, and what is open in it.

It is asked of the app on the editor's own computer (never of the server) when the page opens, after every send, about every 30 seconds while the tab is in view (not while hidden), and at once when the tab comes back into view.

### 25-006: The music page sits inside the dashboard and falls back to a plain link home.

### 25-007: The music page works on narrow screens and phones.
- Where: music / The music page
- What: Below about 900 pixels wide the header stops staying at the top, the filters stack above the results and the add-music panel takes the full width. Below about 600 pixels, text fields use a text size large enough that a phone does not zoom in on focus, and on touch screens controls are at least 44 pixels to tap.

### 25-008: A person can filter by feel, one feel at a time.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: There is one slider per feel, in this order with these labels: "arousal: calm → intense", "valence: dark → bright", "tension: resolved → tense", "organic: electronic → acoustic" (any further feel the library has is added after these, labelled "low → high"). Each runs 0 to 100 in steps of 5; 0 reads "off", any other value v reads "top (100 − v)%" and keeps only tracks ranked at or above v on that feel compared with the rest of the library. Above the sliders: "One at a time: moving a slider clears the others." Releasing a slider resets the others to off and re-runs the current search, or the browse list if the box is empty. Only the high end of a feel can be asked for: there is no way to ask for, say, the calmest tracks.

### 25-009: A person can filter by tempo and by length.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: Boxes for lowest and highest BPM (0 to 300) and shortest and longest length in seconds, committed together by an "apply" button, which re-runs the current search or browse. An empty box or 0 means no limit on that side.

### 25-010: Tracks with no tempo or length yet are hidden by tempo and length filters unless the person asks for them.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: When some tracks have no BPM or no length yet, a tick box "Include tracks with no BPM or length" appears with "<n> tracks have no BPM or length yet" (n is the larger of the two counts), or "every track has a BPM and a length" otherwise. Ticked, a tempo or length filter also keeps tracks whose value is missing. When a filter has hidden tracks only because the value is missing, the results say "<n> track(s) have/has no <bpm|length> and are/is not shown" with an "include them" button (hint "Show tracks the indexing computer has not analysed yet") that ticks the box and re-runs.

### 25-011: A person can filter by a tag: genre, mood, instrument, use case or texture.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: Each tag category (genre, mood, instrument, use case, texture, then any other alphabetically) lists its labels with how many tracks carry each, most common first. Clicking a label selects it (only one at a time across all categories), empties the search box and browses tracks with that tag; clicking the selected label again deselects it. Clicking a tag shown on a track row does the same as clicking that label.

Clear the search to sort the library." With a tag selected and sort left at file name, tracks are ordered by how strongly they carry that tag. Browsing shows at most 500 tracks.

### 25-016: Each track in the list shows what it is and offers play, similar and send.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: Each row has a play button; the file name; its length (m:ss), BPM (rounded, when known) and key (when known); its leading tags (top genre, top two moods, top instrument, top use case), each with a hint "<category> · score <s> · <pct>th percentile"; a match figure (search and similar only); a "similar" button; and a "→ Resolve" button, which opens the player under the row without starting playback. The play button opens the player under the row and starts playing; pressing it again on the open track pauses and resumes. Changing the list closes any open player and stops the sound.

### 25-017: A person can list the tracks that sound most like a given track.
- Where: music / Searching and filtering
- What: "similar" (on a track row and in its player) replaces the list with the 25 nearest-sounding tracks, excluding the track itself, each with a match figure. Similarity deliberately ignores what tracks from the same stock catalogue share because they come from that catalogue, so it does not just return the same catalogue: the catalogue is judged from the shape of the file names (for example a stock-library prefix, long random-looking ids, numeric ids), and only a catalogue with at least 8 tracks in the library is discounted. Text search does not discount catalogues.
- Flag: several ways in: the track row and the open player.

### 25-018: A person can play a track in the browser with a waveform to click on.
- Where: music / Listening in the browser
- What: One player is open at a time, opening under its track's row. It shows the track's waveform with the played part marked, kept in step while playing and when the window is resized; clicking the waveform jumps to that point. A play/pause button and a readout "m:ss / m:ss". When the track ends the button returns to play. Captions under the waveform: no waveform stored yet: "No waveform yet: this track was added by the fleet and the indexing computer has not analysed it. Seeking still works."; connection lost: "The waveform could not be loaded: this page lost the server."; other failure: "The waveform could not be loaded (the server answered N)." Where the server can analyse audio itself, a missing waveform is made on first request and kept.

### 25-019: Listening uses a lighter preview copy where one exists, and starts and seeks quickly.
- Where: music / Listening in the browser
- What: Each track can have a preview copy at about 128 kbps for listening; the original is played where there is none. No preview is made for a track whose original is already at or below about 134 kbps, whatever its format. A preview keeps only the sound (cover art dropped), mixes surround down to stereo, keeps mono as mono and keeps the sample rate; a preview more than 3% shorter than its original is refused as truncated. A preview older than its original is remade. Playback can start anywhere in the track without the whole file downloading first. Removing a track from the library removes its preview copy. Making previews is done on the indexing computer.
- Flag: undetermined: whether previews are made on their own or only when someone runs it; the inventory describes a hand-run step.

### 25-020: A person sends a track to Resolve on their own computer in one of three ways.
- Where: music / Sending to Resolve
- What: The open player has three buttons, which talk to the app on the person's own computer, never to the server:
  - "import to bin": "Import into the Music bin in the media pool. Nothing is placed on a timeline."
  - "place underneath": "Place at the playhead on the first free audio track from A2 down. Nothing moves."
  - "insert at playhead": "Ripple insert on A2: clips at or after the playhead on that track shift later. Refuses if that track is linked to video."
  The page names the track by the library and its place in it, never by a path on the person's computer. While the exchange runs every player button is disabled and the status reads "talking to Resolve…". If the track is not on this computer yet it reads "track isn’t on this computer yet: syncing it down, then sending…" and then "syncing the track to this computer: N%", asking again about every 1.5 seconds. If this computer is busy with another download: the app's own message or "waiting for this computer’s other download to finish, then sending…", retrying when the app says to (about 1.5 seconds by default). It ends with the app's note or "done" (good), or its error or "failed" (bad). If the app answered but refused: "the CC Sync tray answered but refused the request (tray HTTP N)", pointing the person to the app's copy-diagnostics-for-your-admin action, which has the reason. If it could not be reached: "couldn’t reach the CC Sync tray: it is not running, or the browser blocked local connections", followed by a self-test: the address of the app's own status page to open in a tab.

### 25-022: Dropping audio anywhere on the page adds it to the library.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: Dragging files over the page covers it with "Drop audio to add it to the library" / "Your own computer analyses it and uploads it. .ogg is transcoded to mp3." Folders dropped are searched all the way down, keeping only audio files, up to 500. A drop with nothing usable: "Nothing there this app can index: music takes .wav, .mp3, .flac, .aac, .m4a, .ogg and similar." If the app on the person's computer can analyse music, the drop opens the add-music panel (25-026 onwards) and that computer does the work. If it cannot (not running, or too old), the files are uploaded to the server instead (25-024).
- Flag: differs: the same drop is analysed on the person's own computer, or uploaded to the server and analysed later by the indexing computer, and the page chooses without asking.

### 25-023: Which files count as music.
- Where: music / Which files count as music
- What: Audio files in WAV, MP3, FLAC, AAC, M4A, OGG, AIFF (either spelling of its ending) and Opus form. Anything else is refused as "not an audio file (<ending>)" or left out of a drop. A file with no playable sound, or a sound of zero length, is refused ("no decodable audio stream"). OGG files are converted to high-quality MP3 (320 kbps, keeping their details such as title and artist) when added. The library is one flat folder, Assets\Music in the shared tree; when the whole library is analysed, anything inside a folder named "stems" with a leading underscore is skipped.

### 25-024: Audio uploaded to the server is checked, named and filed, and waits for the indexing computer when the server cannot analyse it.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: Any signed-in person can upload. One upload takes at most 64 files, no file over 512 MiB and no more than 512 MiB in all; over that, nothing is written. Each file's name is cleaned (folders dropped; characters Windows forbids replaced by an underscore; an empty name becomes "untitled"). The track is placed flat in the library folder; if the name is taken (in either Unicode spelling, by another track, or by a track still on its way in) it becomes "name (2)", "(3)" and so on, up to about a thousand, then refused. The file is made readable by every editor over the network. Where the server can analyse audio, the track is analysed at once, tagged with the rest of the library and comes back ready ("✓ name - m:ss, NNN bpm"); a file that cannot be analysed is removed again ("could not analyse the audio"). Where it cannot, the file goes into the library but is not searchable until the indexing computer analyses it. The toast reads "Uploading N file(s)…" / "checking for duplicates: this can take a few seconds each", then "Added a/n" or "Queued q/n" (queued adds that nothing was analysed because the server cannot analyse music, that the tracks are in the library and will not be searchable until the indexing computer indexes them, and how many are waiting in all), with a line per file: "◷ name (transcoded to mp3) - m:ss, queued for indexing", "✓ name - m:ss, NNN bpm" or "✕ name - <error>". It stays about 9 seconds. A failed upload says "Ingest failed: …". When anything was added, the page refreshes its totals and tags and shows "Just added". The server refuses the whole upload, naming what is missing, if it lacks the audio tools it needs.

### 25-025: The same track is not added twice.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: A track is refused as "already in the library as <name> (same track)" when its name, once simplified (case, a stock-library prefix and copy markers like "(2)" ignored, only letters and digits compared), matches a track already there or on its way in and the lengths are within 2 seconds of each other (this catches the same track re-encoded). It is refused as "already in the library as <name>" when its bytes are identical to a track already there or on its way in. In the add-music panel these are found before anything uploads: a duplicate is unticked the first time it is found and marked "already in the library as <name or track #id>".

### 25-026: Nothing is written to the library when its folder looks missing.
- Where: music / Keeping files safe
- What: Before adding anything the server checks the library is really there. It refuses, writing nothing, when the folder cannot be read; when it is absent and tracks are known to have been in it: "the music library is not mounted here (nothing at <path>). Nothing is written until it is back."; or when none of a sample of up to 50 known track files (the 25 newest and 25 oldest) can be found: "the music library at <path> is there but empty: none of the indexed tracks are visible, so this looks like a share that is not mounted rather than an empty library. Nothing is written until it is back." On a brand-new library the folder is created. If the only tracks known are ones still on their way in: "the music library folder is not there yet on the server. A drop that is still uploading will create it; try again once that has finished." An analysed track whose upload is in progress waits and retries when the library is not there.

### 25-027: Tracks waiting for the indexing computer, and ones it could not analyse, are listed.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: In the add-music panel: "Waiting for the indexing computer: N track(s). They are in the library already and are not searchable until the indexing computer indexes them." with up to 20 names; and "N could not be analysed. Nothing retries these on their own: fix the file and drop it again." with up to 20 "✕ name - reason" lines. Hidden when both are zero. Refreshed about every 15 seconds while the panel is open.

### 25-030: A person can pick files or a whole folder with their computer's own file picker.

### 25-035: A person follows a running batch and can pause, resume, start it now, hold its uploads or cancel it.

### 25-036: The words shown for each batch and track state.

### 25-037: When a batch finishes the library refreshes and shows the newest tracks first.

### 25-038: A person sees their own past batches, and an admin sees everyone's.
- Where: music / Imports across computers
- What: The batch list has "Mine" and, for an admin only, "All computers" (anyone else asking for all is refused "only an admin can see every computer's batches"). Newest first, at most 200, refreshed about every 10 seconds. ### 25-039: A batch can be cancelled, taken over by another of the person's computers, or have its failed tracks tried again.
- Where: music / Imports across computers
- What: "cancel" on an unfinished batch stops it: at once if no computer holds it, otherwise the holding computer stops when it next checks in. Tracks already in the library stay; tracks analysed but not yet uploaded are taken out of the library again (unless the library looks unmounted, when nothing is removed). "take over on this computer" (waiting batches, Mine only) hands it to this computer: "This computer has taken the batch on." or the refusal. It is refused while a computer is still on the batch: "<computer or 'another computer'> is still working on this batch. Stop it first, then try the failed tracks again."

### 25-040: A batch belongs to one of its owner's computers at a time, and comes back if that computer goes quiet.
- Where: music / Imports across computers
- What: Only the editor who created a batch can run it, from any of their computers. While one computer works on it no other can ("<computer> is already indexing this batch"). If the computer goes quiet for about 5 minutes the batch returns to waiting (keeping the computer's name) and can be taken over; a computer that has lost its batch is told why (cancelled, finished, taken by another computer, or its time ran out) and stops. A computer must prove both which studio computer it is and which editor is signed in on it; if not, it is refused, being told to sign in again from the app.

### 25-041: A track analysed on an editor's computer is searchable at once and playable once uploaded.
- Where: music / Imports across computers
- What: As soon as a track's analysis arrives it can be found in search; it becomes playable once its upload has landed and been checked to be the size the computer sent ("the file in the library is not the size the CC Sync tray sent" otherwise). Its name is fixed when its analysis arrives, in the same way as an upload (25-024); if another batch took the name first the computer simply tries again. A track analysed with a different version of the analysis model than the rest of the library is refused, and the computer is told the library was analysed with a different model version from the one it is running.

### 25-042: Tags across the library are refreshed when tracks are added, at most about once a minute.
- Where: music / Keeping the library's data whole
- What: Because tags are relative to the whole library (25-044), adding tracks refreshes every track's tags and feels. When tracks arrive in quick succession this happens at most about once a minute, and in between the page shows "tags catching up"; the refresh also runs when a batch ends. A refresh that fails keeps the previous tags and leaves "tags catching up" showing; nobody else is told.

### 25-043: What each track ends up described by.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: Each track carries: its length (measured from the sound itself, not from what the file claims); its tempo in BPM to one decimal, always between 60 and 190, judged from the first six minutes, or none if it cannot be judged; its musical key such as "A minor" (major or minor) with a confidence; its loudness (integrated LUFS for anything over half a second, and peak level); its waveform; its tags in five categories and its four feels (25-044); and what it sounds like, both as a whole and in up to twelve 10-second stretches, for search and similar. A file that cannot be analysed is skipped and the rest carry on.

### 25-044: The tags and feels a track can carry.
- Where: music / Adding tracks to the library
- What: Tags are chosen from fixed lists and kept per track as the top 3 genres, 4 moods, 4 instruments, 3 use cases and 3 textures, each with a score and its rank against the rest of the library as a percentile.
  - Genre: orchestral, ambient, electronic, synthwave, hip hop, rock, post-rock, folk acoustic, jazz, classical, east asian, world folk, lo-fi, pop, trailer epic, choral, experimental, blues soul.
  - Mood: tense, ominous, dark, uplifting, hopeful, triumphant, melancholy, nostalgic, peaceful, mysterious, urgent, playful, romantic, aggressive, contemplative, eerie, grand, lonely, determined.
  - Instrument: piano, strings, solo cello, acoustic guitar, electric guitar, synth, drums, percussion, brass, woodwind, choir, electronic beat, bass, east asian str, mallets, organ, harp.
  - Use case: interview bed, montage, opening titles, tension build, emotional payoff, action, underscore, credits, reveal, timelapse.
  - Texture: sparse, dense, lo-fi tape, clean, gritty, pulsing, drone, glitchy.
  - Feels: arousal (energetic against calm), valence (happy against sad), tension (anxious against relaxed), organic (acoustic against electronic), each ranked as a percentile across the library.
  Scores are judged against the whole library, so a track's tags and ranks can shift as the library grows.

### 25-045: Tracks whose files have gone are removed from the library, but not when the library looks unmounted.
- Where: music / Keeping files safe
- What: When the whole library is re-analysed, new and changed files are analysed and tracks whose files are no longer there are removed, with their preview copies. Nothing is removed if no files were found at all ("a share that is not mounted, not an empty library. Nothing pruned."), or if more than 5 tracks and more than a fifth of the library would go, unless the person running it insists. Up to 20 removed names are listed to whoever ran it.
- Flag: undetermined: the inventory describes this as a step run by hand on the indexing computer; whether it should run on its own is open.

### 25-046: The quality of tagging and search can be measured against known tracks.
- Where: music / What the owner wants
- What: A check reports, per tag category, how often one label dominates, and runs ten fixed descriptions each expected to find a named track (for example "retro neon synthwave" should find "Neon City"), giving each one's rank (within 10 good, within 30 near, otherwise a miss) and the overall hit rate; a second check confirms fifteen tracks named for their sound (such as "Solo Piano") carry the expected tag. Tracks not in the library are skipped.
- Flag: dev only

### 25-047: Tooltips and confirmations on the music page work by touch and keyboard.
- Where: whole-product / The look
- What: Every hint appears on hover, on keyboard focus and, on touch screens, on a tap of anything that is not itself a control; Escape or scrolling hides it. A confirmation offers Cancel (already chosen) and "Go ahead". Sections of the page can be folded away, and stay folded the next time the page is opened on that browser.
