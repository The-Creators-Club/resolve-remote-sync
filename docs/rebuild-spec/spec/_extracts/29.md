Hover (mouse) or tap opens a pop-up: "Checking the join…", "The join reads well", "Meaning changed" or "Grammar break", "quick check" when only the quick check has answered, the note, the joined original words, and their fresh English, or "<the assistant> is reading the joined words again…" (today worded with the assistant's maker's name).

### 29-054: Language aids mark the words: verbs, names, numbers, negation, fillers, safe cut points and sentences.

### 29-055: The lane bar sets the lane's height, top or bottom, panels hidden, language, literal line and word size.
- Where: cards-editor / The page: panels, header, warnings and redrawing
- What: Each choice is remembered per device.

Keyboard on the grip: arrows grow or shrink 8 px (Shift 40 px), Page Up/Down 80 px, Home the minimum, End the maximum, Enter back to the default. Double-click the grip: back to the default. Esc during a drag puts it back. The lane is at least 64 px (96 px on touch) and the panels always keep at least 200 px. Default ("the lane is the hero"): 58% of the room the lane and panels share, never under 116 px, leaving the panels at least 300 px or half the room, whichever is less. The wanted height is refitted when the window or panels change size, without being forgotten.
  - **Top or bottom**: the dock button flips it ("Move the lane to the bottom" / "to the top"); dragging that button more than 6 px shows a dashed outline and dropping in the top or bottom half of the window docks it there. **Top by default.**
  - **Hide the panels**: folds the panels away so the lane takes the whole editor, and back ("Show the panels").
  - **Language**: "EN" (English leads, the spoken words under it where they fit) or the original language, labelled by the list's languages ("中文", "RU", "UK", "RU/UK", else "Original"), judged from up to 400 cuts. F9 per its tip. **English by default.** Every surface follows it (the edit view's words, the joins, the words past an edge).
  - **Literal**: shows or hides the word-for-word line. Off by default.
  - **Word size**: smaller / larger buttons (12 to 24 px) and the current size. Automatic by default (the track height divided by 24, between 13 and 20 px: a 116 px track gives 13, 380 gives 16, 480 gives 20). Choosing a size remembers it; double-clicking the number (or a button) returns to automatic.
  - **◐ rate scrub pad**: press and hold, then slide; works like holding the ruler (pauses playback first; short vibration); the press point is the origin.
  - Where the view settings are present, the bar gives the height and top/bottom to them and keeps only the word tools, so there are never two grips. On phones the layout sets the lane's place and height and the bar shows only language and size, on the Lane tab.

### 29-056: Which words a clip shows, and in which language, follows set rules.
- Where: cards-editor / The English a cut shows
- What:
  - A card's lines in a non-Latin script are the spoken words; Latin lines beside them are its English. An all-Latin card is English (an English interview), unless a translation says otherwise.
  - The English comes from, in order: the English the editor's analysis provides; the card's own English line; a stored translation (dimmed when machine-made).
  - With English leading, a clip with no English leads with its original and carries the no-English tag ("no English", "loading…" while it is being made, or the reason once known).
  - The second line shows only when it differs from the first.
  - While an edge moves, the clip's text is re-cut from its timed words at once, before the server has saved it; the stored English is kept only while the words are the same (within two characters, ignoring spaces and punctuation).
  - A word belongs to a cut when its midpoint is inside the cut. A clip's word timings come from its word file, else its subtitles; a clip without any is checked again after about half a minute, so new timings appear by themselves. Up to 60,000 words per clip.
- Exact: yes

### 29-057: The phone's Edit tab shows the cut running down the screen as rows, with the playhead travelling through them.
- Where: cards-editor / Phone and game controller
- What: On phone and landscape-phone layouts, under the Edit tab.
  - One row per cut in timeline order; a gap is a thin row reading "gap N.N s". A point along a row is a point in time; the timeline runs down the screen.
  - Each cut row: its number, short speaker name, the words in the lane's language (only at row heights of 48 px or more), its length (or "+/−N.NN s" while a trim is held), and the cut's waveform behind at the playing level (a flat line when there is none). Selected and picked-up rows are marked.
  - **The playhead** is a marker that travels across the row it is in; the list moves up under it so it stays 38% of the way down. A finger scrolling the list "browses": the playhead stays and the list stays where it was put until the playhead moves again (or play starts).
  - **Row height**: four steps, 24, 48, 72, 96 px (48 by default), set with "Shorter rows" / "Taller rows" or a two-finger pinch (each 30% change in finger distance is one step). Remembered per device.
  - **The dial** (a strip down the right edge) is the only thing that scrubs. Dragging it pauses playback and moves the playhead down or up the rows, heard as it goes; the further left of the dial the finger is, the finer (down to a quarter, over 160 px). Keyboard on the dial: Up/Down one second, Shift ten.
  - **Tap** in the list: play / pause, after a 300 ms wait (so a second tap can be a pick-up).
  - **Two taps** on the same row within 450 ms pick it up (short vibration; it becomes selected): "Picked up cut N / the gap. Tap where it goes: the top half of a row puts it before, the bottom half after. Tap it again to put it back." The next tap moves it ("Moved. Undo puts it back.") or, on itself or empty space, puts it back ("Put back.").
  - The browser's long-press menu never appears on the list or the loupe.
- Exact: yes

### 29-058: On the phone's rows, a press-and-hold trims an edge, auditions it, and a loupe shows the waveform close up.
- Where: cards-editor / Phone and game controller
- What:
  - **Press and hold 450 ms then drag** on a cut row (short vibration): trims its in point (pressed in the left half) or out point (right half), arms that edge, heard as it moves. A drag the full width of the screen moves the edge 5 s whatever the cut's length; the cut keeps at least 0.1 s; values move in 1 ms steps. On release one trim is committed and auditioned: the playhead goes 1.5 s before the new edge and plays for 3 s, then pauses. Moving 10 px before the hold cancels it.
  - **Loupe**: a 4 s waveform strip (2 s either side of the playhead, or of the edge being trimmed) at the foot of the list, with the cut's own in/out lines labelled, a centre line (one colour for the playhead, another for a held edge), and under it the word at that point, "· 4 s across" and "· trim"/"· fine". "the head is in a gap" when it is. It appears by itself while a trim is held; the Loupe button pins it on ("Loupe on: the waveform around the head, 4 s across" / "Loupe off. It still appears while you trim."), remembered per device. A press-and-hold that starts on the loupe trims the nearer edge of the cut the playhead is in, 4 times finer; in a gap: "The head is standing in a gap. A hold trims the cut the head is in; take it into one with the dial."
  - **Bar buttons**: "2x" (twice speed with pitch following; again for normal speed), "Split" (splits the cut at the playhead), "Delete" (the first tap turns it into "Tap again" for 3 s; the second deletes the cut under the playhead: "Deleted the cut under the head (it is on the shelf). Undo puts it back."), "Loupe", "Shorter rows", "Taller rows". Split and Delete are disabled with "Put the head inside a cut first" when the playhead is in no cut; tapping them then toasts that reason.
- Exact: yes

### 29-065: Every assistant suggestion and every import arrives as a proposal the person previews and accepts in whole or in part.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: The edit chat, shortening, montage, search inserts and bringing changes back from Resolve all hand the editor a proposal.
  - A proposal has a title (up to 200 characters), a reason (up to 4,000), its changes, where it came from, and optional labels per change. Refused: no title ("a proposal needs a title"), no changes, or a change this editor does not know ("the <source> proposal has an op this editor does not know").
  - Preview: what it would do card by card (added, removed, changed with the fields, moved, shelved, unshelved) and the lane as it would be (clips the same, added, changed or moved, spans taken out, the length before and after). With nothing picked: "nothing is picked".
  - Accept all, accept some (the picked changes, in their own order), or none (rejected). An accepted proposal is ONE edit and one undo step. Refusals: "that proposal is gone", "that proposal was already <status>", "no cut list is open", "those changes do not stand without the ones left out: <reason>", or the editor's own refusal; nothing is applied half way.
  - Once the server has taken the accepted edit, the proposal is gone from the server too; if the server refuses the edit, the proposal reopens with "the server refused it (the cut list had changed meanwhile); look at it again". Rejecting a proposal removes it from the server as well.
  - If the proposals cannot be fetched: "the proposals could not be fetched (<status>)". Proposals for another cut list never appear in this one; the same one is never shown twice.
  - Up to 50 open proposals (the oldest dropped) and 50 closed ones kept for history; switching cut lists clears them.
- Exact: yes

### 29-066: The editor opens and edits with no network, once it has been opened on that device before.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: After one visit, the editor itself opens with no network. A request the server has not answered within about 4 seconds is treated as offline. Offline, the editor still has the last cut list it saw, each clip's facts and its waveform; other answers from the server (for example sound fetched from the server, search on the server) are not available unless the list is kept on the device (29-067). A new version of the editor is picked up as soon as the device is online. A sign-in page or a failed answer is never kept as if it were the editor. Updating the editor never clears cut lists kept on the device. Edits made offline are kept on the device and sent when the connection returns (see the sync pill, 29-010).

### 29-067: A cut list can be kept whole on the device, so everything works from it offline.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: For a cut list kept on the device, every part of the editor works with no network without knowing it:
  - The server is still asked first, but waited on for at most 5 seconds; on no answer, a server error, or no network, the kept copy answers. A good answer from the server quietly refreshes the kept copy (at most about once a minute per item, only when it changed).
  - Analysis the editor shows (English, links between English and original words, language aids, join checks) is collected into the kept list while online, and served whole from it offline.
  - Search offline is a plain search of the kept transcripts (any letter case, or exact when the query is in double quotes), up to 200 results, including matches in the cards' text, and the results are marked approximate.
  - Sound and waveforms of kept clips come from the device, online or not, sparing the server; a stretch of sound the kept copy cannot play is fetched from the server instead.
  - The kept preview video plays from the device, seeking included, with no network; only a completely kept preview is used.
  - A list that is not kept is untouched by any of this.

### 29-068: Kept cut lists live in the app's project folder, or in the browser, and say which.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: Where kept lists live is chosen by itself:
  1. Inside the studio's Windows or Android app: real files in the app's project folder on disk (on Windows, under Documents, a "Timeline Cards" folder with one folder per episode and one per cut list). Shown as "The Windows app's project folder" / "The Android app's projects folder".
  2. Otherwise in the browser's own storage, if it actually works there (a private window that refuses falls through). Shown as "This browser".
  3. Otherwise nothing can be kept: "Nowhere (this browser keeps nothing)".
  - In the apps' folder, every folder name is made safe for Windows (forbidden characters become "_", trailing dots and spaces removed, at most 120 characters, reserved names such as CON get a leading "_"); two lists landing on one name get " (2)", " (3)"; anything that is not a cut list goes under "Other". Clip files are named after the clip.
  - A kept file is never left half-written: what has arrived is held aside until it is complete and checked, then put in place. A short download resumes from where it stopped ("<file>: N of M bytes arrived"); a wrong size is thrown away ("<file>: N bytes arrived, M were expected"); a wrong content is thrown away ("the bytes on the device are not the bytes that were sent"). A closed tab keeps what had landed. Two tabs keeping lists at once never lose each other's work. Checking a large download never freezes the page.
  - Removing a kept list deletes its folder (an episode folder left empty goes too) and reports the space freed; a keep in progress notices and stops.
  - Usage shows the space held, the space the browser allows (or the disk's free space in the apps), whether the browser has promised not to clear it (the apps always say yes), and the folder on disk (apps).
  - Apps only: show the folder in the file manager; move the project folder, refused while lists are kept there unless told to move them or leave them: "N cut list(s) is/are kept in the current folder: move it/them, or leave it/them where it is/they are". Errors: "this device has no room left for it", "the app answered <status> for <what>". The app accepts no writes from another web page.

- What: A hidden per-browser choice can force which kind of storage keeps lists (or none). No control sets it.
