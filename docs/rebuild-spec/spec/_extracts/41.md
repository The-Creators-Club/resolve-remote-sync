# Extract of inventory section 41

## Items

### 41-001: An interview's recordings become ready to cut with no step a person runs.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: When an interview's recordings are in, the product makes, on its own and for each interview:
  - the interview's sound as one file that plays in both ears (41-006);
  - a transcript in the language spoken, split into paragraphs, each paragraph with a permanent address, interviewer questions marked (41-016 to 41-020);
  - English for every paragraph (41-021);
  - the time of every spoken word, in the clip's own timecode (41-004, 41-011);
  - links between the English and the original words and phrases (41-022);
  - a small picture preview that lines up with the sound from its first frame (41-023);
  - the first proposal of themes and a first sort onto the board (41-034, 41-035).
  None of this may need the editing program to be open, or any person to start, order or repeat a piece of it. Today a person starts each piece by hand, in Resolve and outside it, and several pieces need Resolve open; in the rebuild that is gone. Every decision the product makes along the way is listed as its own item below, because each needs a manual override.

### 41-002: Every automatic decision on the way in is visible and can be overridden by hand.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: The decisions made on their own between footage arriving and the board being ready are: which person a recording belongs to (41-003); the starting timecode of the words (41-004); the frame rate (41-005); whether a one-sided stereo recording is made one-channel (41-006); the language of each stretch of speech (41-008); the clip's main language (41-009); how Chinese is written (41-010); whether a recording needs transcribing again (41-012); where paragraphs break and how long one may be (41-016); which lines are interviewer questions (41-018); the English of each paragraph (41-021); which picture the preview shows (41-024); where each camera piece sits in time against the sound (41-025); where the interview really begins (41-028); each interviewee's colour (41-029); who is speaking in a montage clip (41-032); the themes proposed and where each paragraph is first placed (41-034, 41-035). Each must show what it decided and let a person change it.

### 41-003: The product decides which person each recording belongs to, from its name.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Grouping clips and building the multicam
- What: A recording is filed under an interviewee by comparing its name (with a trailing "Multicam" removed) with the people already in the episode, in this order:
  - a hand-given pairing of that recording to a person always wins;
  - a person whose name is the same, ignoring letter case;
  - the closest name, when at least half of the recording name's parts match a person's name parts (a part found inside another part counts as a partial match). This catches a first name given alone, and given and family names in the other order or spelt with a hyphen differently;
  - otherwise a new person, named after the recording.
  A sound file whose name matches no known interview clip is skipped and reported, never guessed. Folders named "archive", "script docs" or "_archive" in any case are never treated as people.
- Flag: differs: the present product has two copies of the close-name rule that score partial matches slightly differently, so in rare cases one recording could be filed under different people by different steps.

### 41-004: The product decides the starting timecode every word time is counted from.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Facts about Resolve: rates, multicams, timecode
- What: Word times, subtitle times and the transcript all carry the clip's own timecode, so a word's time matches what the editing program shows. The start is taken, in order: from a start a person gives; the clip's own start timecode; the start of a timeline made from it; the project's timeline start setting; otherwise 01:00:00:00. Clips with no picture timecode (outside montage clips) start at zero.

### 41-005: The product decides each interview's frame rate.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Frame rates
- What: The frame rate recorded with an interview is the clip's own rate, otherwise the project's timeline rate. Where nothing gives a rate, 29.97 is assumed (this is the default for timecode typed with frames). For montage clips the rate is read from the file, otherwise the same default.

### 41-006: A stereo recording with all its sound in one channel is made one-channel, so it plays in both ears.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Cameras, sound recorders and their clocks
- What: An interview recording whose sound sits in only one side of a stereo file plays in one ear in headphones. The product decides on its own: it measures each channel's loudest point over the whole file and rewrites the file as true one-channel sound only when exactly one channel rises above a silence floor of 30 dB below full scale. It leaves a file alone, saying why, when it already has one channel, has no sound, has sound in more than one channel (with each channel's level), is silent in every channel, or cannot be measured. The same sound on both channels is never changed. The rewritten file keeps the original's sample depth (24-bit when the original is not plain samples), may be larger than 4 GB, keeps its embedded details, and must be one channel and the same length to the millisecond before it replaces the original; any failure leaves the original as it was. Overrides a person has today: change the silence floor; force the change for a recorder known to write one side only, taking whichever channel is louder on average.
- Flag: differs: run by a person, the stereo original is moved into a "legacy" folder beside it (a name already there gets " (2)", " (3)"), and if that move fails nothing is changed; done automatically during transcription, the stereo original is not kept.

### 41-007: What footage-in must never do to a recording.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Footage files and files made from them
- What: Files made from a recording (one-channel sound, previews, transcripts) appear whole or not at all: a half-made file is never visible under its final name. A person's own files are never deleted. A change to an original recording (41-006) is checked before it replaces the original.

### 41-008: The product decides the language of every stretch of speech.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Speech-to-text: rulings
- What: Interviews often mix languages. Unless a person fixes one language for the whole recording, the product decides per stretch:
  - speech is cut into pieces at pauses of half a second or more, each at most 20 s long;
  - each piece of at least 1 s has its language judged; a judgement under 60 % sure decides nothing, and an undecided piece takes the language of the piece before it (at the start, the first decided one);
  - neighbouring pieces in one language form a run;
  - a run with less than 3 s of speech whose language differs from all its neighbours is taken into the run before it (or after it at the very start), so a stray word or two is not transcribed as another language;
  - each stretch is then transcribed in its own language, and the transcript stays in time order.
  When this cannot be done, the language is judged per 30 seconds instead, and failing that once for the whole recording, and the person is told which happened. Each recording records how its language was decided (per stretch, per 30 seconds, or fixed by a person) and the minutes per language, shown for example as "en (en 27.1 min, zh 1.4 min, 3 runs)".

### 41-009: The product decides each recording's main language.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Speech-to-text: rulings
- What: A recording's main language is the one with the most seconds of speech; a tie goes to the language heard first. A recording is marked mixed-language when more than one was heard.

### 41-010: Chinese is always written in Traditional characters with Taiwan phrasing, and the house form of one character.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Chinese script
- What: Every stretch judged to be Mandarin, or containing any Chinese character, is converted to Traditional Chinese as written in Taiwan, phrase by phrase with Taiwan phrasing, and then every 臺 becomes the house-style 台. The words of a stretch are converted together as one phrase and then split back, so each word's text matches the sentence text exactly (converting word by word made some paragraphs impossible to find in the word timing). Overrides: no conversion; keep 臺; another conversion. When conversion cannot be done, the text stays as heard and the person is told. Montage clips are converted only when judged exactly Mandarin or containing Chinese characters.

### 41-011: The transcript, its subtitles and the word times come from one hearing, so they always agree.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: The text of an interview, its subtitle lines and the time of each word come from one transcription, so a paragraph can always be found in the word times. A stretch that came back with no separate words becomes one word covering the whole stretch, so its text is never lost. The text keeps a pause marker "(...)" where speech paused for a while.

### 41-012: The product decides when a recording needs transcribing again.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: A recording already transcribed is not done again while its sound is unchanged: same size and modified time (to a hundredth of a second), or failing that the same content at its start and end. A recording whose words are missing, empty or whose sound has changed is transcribed again. Override: transcribe again anyway.

### 41-013: Transcribing shows its progress and says when it is running slowly.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Speech-to-text: facts and situations
- What: While a recording is transcribed the person sees, at most every 3 seconds: which recording of how many, the percentage, minutes done of the total, lines so far, the language and the time left. On a computer whose graphics card cannot be used, transcription still runs on the processor, and the person is told the reason and that it will be much slower. At the end of a batch the person sees how many were done, failed and skipped, minutes of sound against minutes taken, and each failure with its reason; one failed recording never stops the others.

### 41-014: Nothing an interviewee said may be missing from the transcript.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Speech-to-text: rulings
- What: A transcript must cover the whole recording, including its end. The present product checks this against a second source and tells the person:
  - a gap between lines counts when it is at least ten times the typical gap between lines and never under 6 s (or a length a person sets);
  - a gap is dropped speech when the second source heard at least 2 s of speech in it, otherwise it is genuine silence;
  - a transcript ending more than 10 s before the clip ends is cut short; a sound file more than 1 s shorter than its clip is short;
  - the result lists every dropped stretch with its times and the total, a chart of how much of each minute is covered, and every run of minutes under 35 % covered; with no dropped stretch it says so plainly.
  Dropped stretches left in become holes on the board, so the person is told to fix them before anything else.

### 41-015: Facts about Resolve's own transcription.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Facts about Resolve's own transcription
- What:
  - Resolve's own speech-to-text silently stops early on 32-bit float recordings that carry samples far above full scale. A 24-bit copy of the same sound does not have the problem.
  - Resolve keeps an existing transcription: running it again without clearing it first returns the same, cut-short result.
  - Resolve's scripting gives no way to change a multicam's angles; swapping the sound on a multicam can only be done by hand.
  - Resolve's transcript holds its own "(...)" pause markers and paragraph breaks, and its subtitle exports carry the project's start timecode (normally one hour).
  - An in or out mark left on a clip silently shortens anything rendered from it.
  - Resolve's own transcription can only be started by a person inside Resolve.

### 41-016: The product decides where paragraphs break and how long one may be.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: A transcript is split into paragraphs by meaning, each one card on the board. A plain first text breaks at every pause of 1.2 s or more. A paragraph may hold at most about 60 s of speech, aiming at about 20 s; a longer one is refused until it is split at the answer's own turns, and the person is shown each over-long paragraph with its length and opening words. Length is estimated at 4.5 Chinese characters a second when any line holds 10 or more Chinese characters, otherwise 2.5 words a second; addresses and source lines do not count. Overrides today: another limit, or allow long paragraphs. A report gives the paragraph count, the typical, ninth-decile and longest length and how many are over the limit.

### 41-017: Splitting a paragraph never changes a single character of what was said.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: A paragraph is split at points named by their exact opening words; for a paragraph holding both the English and the original, the split points are given per language, both must give the same number of parts, and the parts are paired in order. A split is refused, with nothing written, when a split point is not found, is found twice, or is at the very start; when a line or paragraph would lose or gain any character other than spacing; when it would give only one part; or when the languages give different numbers of parts. The person is shown how many paragraphs became how many and which are still too long, and can try it without writing.

### 41-018: The product decides which lines are interviewer questions.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: Lines where the crew asks a question are marked as interviewer questions. A paragraph counts as a question when every line of it is so marked. A question is kept directly above the answer it belongs to, on the board as in the transcript.

### 41-019: The interviewee's wording is never altered, in any language.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: Making paragraphs, marking questions and pairing the English with the original never change the words heard in any language; only line breaks and markers are added.

### 41-020: Every paragraph has a permanent address that survives edits elsewhere.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: Each paragraph gets a short address of 6 lowercase letters and digits, unique within its transcript; a paragraph that already has one keeps it. Across a shoot each paragraph also has a key made from the person and its address, so editing one person's transcript never changes another's paragraphs. Two different paragraphs with the same key stop the work with a message naming them and saying to give one a new address. A finished transcript is never overwritten by a new one without a person deleting or renaming it first.

### 41-021: Every paragraph in another language gets English, by fixed rules.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: English is given for every paragraph that has Mandarin and no English. The rules: fluent English as for documentary subtitles; every name transliterated; nothing but the translation, on one line. English that still contains any Chinese character counts as missing. An answer with Chinese characters in it is rejected and asked again more firmly, and a third time with one worked example; after three refusals the paragraph keeps its Mandarin alone and the person is told. A connection failure fails only that paragraph. A line counts as the Mandarin one when at least a fifth of it is Chinese. Each paragraph ends up as: question marks, English, then the original, then its address.
- Flag: differs: interview transcripts are translated by the assistant while the paragraphs are made; montage clips by a separate translator on the studio's own computer, which needs no outside account.

### 41-022: English and original words are linked phrase by phrase, on their own, and only what is new is done.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Word links between the original and the English
- What: For every finished transcript the product lines up English phrases with the original phrases and links words between them, for the editor to show the English a cut carries. It runs by itself once a transcript is finished, does only what is new, and when it reaches its limit on assistant calls it stops, says so, and carries on the next time.
- Flag: undetermined: what exactly is written, and how big the limit is, is not visible from this part.

### 41-023: Every interview gets a small picture preview exactly as long as its sound.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Footage files and files made from them
- What: Beside each interview's sound the product makes a small preview: 480 lines tall keeping the picture's shape, the multicam's frame rate, a fresh full frame at least every second so it can be scrubbed, mono sound from the camera, exactly as long as the interview sound, black before and after the picture, lined up with the sound from the first frame. Several camera pieces are laid over one black background, each fitted to the first one's shape, their sounds mixed. A graphics card is used when one can be. A preview already there is kept unless a person asks to remake it. Raw camera files are taken from their proxies. Making previews never needs the editing program open.

### 41-024: The product decides which picture the preview shows.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Grouping clips and building the multicam
- What: The preview shows the multicam's first angle; if that angle has no picture the next angle with picture is used, and the person is told. Every camera piece is placed at its own time; where pieces overlap, the later one wins and the person is told how much of the earlier still shows. A piece whose camera file is missing shows black and is noted. Clips nested inside an angle are opened (up to 8 deep); one that cannot be opened shows black. Still images are skipped with a note. A piece that may have a speed change is noted and kept only if its sound check passes (41-025). The person is told how many seconds of the whole are black.

### 41-025: The product decides where each camera piece sits in time, by matching its sound.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Grouping clips and building the multicam
- What: Before a preview is made, each camera piece's own sound is matched against the interview sound at 6 points along it: up to 15 s of the interview sound, searched for within 25 s either side of where the piece is said to sit. A point counts when the match is clearly strong. At least 3 points, and at least half of those tried, must agree within two frames; the agreed offset then moves the picture. A piece too short to test (under 4 s) borrows the offset of another piece from the same file. A piece that fails is dropped, whatever it covered shows through, and the check runs again, up to 4 times, with the reason given (how many points matched, or that the file is too short for the piece). If nothing usable remains, no preview is made and the person is told why. Override today: keep a failed piece where it was said to sit, marked unverified.

### 41-026: A finished preview is checked against the sound, and a preview more than a frame out is flagged.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Footage files and files made from them
- What: After a preview is made its sound is compared with the interview sound at 5 points inside each piece, and the person is shown the offset in milliseconds with a clear warning when it is off by more than a frame (1/30 s), or that no clear match was found. A preview that is off is not remade or withheld.

### 41-027: A preview that cannot be made never stops anything else.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Footage files and files made from them
- What: Every recording without a preview gets one plain reason (not a multicam, sound check failed, no way to read where the clips sit, and so on); the others carry on, and the person sees how many were made, already there and failed.

### 41-028: The product finds where the crew asks the interviewee to introduce themselves.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Grouping clips and building the multicam
- What: The real start of an interview is where the crew asks the person to introduce themselves; the multicam is meant to begin there. The product listens to the opening 15 minutes (a person can move or widen the window) and looks, earliest first within each search:
  1. for a question: 自我介紹, 介紹一下, 介紹自己, 介紹您自己, 介紹你自己, 先介紹, 簡單介紹, 請介紹 (in either script), or, ignoring case, "introduce yourself", "introduce yourselves", "tell us who you are", "tell me who you are", "who you are and what you do", "your name and", "start by introducing";
  2. for a bare 介紹;
  3. for the answer, only in the first 10 minutes: a line that begins 我是, 我叫 or 大家好 after any filler, or "my name is", or "I'm" / "I am" followed within 100 characters by "and I".
  The moment chosen is 0.75 s before the question (1.5 s before an answer), never before the window. The person sees the time ready to paste into the editing program, how it was found, and the line with two lines either side; or "not found" with the lines heard, and advice to widen or move the window.

### 41-029: Each interviewee is given a colour on their own.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Grouping clips and building the multicam
- What: Each interviewee gets one colour, used for their cards on the board and for their clips in the editing program, assigned automatically when footage comes in; until then a person sees "no colour assigned yet". How the colour is chosen belongs to another part of the inventory.

### 41-030: A folder of outside clips for a montage becomes searchable transcripts with word times.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Montages
- What: Clips gathered for a montage (for example news clips) are transcribed like interviews, with word times, a readable transcript with paragraph addresses, and English (41-021). Every video or sound file in the folder and below is taken, except folders of proxies or of transcripts or clips, hidden folders, and files that are partial, temporary, failed or original copies kept by a download or conversion. A second file with the same name in one batch is skipped and the person is told. A clip downloaded from YouTube carries its channel, title, address and upload date into its transcript. The person is told before starting how many files there are, how many are already done and how many are to do, and can see the list without doing it.

### 41-031: A montage clip's readable transcript is laid out by pauses, and hand edits are kept.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Montages
- What: Paragraphs break where speech pauses, with a "(...)" marker in the gap. Speaker headings appear only when more than one speaker was found. A transcript someone has edited by hand is never overwritten unless a person asks to redo it, and the person is told it was left alone.

### 41-032: The product can decide who is speaking in a montage clip.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Montages
- What: When asked, the product separates the speakers in each montage clip. Each word is given the speaker it overlaps most; a word overlapping no speaker takes the nearest within half a second, else no speaker. The person sees the number of speakers and turns per clip. If separating speakers fails for any reason, the transcript is kept with no speakers and the reason is recorded and shown; when the reason is that an outside account has not accepted the terms needed, the person is told that trying again will not help.
- Flag: dormant: off unless a person asks for it on a batch.

### 41-033: A person's edits to a transcript are never lost to an automatic redo.
- Where: cards-footage-in / The transcript and its English
- What: When a better transcription replaces an earlier one, the earlier text and subtitles are kept once as a backup. Today a second replacement overwrites the current text with no new backup, so hand edits made after the first replacement are lost; that must not happen in the rebuild.

### 41-034: The assistant proposes themes for the board, and a person picks, renames and adds.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: Having read every paragraph of every interview, the assistant proposes themes: broad headings, always including "Personal Stories" and "Ending", and any theme that would hold 80 or more paragraphs is split. Each proposed theme has a name, a description of what belongs and the evidence for it. The person ticks which to keep (a counter shows how many are ticked against a target, 12 unless the proposal sets one), renames any, and adds their own with a description that paragraphs are matched against; then the first sort runs.
- Flag: differs: the page says up to 15 themes; the assistant is asked for at most 25.

### 41-035: The owner's rules for the first sort of paragraphs onto the board.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Every paragraph goes into exactly one theme. "Uncategorised" is only for paragraphs with no content, and must hold under a tenth of them. An interviewer question goes with its answer, directly above it. Each theme has a role: personal stories, body or ending; body themes are placed in story order. Paragraph text is copied exactly, never reworded.

### 41-036: One shoot's interviews can feed several episodes, each with its own board.
- Where: cards-board / What the board is
- What: When one shoot is cut into several episodes, they share one set of transcripts, and each episode gets its own themes, sort and board. With no episodes named, the episode is named after the shoot. The list of episodes must be a non-empty list of names, else the person is told. An unknown episode name quietly opens the first episode. Today the shoot's paragraphs must be divided among the episodes by hand before themes can be proposed; the person is told so.

### 41-037: The assistant can search every transcript for an idea and return exact quotes.
- Where: cards-editor / Search and the transcript panel
- What: A search over one person's transcript or a whole episode finds the paragraphs that discuss an idea, not just a word, and gives the best passages as exact quotes. Every quote is copied from the transcript, never retyped; a result naming a paragraph that does not exist, an unknown person, no paragraph, or a score other than 3 (on point), 2 (relevant) or 1 (tangential) is refused before anything is written. The results show the date, scope, counts by score, an optional summary, which people had hits and their best score, who had nothing, and per hit its stars, the reason, and the nearest interviewer question within the 4 paragraphs before it. A results page is named by date, person and query (cut to 90 characters) and never overwrites an earlier one. The same search can also list transcripts with their paragraph counts, find every paragraph matching a pattern with paragraphs either side, and show paragraphs by address. Where a person has several transcripts, the one with the most paragraphs is used and the person is told.
- Flag: undetermined: today this is a separate assistant tool writing pages for the notes program, outside the product.

### 41-038: Chinese text is compared as the same words whatever script each side is in.
- Where: cards-editor / Chinese text and matching
- What: Matching text, searching and placing a cut compare Traditional (including Taiwan forms such as 臺) and Simplified Chinese as the same words, character for character, without changing lengths or positions and without rewriting any text. The present table holds 4,112 character pairs. If it cannot be used, texts are compared exactly.

### 41-039: Subtitle lines made from a transcript break at sentences and keep to a line length.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Subtitles and delivery
- What: Subtitle lines end at a paragraph break, at sentence-ending punctuation (Latin or Chinese, with any closing quote or bracket), or before they pass 60 characters, or 20 when the language is Chinese, Japanese, Korean or Cantonese (decided from the language recorded, else from more than 30 % wide characters in the first 400 words); a person can set another length. Pause markers are left out. A line too short to have length gets one millisecond. Times carry the clip's start timecode, as Resolve's own subtitle exports do.
- Flag: undetermined: today these subtitles are a working copy on the way to the transcript, not a delivery the owner chose.

### 41-040: Footage-in today runs on one Windows computer only.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: Every piece of the present path from recording to transcript assumes Windows; nothing of it exists for macOS. The heavy work (transcribing, previews) prefers a computer with a graphics card. The present page for running it is reachable only from that same computer and asks nobody to sign in.
- Flag: differs: Windows only today.
