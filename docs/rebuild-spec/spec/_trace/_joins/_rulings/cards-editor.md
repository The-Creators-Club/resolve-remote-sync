## Questions for the owner

### Q1: When your notes name an interviewer's question for a place in the film, does that question play in the cut there?
- Findings: cards-board F1
- The two sides: the board flags a question in play unless your notes name it for that place, and then shows it as exempt ("his one exception is a question his notes place"); the editor refuses any edit that would put an interviewer's question in the cut ("no questions included in the edit ever"), so when the board is handed over, the named question could only go to the shelf.
- Options: A: a question your notes name for a place is handed to the cut there and plays, shown as exempt in the editor too; every other question still goes to the shelf and cannot be placed. B: no question ever plays: the board may hold a named question in its place, but the hand-over puts it on the shelf like every other question.
- Recommend: B, because "no questions included in the edit ever" is the rule the editor enforces everywhere, and one rule with no exemption has nothing to check.
- What you would notice: with A, a question your notes name can be heard in the cut where you put it; with B, it waits on the shelf and the cut never contains a question.

### Q2: Should the editor mark filler words (hesitations such as "um" and "uh") as one of its reading aids at all?
- Findings: cards-editor-1 F3
- The two sides: the editor's reading aids include "Fillers: hesitations and filler words, marked as trimmable" (greyed with a faint strike-through), part of the ten aids you asked for; an earlier ruling of yours lists "hesitation and filler marks" among features that must never be built again ("lets ditch all this, it's not useful actually").
- Options: A: keep the fillers aid, off until a person switches it on. B: keep it, on by default for languages you do not read, like the other aids. C: take it out; the reading aids are the other nine.
- Recommend: C, because you said of these marks "lets ditch all this", and one fewer aid is one fewer part.
- What you would notice: with A or B, filler words can be greyed and struck through on the cards, the transcript and the lane; with C they never are.
