## Choices

### 22.1 Should every interview's sound be loudness-levelled on its own, or only a recording a person swaps in?

- **Today:** a one-off repair that puts the Zoom recording under every angle also levels it to -23 LUFS (EBU R128) on its own; the normal build does not level anything.
- **Options:** A: level every interview's sound to -23 LUFS on its own, with a switch to turn it off per interview. B: level only a recording a person swaps in (which your rule on swapping audio requires), and leave the normal interview sound as recorded.
- **Recommended:** B, because it keeps the recorded sound untouched unless a person acts, and the swap rule already asks for levelling there.
- **What you would notice:** with A every interview arrives at one loudness; with B interviews sound as recorded and the editor sets levels later.
- (cards-footage-in-Q1)

### 22.2 When an interview's preview is found to be more than a frame out of sync, what should happen?

- **Today:** it is only flagged on the run's report; the preview is used anyway.
- **Options:** A: keep it, with a warning on the interview and on its cards, and a way to make it again. B: hold it back until it is made again in sync. C: make it again on its own.
- **Recommended:** A, because a preview is only for watching while cutting and a warning tells the editor what to trust, with no extra machinery.
- **What you would notice:** with A the editor sees the preview at once with a warning; with B no picture until fixed; with C it may change under them.
- (cards-footage-in-Q2)

### 22.3 Should one translator make the English for both interviews and montage clips, and which?

- **Today:** interview English is made by the assistant while paragraphs are made; montage-clip English by a separate translator on the studio's own computer that needs no outside account.
- **Options:** A: the assistant for both; with the assistant switched off, no English is made on its own and a person can write it. B: a translator on the studio's own computers for both, which keeps working with the assistant off but cannot correct mishearings or transliterate names as well. C: keep both.
- **Recommended:** A, one way, and the one that can follow your rules (names transliterated, mishearings corrected only in the English).
- **What you would notice:** with A, montage English is of the same quality as interview English, and a site with the assistant off gets no English on its own; with B a site with the assistant off still gets English.
- (cards-footage-in-Q3)

### 22.4 When no computer with a usable graphics card is free, should transcription wait for one, or run slowly at once on whatever computer can reach the footage?

- **Today:** transcription runs on the processor of the computer it was given when its graphics card cannot be used, and says it will be much slower.
- **Options:** A: wait for a computer with a usable card; run on a processor only when the studio has no such computer, or when a person picks one. B: run at once on any free computer, card or not.
- **Recommended:** A, because a run on a processor can take many times longer and hold a computer for hours that a card would free in minutes.
- **What you would notice:** with A an interview may wait for the card computer to be free; with B it starts sooner but may finish later.
- (cards-footage-in-Q4)

### 22.5 Does your rule that a card holds at least two sentences apply to an interviewer's question and to chatter too?

- **Today:** paragraphs are made with one interviewer question each and chatter in small paragraphs of its own, which is often one sentence; your rule says a card is never a single sentence.
- **Options:** A: the two-sentence floor applies to answer paragraphs; a question stays its own paragraph directly above its answer, and chatter stays apart. B: it applies to every paragraph, so a one-sentence question is joined into its answer and chatter into a neighbour.
- **Recommended:** A, because a question travels with its answer anyway and chatter is left out of the themes, so both stay findable and separable.
- **What you would notice:** with A a question is a small card of its own above its answer; with B the question is the first sentence of the answer's card.
- (cards-footage-in-Q5)

### 22.6 When someone has edited the readable Markdown copy of a transcript in another program, what should happen when the product writes the copy again?

- **Today:** a montage clip's readable transcript that someone edited by hand is never overwritten unless a person asks to redo it; your requirement says the product keeps the copy in step and does not follow changes made to it elsewhere.
- **Options:** A: set the edited copy aside under a name that says so, write a fresh copy, and tell the person. B: overwrite it. C: stop updating that copy until a person decides.
- **Recommended:** A, because nothing anyone typed is lost and the copy is still kept in step.
- **What you would notice:** with A an edited copy turns up beside the fresh one; with B outside edits vanish; with C the copy goes stale.
- (cards-footage-in-Q6)

### 22.7 When one drop holds several sessions (gaps of more than 30 minutes), should each session become an interview of its own on its own?

- **Today:** building refuses until a person names one session, or asks for all sessions to be laid out together.
- **Options:** A: each session becomes its own interview on its own, and a person can join sessions. B: the whole drop is one interview, and a person can split it.
- **Recommended:** A, because a gap of half an hour almost always means another interview, and a join is one act.
- **What you would notice:** with A a day of three interviews arrives as three; with B it arrives as one to be split.
- (cards-footage-in-Q7)

### 22.8 How close must a device's timecode be to its file dates for the timecode to count as the time of day?

- **Today:** the one-step build accepts timecode as the time of day within 60 seconds of every clip's creation date; the older way accepted it only within 3 seconds, or used each camera's own timecode from its first frame when asked.
- **Options:** A: within 60 seconds. B: within 3 seconds.
- **Recommended:** A, because file dates are only good to a second and slightly-off clocks are common, and the sound places every take within a frame afterwards anyway.
- **What you would notice:** almost none; with B more cameras are placed by file date before the sound lines them up.
- (cards-footage-in-Q8)
