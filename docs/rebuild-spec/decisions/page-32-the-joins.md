## 32.1 When an editor signs in on a new computer, does it start syncing straight away, or wait until an admin approves it?

- **The two sides:** one piece says signing in on the computer is all it
  takes, because the admin already let that person in. Four others (the
  dashboard, syncing, the editor's app and the server set-up) have the new
  computer wait on a list until an admin approves it and confirms whose it
  is, and the editor's finish page says "waiting for your admin to approve
  this computer". Your ruling says an admin grants a person access "with no
  other step"; your answer to 8.14 speaks of approval as something that
  exists.
- **Options:** A: signing in is enough. The computer is the signed-in
  person's and gets their projects within about a minute; the admin sees it
  appear and can end it ("a device was lost"). B: the computer waits until
  an admin approves it under its owner (filled in from the sign-in), and
  nothing syncs until then.
- **Recommended:** A, because the admin already decided the person may come
  in and the sign-in already says whose computer it is, so approval asks the
  admin to confirm what is known. It is one list, one warning and one
  waiting page fewer.
- **What you would notice:** with A a new or reinstalled laptop syncs
  minutes after the editor signs in, with no admin step. With B the editor
  waits until an admin presses approve.
- (accounts-privacy-legal F2, editor-app F1, sync-2 F3, dashboard-1 F24)

## 32.2 When your notes name an interviewer's question for a place in the film, does that question play in the cut there?

- **The two sides:** the board flags a question in play unless your notes
  name it for that place, and then shows it as exempt ("his one exception is
  a question his notes place"). The editor refuses any edit that would put
  an interviewer's question in the cut ("no questions included in the edit
  ever"). Both are your rulings.
- **Options:** A: a question your notes name for a place is handed to the
  cut there and plays, shown as exempt in the editor too; every other
  question still goes to the shelf and cannot be placed. B: no question
  ever plays: the board may hold a named question in its place, but the
  hand-over puts it on the shelf like every other question.
- **Recommended:** B, because "no questions included in the edit ever" is
  the rule the editor enforces everywhere, and one rule with no exemption
  has nothing to check.
- **What you would notice:** with A, a question your notes name can be
  heard in the cut where you put it. With B, it waits on the shelf and the
  cut never contains a question.
- (cards-board F1)

## 32.3 Should the editor mark filler words ("um", "uh") as one of its reading aids at all?

- **The two sides:** the editor's reading aids include "Fillers:

  strike-through), one of the ten aids. An earlier ruling of yours lists
  "hesitation and filler marks" among features never to be built again
  ("lets ditch all this, it's not useful actually").
- **Options:** A: keep the fillers aid, off until a person switches it on.
  B: keep it, on by default for languages you do not read, like the other

- **Recommended:** C, because you said of these marks "lets ditch all

- **What you would notice:** with A or B, filler words can be greyed and
  struck through on the cards, the transcript and the lane. With C they

- (cards-editor-1 F3)

## 32.4 When an interview's camera and recorder cards are copied and sorted in the Footage Sorter, does that interview turn up in Timeline Cards and start processing by itself?

- **The two sides:** Timeline Cards says footage brought in from camera and
  recorder cards reaches the episode on its own. The Footage Sorter files
  the clips into the episode's interviewee folder and hands nothing to
  Timeline Cards.
- **Options:** A: clips the Sorter files into an episode's interviewee
  folder are taken into that episode in Timeline Cards and processed with
  no further step (when both parts are switched on). B: the Sorter files
  them, and a person drops the interview's clips onto the episode in
  Timeline Cards, as with any other footage.
- **Recommended:** A, because you asked that "the whole pipeline should be
  automated" and that no step waits for a person, and the product already
  knows whose interview those clips are. B has fewer parts (dropping clips
  on the episode is then the only way in), so say B if you would rather
  start each interview yourself.
- **What you would notice:** with A an interview starts transcribing and
  translating as soon as its cards are sorted, using computer time and the
  assistant's budget without being asked. With B it starts when someone
  drops its clips onto the episode.
- (footage-sorter-and-camera-cards-2 F1)

## 32.5 A link that lets a distant client download full-quality folders: who makes it, and does it exist when the Footage Sorter is switched off?

- **The two sides:** the b-roll piece describes a "delivery link" any
  editor can make, revoke, bring back and renew. The Footage Sorter's piece
  describes a folder link only an admin makes and can only switch off. They
  are the same job, and by your answer to 21.5 there are two kinds of link
  handed outside the studio (a folder to download, and a client folder to
  preview), so there will be ONE download link whatever you answer here.
- **Options:** A: an admin makes it, from the Footage Sorter; a studio with
  the Sorter switched off cannot hand a client full-quality files. B: an
  admin makes it for any folder of the studio's tree, whatever parts are
  switched on; it sits in the one list of links with the others. C: as B,
  and any editor may make one, not only an admin.
- **Recommended:** B, because handing a client a folder does not depend on
  camera cards, and an admin deciding what leaves the studio is your
  existing ruling.
- **What you would notice:** with A, switching the Sorter off also removes
  the way to send a client full-quality files. With C, editors can hand
  footage outside the studio without an admin.
- (broll F6, footage-sorter-and-camera-cards-3 F1)

## 32.6 A file moved on the server that one computer has still not followed after 14 days: does the move lapse, or wait however long it takes?

- **The two sides:** your answer to 6.15 gave every request aimed at one
  computer one rule: it waits up to 14 days, then lapses and says so. An
  older lesson says a move waits for each computer however long it is
  offline or its drive is away, because a computer that never followed the
  move still holds the file in its old place and sends it up again when it
  comes back.
- **Options:** A: a file move never lapses; it is the one request that
  waits for as long as the computer is away. B: it lapses after 14 days
  like every other request, the admin is told, and the file may come back
  in its old place when that computer returns.
- **Recommended:** A, because the whole point of moving through the product
  is that the file does not come back, and a laptop away for three weeks is
  ordinary.
- **What you would notice:** with B, after a long absence a moved file can
  reappear where it used to be.

## 32.8 The red "Send to Resolve" button in the editor's header: does it stay?

- **The two sides:** the editor you use today has a red "Send to Resolve"
  button in its header (and a row in the phone's More sheet). A ruling of
  yours from 31 August, made on the page the editor has since replaced, says
  sending is an action on the cut and "the top bar holds no such button". On
  12 November you said the editor's screens, keys and gestures are kept
  exactly. The settlers followed the August ruling, so the specification now
  has no Send button in the header; and because nothing says where "the
  cut's own actions" are, a fixer placed Send on the open cut list's row in
  the Cut lists sheet, which is a place of its own invention.
- **Options:** A: the header keeps its Send to Resolve button, exactly as
  the editor has it today, and the August ruling is retired. B: no button in
  the header; say where Send lives instead (the Cut lists sheet row, or
  somewhere else you name). Either way its key and the command palette
  entry stay, and pressing it opens the Send tab with a fresh dry run.
- **Recommended:** A, because "keep the editor exactly" is your later word
  and is about this editor, and it needs no new place to be invented.
- **What you would notice:** with A nothing changes from what you use now.
  With B the red button leaves the header.
