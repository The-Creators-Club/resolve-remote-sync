### 30-016: A find box searches both lines of a transcript, folding Chinese scripts, Russian ё and apostrophes, and understands quotes, paragraph ids and pinyin.
- Where: cards-editor / Chinese text and matching
- What:
  - The box: "Find: words, or pinyin (chu1, chuqu, 出qu)", in the transcript header, with a count and previous/next buttons (disabled with fewer than 2 hits).
  - Count: empty when nothing is typed; "0" with no hits; "17" after typing (no current hit yet); "3 / 17" once Enter has gone to a hit.
  - Plain text: case-insensitive, on both lines, Simplified and Traditional characters treated as the same (醫院 finds 医院, 陳 finds 陈). Russian ё is treated as е, and the typographic apostrophes (’ ʼ ‘ ` ´) as a plain apostrophe.
  - Quoted ("..." or with curly quotes, at least 3 characters including the quotes): exact case, no script folding, and whole word wherever the query's edge is a letter or digit that is not a Han character ("I" does not match the i in "it"; Cyrillic counts as letters).
  - "^id" (the first non-space character is ^, then letters, digits, underscore or hyphen): goes to the paragraph with that id (case-insensitive). Only when no paragraph has the whole id, every paragraph whose id starts with it.
  - Mixed Han and Latin letters (老ren, yi院, 出qu, guo王): a pinyin search. Characters match themselves and each letter run must be the sound of the characters in that spot, where a prefix of each reading is enough (dh finds 的話).
  - Pure pinyin (letters only, with optional tone digits 1 to 5, spaces or apostrophes; at least 2 letters): each Han character takes a whole syllable, the last may still be being typed (chuq finds 出去). A digit after a syllable is its tone (5 = neutral); without one any tone matches. ü is typed v (lv = 綠). Ordinary text hits are kept as well ("he" finds the English word and 和). Every reading of a character counts (得 = de and dei). A query longer than 64 characters is never pinyin.
  - When the server cannot convert Chinese scripts or give readings, the find is case-folded only (no Chinese folding, no pinyin).
  - Each key gives its answer at once, even in a long transcript.
- Exact: yes

### 30-017: Typing in the find marks hits without moving anything; Enter steps to a hit and cues it so Space plays it; Ctrl+F reaches the box from anywhere.

### 30-018: The transcript plays its own audio, at the lane's levels, and Space decides what to play in a fixed order.
- Where: cards-editor / Playback and audio
- What:
  - The transcript plays the clip's audio from a source second, optionally stopping at another; only the newest request ever plays.
  - Loudness: it plays at the same per-clip level the lane uses (each interview levelled to the cut's speech level, through the master volume and limiter), gliding as playback moves. If that cannot be had, the audio plays as recorded.
  - Player row in the header: a Play/Pause button (key hint Space, disabled when the transcript has no timings) and the time m:ss while playing. Its tooltip says what Space will play: "From ^<ref> at m:ss", "The selection, N.N s", "Click a word to put the playhead there", or "No timings: this transcript cannot play". Only an error shows as visible text.
  - Space (or the Play button), in order:
    1. if the transcript is playing: pause;
    2. if the transcript has no clip or no timings: "this transcript has no timings, so it cannot play";
    3. if a find hit was just cued and the head is still on it and no text is selected: play the cued hit;
    4. if a selection made with the controller is live: play it from 0.4 s before to 1 s after;
    5. if the transcript playhead is placed: play on from its word (no end);
    6. if a text selection with timings exists: play it from 0.4 s before to 1 s after;
    7. a text selection with no timings: "that selection has no word timings to play: insert it, or pick the paragraph";
    8. else the first picked paragraph that is timed, from its start to its end;
    9. else the cut the panel was opened on (if it is of this transcript's clips), its in to its out;
    10. else: "click a word to put the transcript playhead there, then press Space".
  - Hold Space (in the panel, held for 280 ms or more): plays at 2x while held (starting playback if it was stopped). Releasing returns to 1x; if it was stopped before the hold, it pauses where it got to. A tap under 280 ms acts on release as Space above. Losing window focus ends a hold the same way.
  - Space anywhere else while the transcript plays (not in a text box, button or editable area, no modifiers): pauses the transcript and does not start the lane.
  - One thing plays at a time: starting the transcript pauses the lane; the lane starting pauses the transcript.
  - While playing, the transcript playhead follows the spoken word on the line it is on (or the cursor's line, or the original line when it has timings), 20 ms ahead; when it moves to another paragraph, that line is scrolled into view if needed.
  - Errors (status line and the player row): "There is no audio for <clip> on this server, so the transcript cannot play it."; "It would not play: <reason>" (a pause before play started is not reported).
- Exact: yes

  - Skipped: an English interview, a paragraph or card with no original line, or a line whose language cannot be told.

### 30-040: The aids read each line's grammar, words and names in Russian, Ukrainian, Chinese and English, and fall back to word lists when that analysis is missing.
- Where: cards-editor / The English a cut shows
- What:
  - Russian, Ukrainian, Chinese (both scripts) and English lines are analysed for words, sentences, parts of speech, grammar, and names. Other languages get only the romanisation and no analysed aids. A Cyrillic line with no stated language counts as Ukrainian if it has і, є, ї or ґ, else Russian; a Han line counts as Chinese. Lines over 6,000 characters are not analysed.
  - Verbs: the grammar's verbs, not inside a compound. Numbers: numerals and amounts, money, percentages, ordinals. Names and places: named people, organisations, places and buildings; for Chinese, runs of proper nouns.
  - Dates: dates and times, plus each language's date words as whole words (months and weekdays with their case endings, yesterday/today/tomorrow; for Chinese, a number followed by 年 月 日 號 點 分 秒 週 星期, and 昨天/今天/明天/去年/今年/明年/前年/後天) when they are not used as adjectives, verbs or pronouns.
  - Negation, condition, contrast and filler word lists per language (Russian, Ukrainian, Chinese in both scripts, English), including two-word entries ("как бы", "you know", "тем не менее"). Chinese entries are found inside a word, longest first (沒有 before 沒). Sure words always count (for example не, если, однако, 如果, 但是, not, if, um); others count when the grammar agrees (a negating particle; a subordinating "if"; a joining "but" or a sentence-opening adverb; a discourse word, interjection, hesitation, or an opener followed by punctuation such as "Ну, ..." or "Well, ..."). Words the grammar marks negative or conditional are marked even if not listed.
  - Hesitations are always fillers: Russian э/эм/мм/хм, Ukrainian е/ем/мм/хм, Chinese 嗯呃欸誒诶唔, English uh/um/er/erm/hmm/mm/ah.
  - Without the analysis: only sure list words, hesitations and numbers written in digits; sentences by punctuation; no verbs, names, cut ticks or analysed marks. A small line above the panel's status line then says "Language aids are limited: the server has no language analysis (it is not installed), so only the word lists and the romanisation show." when a paragraph with an original line is on screen and any aid is on for its language; it has a dismiss x, remembered on this device for good.
  - Sentences: as the grammar finds them, else by punctuation (。！？!?…;； or a line break, a full stop followed by a space or the end and not by a digit, closing quotes and guillemets kept with their sentence).
  - Sentence numbers: when the original and its English have the same number of sentences, they are numbered in order; otherwise each English sentence takes the number of the original sentence most of its phrases point into, and one that points nowhere gets no number.
  - Analysis results are shared by every cut list and kept, so each line is analysed once.

### 30-041: Romanisation under Russian and Ukrainian follows the national standards and never leaves a Cyrillic letter.
- Where: cards-editor / The English a cut shows
- What: Russian uses BGN/PCGN (е is "ye" at a word start and after a vowel; ё "yo"; х "kh"; щ "shch"; ъ and ь dropped). Ukrainian uses the national system (є ї й ю я are "ye yi y yu ya" at a word start, "ie i i iu ia" inside; зг is "zgh"; apostrophe and ь dropped). The system is the clip's language, but a word with a letter only the other language writes uses the other system; letters of other Cyrillic alphabets get a plain fallback, so no Cyrillic letter ever appears in the Latin line. Capitals follow the original (all-caps words stay all caps). Latin words, digits and punctuation pass through. It needs no assistant.

### 30-042: The word-by-word gloss and the lit English of the spoken word come from the assistant's word links inside each phrase, checked before use.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Word links between the original and the English
- What:
  - Inside each phrase of a line-up the assistant links each English word to the original word(s) it renders, gives every original word a literal gloss of 1 to 3 words, and names runs of original words that form one word, name or fixed expression (linked and glossed as a whole, the gloss on its first word).
  - Kept only if: every link stays inside its phrase (a stray one is dropped, not the answer); at least half the phrases get a link; a gloss is at most 40 characters on one line; runs are 2 to 12 words and do not overlap (odd ones dropped). Word links go stale with their line-up.
  - The gloss line shows each original word's gloss in the original's order, "·" for a word with none, words inside a run skipped, two spaces between.
  - The English of a spoken word (aid 1): its word links; with none, its whole phrase, but only when that phrase is 4 English words or fewer. With word links, a spoken word no link names (a filler) lights nothing.
  - A cut's word links come from its paragraphs' links when every paragraph it lies in has them, without asking the assistant again. Glosses from several paragraphs on one word are joined with " / ".
  - A failed answer is final for that text; one with no answer is retried after an hour. They share the aids' daily allowance (30-039) and are kept with the episode's cut lists.

### 30-043: Safe cut points: green and red ticks between original words say whether a cut there leaves a whole thought, and Alt+S moves an edge or a pick to the nearest safe cut.
- Where: cards-editor / The lane: clicking, trimming, zooming and dragging
- What:
  - Between two timed words of the original: a cut may end there when nothing on the left waits for a word on the right, nothing the left needs went right (an object, a "that" clause, the rest of a name) and the left has a verb or a sentence root; a cut may start there when only a new clause hangs off the left and the right has its own verb. A sentence end is safe both ways; so is a gap with only fillers before it in its sentence, or only fillers after it. A gap that splits one word is unsafe ("This splits the word "<w>"").
  - Drawn: a green tick when both ways are safe; a red tick only where a cut is tempting (a pause of 0.35 s or more, or punctuation in the gap) but would strand a clause; nothing for ordinary gaps inside a clause. A hollow green tick is safe for the words but has less than 0.12 s of silence (the cut will clip). Each tick has a reason, for example "A sentence ends here", "A whole thought ends here", "Cutting here strands a clause: "<w>" is left waiting for "<h>"", "There is no verb yet", "What follows has no verb of its own", "<h> loses its negation <w>". Without the language analysis there are no ticks. The ticks are hidden from screen readers and keep their colours in high-contrast mode.
  - In a gap an out point goes a little after the left word (half the pause, at most 0.12 s), an in point a little before the right word (half the pause, at most 0.08 s). Edges are judged with up to 40 words (and 20 s) of the clip on either side.
  - Dragging an edge (with aid 4 on) snaps to the safe gap nearest the edge within 2.5 s whose side fits it (an out edge needs "may end", an in edge "may start"), preferring a gap with a breath by 0.15 s.
  - Alt+S (shortcut sheet: "Move the armed edge, or the picked words, to the nearest safe cut"), shown as a toast:
    - with words picked on the lane: moves both ends of the pick to safe boundaries inside its cut, each by the fewest words (up to 12; ties outward): "The pick now starts and ends where a cut leaves a whole thought." / "The pick already starts and ends at safe cuts." (does nothing when aid 4 is off);
    - else with an armed edge: moves it to the nearest safe cut within 6 s, one undo step: "The <in/out> point moved to the nearest safe cut. Ctrl+Z puts it back." / "No safer cut point near this edge (or its words are still being read)." (also when the nearest is under 0.02 s away). This works even when aid 4 is off;
    - neither: "Arm an edge (click near a cut’s end) or pick words first.".
- Flag: differs: Alt+S on an armed edge acts whether or not aid 4 is on, while the pick nudge and the drag snap do nothing when it is off.
- Exact: yes

### 30-044: The join check reads every new splice of one speaker again and says on the card and in the lane whether it reads well, changed the meaning, or broke the grammar.
- Where: cards-editor / The English a cut shows
- What:
  - A join is where two consecutive cuts meet and the speech should run on: the same clip (not simply continuing within 0.04 s with no gap) or the same speaker. Two speakers meeting is not checked. A gap row between them still counts; a heading row ends the run. Cuts whose word timings are not known yet are checked when they arrive.
  - Compared: the last words of the first piece (at most 30, from its last sentence start when that leaves at least 3), the first words of the second (at most 30, up to its first sentence end), for the same clip the words dropped between them (at most 60, only when the second piece is later in the clip), and each cut's English when shown. Without word timings, the last/first 160 characters of each cut's text.
  - Quick check (at once, needs no assistant):
    - meaning: "The cut drops "<word>" (a negation) from between the two pieces: the joined sentence may say the opposite.";
    - grammar: "The first piece ends on "<word>", which waits for a clause that is not there." (the first piece ends, not at a sentence end, on a condition or contrast word);
    - grammar: "The first piece ends mid-clause: ..." / "The second piece starts mid-clause: ..." (or "A side of the join strands a clause.").
    - Nothing found is not "ok"; only the assistant says that.
  - The assistant's check: the joined original is translated afresh and judged ok, meaning (a negation, condition or contrast lost, a reversed claim, the wrong subject) or grammar (a hanging clause, a missing verb or object, disagreement across the join), with a one-sentence note (at most 300 characters).
  - The assistant is asked only for joins an edit made or moved within the last 15 s, when aid 6 is on for that language, about a second after the edit settles; opening a cut list only shows checks already made. A check belongs to the joined words: moving the join makes the old answer stop applying. A check never blocks an edit. Switching cut list withdraws the previous list's waiting checks. It counts against the aids' daily allowance.
  - On the second card of a join: a button above the card: "Checking the join" (hollow dot), "Join reads well" (green), "Join: meaning changed" (red), "Join: grammar break" (gold), plus "quick check" when the answer is the quick one. Spoken label "Join check with the card before: <label>" ("the join reads well", "meaning changed: <note>", "grammar break: <note>", "checking the joined words"). Clicking it shows/hides a box with the note, the joined original, and "Now reads: <fresh translation>" (or "[assistant] is reading the joined words again."). Pressing it never selects or drags the card. Touch targets are 44 px on touch screens.
  - In the lane: a dot on the seam with the same states and an open box ("Now reads: ..."); a quick answer's dot has a dashed ring.
  - "Checking" shows only after the assistant was asked for this join.
- Exact: yes
