# The whole system as a distributed system (SYS)

## Summary

Per-component, this system is unusually well defended: the lane B breaker, the
fleet halt, `.ccsync-trash`, `root_guard`, `script_server.ready_to_connect`,
the signed upgrade channel, the Resolve undo journal, `CollectorWatchdog` and
the atomic latch files are all real, tested guards written by someone who had
already been burned. The weakness is not in the guards, it is in the **nervous
system between them**. I read the 4,821-line ledger as a taxonomy: **~40 % of
entries were found by the owner noticing, ~58 % by periodic hand audits, ~2 %
by a test, and 0 % by the system raising an alarm on its own.** The fleet
dashboard, whose stated job is to tell everyone whether their footage is
syncing, has never once been the discoverer of an outage, and in SYNC-17,
CR-27a, CR-86, CR-90 and CR-91b it was actively showing green or lying.

The biggest risk I found is **SYS-2**: the companion's sequencer thread has no
top-level exception handler and nothing supervises it, so a single unhandled
exception leaves the machine online, reporting, and frozen on its last
lane state forever - a perfect, permanent "green while dead" with no log line
after the traceback. The best cheap wins are **SYS-4** (the server's own
`received_at` is already in every report reply and the companion throws it
away, so clock skew - which silently disables lane B entirely via `--min-age` -
is undetectable for the sake of three lines) and **SYS-3** (a report section
the companion has computed and sent for weeks is dropped by an undeclared
pydantic field; this is the third recurrence of the same mechanism).

Findings are ranked by (severity x how many ledger classes they close).

## Findings

### SYS-1: No evidence-based liveness contract; "no error" is rendered as green fleet-wide
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/health.py:62-96` (`editor_status`), `health.py:106-114` (`lane_chip_status`), `companion/src/ccsync_companion/sync/sequencer.py:1526-1580`
- **Scenario:** leso's MacBook, 2026-08-28. Lane A reports `state=syncing, transferring=1, last_sync=NULL, last_error=NULL` for 2 h 20 m. Nothing moves; no SFTP session ever exists on the NAS. Because lane A takes its turn first, lane B never runs, so the editor downloads nothing for the whole period.
- **Today:** `lane_chip_status` reddens only on *report* staleness (`received_at` older than 15 min) - and the reports were flowing. A lane in `syncing` is unconditionally AMBER, forever. `editor_status` has the same hole. This is CR-91b, open. The identical shape produced SYNC-17 (lane C green 18 h), CR-27a (a parked machine rendering `idle, queued=0`), CR-86 (a silent identity stop for two days) and CR-83 ("Symptom: nothing").
- **Proposed:** one contract, applied to every non-terminal state in the system: **a state may not be green or amber without a monotonic progress token and the time it last changed.** Concretely: (a) `LaneReportIn` gains `progress_token` (cheap: `bytes_done + files_done + current_project`, which the lane already tallies in `RcloneRunTally`) and `state_since`; (b) `health.lane_chip_status` returns RED with detail `"syncing, no progress for 47 min"` when `state != idle` and the token is unchanged past `max(3 x project_rotation_seconds, 30 min)`; (c) the same rule applied to proxy generation, b-roll/music ingest, ytdl jobs and each collector `poll_runs` kind. Bytes moved, not wall clock, so a genuinely slow 40 GB file over a thin uplink is not mistaken for a hang. **Put the detector on the SERVER**, not only in the companion (CR-91b's own "wanted" text puts it in the companion): the thread that would run a companion-side watchdog is precisely the one the fault wedges, whereas the dashboard is a healthy independent observer that already holds the history.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** critical   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** CR-91b (open), SYNC-17, CR-86, CR-27a; `docs/SYNC_SAFETY.md` §1

### SYS-2: The sequencer thread can die and the machine keeps reporting its frozen state as healthy
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/sync/sequencer.py:737-788` (`_run`), `sequencer.py:452-476` (`start`), `companion/src/ccsync_companion/reporter.py:884-920`
- **Scenario:** an editor's mapped `P:` drops mid-pass (VPN flap, NAS reboot, an ejected SSD). `_reconcile_paths`, `_update_known_selection` or `_run_pass` raises an OSError the inner handlers do not cover.
- **Today:** `_run` has **no try/except around the loop body**, and the `self._state = STATE_STOPPED` assignment sits *after* the `while`, so an exception skips it too. The thread dies with `self._state` frozen at `STATE_BETWEEN_PASSES` (or `STATE_SYNCING`). `start()` is never called again. The reporter thread is untouched and cheerfully reports that frozen state every 30 s forever. The fleet grid shows a healthy machine. Nothing syncs until someone restarts the tray. Contrast the dashboard, which solved exactly this: `Collector.thread_died()`/`seconds_since_heartbeat()`/`restart()` (`dashboard/.../collector.py:153-184`) driven by `CollectorWatchdog` (`dashboard/.../app.py:214-251`, wired at `:474-478`) - the pattern exists in this repo, on the side where the failure was *not* observed.
- **Proposed:** mirror it. `Sequencer` stamps `self._heartbeat = time.monotonic()` at the top of the loop (as `collector._loop` does at `:216`); a small `LaneWatchdog` in `app.py` beside the existing report loop checks `thread_died()` and `seconds_since_heartbeat()`, restarts the sequencer, and - critically - **records the restart in a state file under `~/.ccsync/state/` and reports it** so a machine that needs restarting three times an hour is visible rather than merely self-healing. Wrap `_run`'s body in a log-and-continue try/except so one bad selection payload costs one pass, not the process. Same treatment for `_watcher_thread` and `_media_tree_thread` (`app.py:5497-5536`), which are also unsupervised.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** critical   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** the ledger's class "green while dead"; `crash_report.py` records the traceback locally but changes no control flow, by design

### SYS-3: Report telemetry the companion computes and sends is silently discarded by the dashboard - for the third time
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/app.py:4399-4415` (sends `sync_guard.syncthing_supervisor`), `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:4647-4660` (`SyncGuardIn` does not declare it), `api.py:5060-5126` (`ReportIn`, pydantic default `extra='ignore'`)
- **Scenario:** an editor's Syncthing engine keeps dying. The supervisor restarts it, fails three times, and reports how long it has been down and how many restarts failed - the difference between "he rebooted" and "that machine has needed a human since Tuesday".
- **Today:** the field is dropped at the model boundary with no log line. The companion's own source names the cost: *"THE DASHBOARD DOES NOT READ THIS YET ... `extra="ignore"` drops it (BROLL-ING-1 is what that costs when nobody says so out loud)."* This is the **third** occurrence of the identical mechanism: B17 (`transport_health` dropped for months), then `proxy_coverage`/`youtube_import` ("have ridden every heavy tick since their features shipped and reached nobody"), now this. Each time it was found by a human reading the code, never by a signal.
- **Proposed:** make silence impossible. Set `model_config = ConfigDict(extra='allow')` on `ReportIn` and, in `api_report`, count and log-once-per-day any top-level key that is not a declared field (`log.warning("report from %s carries section %r that this dashboard does not read")`), plus a `meta` row rendered on the admin health page as `[ 1 REPORT SECTION IGNORED: syncthing_supervisor ]`. Add a cross-component test in `server/tests/test_cross_component.py` (which already pins the video-extension lists byte-identical across three components) asserting **every key the companion's `_build_payload` can emit is declared in `ReportIn`** - the parity gate this repo already uses for other duplicated truths, applied to the wire contract.
- **Effort:** S   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** BROLL-ING-1, B17, `docs/SYNC_SAFETY.md` :238-244 (names this gap in prose)

### SYS-4: Nothing anywhere measures clock skew - and a slow clock silently switches lane B off completely
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/sync/rclone_lane.py:1570-1577` (`--min-age`), `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/db.py:3449-3452` and `db.py:3044-3052` (retention/eviction on client-supplied `reported_at`), `reporter.py:764-855` (the reply's `received_at` is never read)
- **Scenario:** a Mac restored from a Time Machine backup, a PC with a dead CMOS battery, a VM resumed from a snapshot. The machine's clock is 20 minutes behind real time.
- **Today, two independent failures.** (1) Lane B passes `--min-age 60s`; rclone computes a remote file's age as *local clock minus the file's modtime*. With a slow clock every file on the NAS looks like it was written in the future, age is negative, **every file is excluded, rclone exits 0 having transferred nothing, and the lane reports idle-and-green.** The editor downloads no proxies at all, indefinitely, with no error anywhere. (2) `prune()` deletes `machine_state` rows on `reported_at < cutoff` and `evict_extra_machines` orders by `reported_at` - both the *client's* clock, while every neighbouring table correctly uses `received_at`/`refreshed_at`. A machine more than `MACHINE_STATE_MAX_AGE_DAYS` (30, `db.py:579`) behind vanishes from the fleet grid on every prune while reporting perfectly; a machine with a future clock pins itself as "most recent" forever and evicts its owner's genuinely live computers. I grepped: there is no skew check, anywhere, in either component.
- **Proposed:** the data is already on the wire and thrown away. `api_report` returns `received_at` in the reply (`api.py:5453`); have `post_once` compare it with the local clock, store `skew_seconds`, warn in the tray past 60 s (*"This computer's clock is 20 minutes behind the server. Sync will not work correctly until it is fixed."*), and send it as a report field so the grid can chip `[ CLOCK 20m SLOW ]`. Independently: switch the two `reported_at` retention/ordering predicates to `received_at`, which is the only clock the dashboard controls. Optionally refuse to start lane B past a hard skew threshold rather than running a pass that provably transfers nothing.
- **Effort:** S   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** CR-89 (a naive timestamp 500'd a page for three days); no existing guard

### SYS-5: Free disk space is invisible to the sync path and absent from the report
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/sync/rclone_lane.py` (no `disk_usage` call anywhere in `sync/`), `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:5060-5126` (`ReportIn` has no disk field)
- **Scenario:** an editor's project drive fills. Or the NAS pool hits 100 %, at which point ZFS stops taking snapshots too.
- **Today:** the ingest paths *do* check (`broll_ingest.py:2626`, `broll_vlm_sidecar.py:446`, `proxy_gen_free_space_floor_gb`), so the pattern exists - it was simply never applied to the three lanes that move the actual footage, nor to anything the dashboard can see. Lane B thrashes per-file ENOSPC, lane C goes out-of-sync, lane A keeps trying. The grid shows red dots with no cause, and the owner's first question ("why?") has no answer on any page.
- **Proposed:** `disk: {local_root_free, local_root_total, system_free}` in the report (it is one `shutil.disk_usage` per heavy tick), flattened into `machine_state` like `transport`/`guard`/`proxy` already are (`api.py:flatten_*`), rendered as a `[ DISK 4% ]` chip past a threshold, and used as the *first* branch of SYS-7's decision tree. Companion-side: below a floor, park lane B through the existing `lane_guard` `paused` mechanism (never `error`) with the tray line *"Not downloading proxies: this drive has 8 GB free"* - a park that a human clears, exactly like the breaker. Server-side: poll pool capacity in the collector beside the existing Syncthing polls and refuse new ticks past a ceiling.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** existing free-space floors in the ingest/proxy paths; `docs/BACKUP_RESTORE.md` (a full pool also silently ends snapshots)

### SYS-6: Every build reaches the entire fleet at once, and crashes never leave the machine - so there is no canary and no rollback signal
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:3893-3936` (`_upgrade_info`), `companion/src/ccsync_companion/crash_report.py:1-36`
- **Scenario:** a companion build has a bug that kills a background thread on macOS only. `-MakeCurrent` publishes it; every Mac in the fleet takes it within one report interval.
- **Today:** `_upgrade_info` offers the current package to every machine of that platform unconditionally; the only staging that exists is `min_version` (a downgrade floor) and the human choosing when to click MakeCurrent. Crash reports are written to `~/.ccsync/crashes/` and, by explicit design, *"send anything by default. Not a counter, not a ping"* - the local half is excellent and the fleet is blind to it. So the fleet-wide signal that would justify a rollback (which the channel already supports as a first-class operation: "different, not newer") does not exist. Class B in the ledger - the stale build - is the single largest live exposure, with ~60 entries marked fixed-but-unshipped.
- **Proposed:** two parts, both small. (1) **A crash counter, not a crash report:** add `crashes: {count_24h, last_thread, last_at, version}` to the report payload - counts and a thread name, no traceback, no privacy change, disclosable in `TELEMETRY.md`. (2) **A canary cohort:** `packages` gains a `rollout` column; MakeCurrent offers the build to machines whose `machine_id` hashes into the first N % (or an explicitly named canary machine - the base rig) and to nobody else until an admin clicks [ PROMOTE TO FLEET ]. The Packages page shows *"canary: 2 machines, 6 h, 0 crashes, 3 successful lane passes each"* against the same numbers for the previous build, and offers [ ROLL BACK ] which is just re-publishing the prior version - machinery that already exists. Auto-rollback can come later; the *decision support* is the win.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `docs/RELEASE.md`; `commands.upgrade` (CR-27a's lesson); ledger class B

### SYS-7: "Why is my footage not syncing" has an answer on the machine and no route to the person asking
- **Lens:** user-error / safeguard
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/app.py:5100-5265` (`build_diagnostics`), `app.py:5266-5320` (`copy_diagnostics`), `settings_window.py:360`
- **Scenario:** an editor's proxies stop arriving. They message the owner. The owner opens the fleet grid, which says amber.
- **Today:** `build_diagnostics` is genuinely good - identity, token expiry, root state, config problems, sequencer state, rclone availability, the Resolve bridge, all fault-isolated per section. It goes **to the clipboard**, and the editor must *"Paste them to your admin in a message."* If any CCSync window is open it silently goes to the log instead. So the one artefact that answers the question exists only if a non-technical editor performs a manual step at the right moment, on the machine that is broken.
- **Proposed:** (a) `POST /api/v1/diagnostics` - the same bundle, uploaded on the same authenticated channel the report uses, on three triggers: the button, any lane transition into `error`, and an admin request. (b) The admin request rides the reply as `commands.diagnostics` - the channel already carries five other commands - so the fleet page gets [ ASK THIS MACHINE WHY ] and an answer within one report interval, without an inbound connection to an editor's PC. (c) **A decision tree, server-side, in one function** that renders one sentence at the top of the machine's row, ordered by what actually goes wrong here: not signed in / EULA not accepted -> clock skew (SYS-4) -> sync drive absent or misplaced (`root_state`) -> disk full (SYS-5) -> fleet or local halt -> lane B breaker tripped -> Syncthing engine down (SYS-3's section) -> no plan for this machine -> no Syncthing share for a ticked project (SYS-9) -> upload-only tick, so lane B is *meant* to be idle -> lane stalled with no progress (SYS-1) -> transport offline. Every one of those states is already computed somewhere; none of them is ever composed into a sentence.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `docs/ZERO_TOUCH_PLAN.md` WP G (a redacted diagnostic bundle - unbuilt)

### SYS-8: There is no outbound notification of any kind; detection latency is "until the owner next looks"
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** no SMTP/webhook/notification code exists in `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/` (verified by grep); `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:917-990` (`/health` is the only machine-readable summary, and nothing polls it externally)
- **Scenario:** a lane B breaker trips on two machines at 18:00 on Friday. The dashboard renders the alarm perfectly. Nobody has the page open until Monday.
- **Today:** every alarm this system raises - tripped breaker, fleet halt, folder errors, stale collector, an editor 12 GB behind - is **pull-only**. The ledger bears this out: 0 of ~120 entries were discovered by the system telling anyone, and every long outage (SYNC-17 18 h, CR-27a 18 h, CR-86 two days) was found by the owner happening to look. Editors never report; CR-83 records every editor's machine broken with *"Symptom: nothing"*.
- **Proposed:** a **weekly fleet health report** plus **event alerts**, both rendered by one function and delivered by a pluggable sink (SMTP via a site setting, or a Tailscale-reachable webhook - no new inbound surface). Compose it from data that already exists: machines silent > 24 h with their last-seen and last-known state; every RED and its one-sentence cause from SYS-7's tree; builds vs fleet coverage (*"0.9.55: 6 of 8 machines; leso's Mac has been on 0.9.2 for 41 days"*); breakers/halts set or cleared this week; bytes moved per lane per machine (the fleet's only throughput history); `.ccsync-trash` and `.stversions` sizes; **and the absence checks from SYS-14** ("no snapshot schedule is configured on this NAS" as a standing red line). Send it Monday 08:00. Send an immediate alert only for: a breaker trip, a machine going silent past 24 h, a collector watchdog restart, and a disk below floor - four events, deliberately, so it stays readable.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `/api/v1/health` (process-level only); ledger section 4

### SYS-9: No continuous invariant checker - every invariant in this system is enforced only at the moment something writes
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/collector.py:933-1191` (`_run_enforce`, the closest thing that exists), `collector.py:73-93` (`folder_tuning_drift`, drift repair for exactly one kind of fact)
- **Scenario:** a tick is written while Syncthing is unreachable; the enforce cycle's later runs compute `desired` from a table that has since changed; a project is renamed on the NAS by hand; an editor is approved under a machine name (CR-91a) and the next enforce cycle unshares their device from every folder (B16).
- **Today:** `folder_tuning_drift` proves the pattern is understood - it re-reads a folder's settings each cycle and repairs the keys that drifted - but it covers Syncthing folder tuning only. Everything else is enforced write-time and never re-verified. Nothing ever asks whether the system's cross-component facts still agree.
- **Proposed:** a **ninth collector kind, `invariants`**, on a slow cadence (15 min), writing one row per (invariant, subject, ok, detail) into a new table, rendered as a single admin page and fed into SYS-8's weekly report. It repairs nothing by default - it *names* things, which is the whole gap. The invariants, all computable from data already held: **(1)** every `selections` row with `sync_mode='full'` has a Syncthing folder shared with that machine's device id; **(2)** every reporting machine has a plan or is deliberately in the unassigned bucket or is `mode='base'` (CR-28); **(3)** every `machines` row's `syncthing_device_id` appears on exactly one row (data-model-5) **and no two hostnames report the same `machine_id` within one interval** - the disk-clone signature, which today makes `adopt_renamed_machine` ping-pong a plan between two live PCs; **(4)** every active project's `.ccsync-project` marker exists and its slug matches the row; **(5)** the NAS tree marker directories exist at `remote_root` (the companion checks this per process via `sequencer._check_remote_root`; the server never does); **(6)** the current package's `min_version` <= its own `version` and >= the previous floor (CR-52's brick, checked continuously rather than only at publish); **(7)** every machine's `companion_version` is >= the minimum needed by the features its plan uses (upload-only needs 0.9.54, `[ RESUME ]` needs 0.9.43, `commands.upgrade` needs 0.9.3) - see SYS-13; **(8)** `.stversions` `maxAge` agrees between NAS-side and editor-side provisioning (365 d vs 30 d, unreconciled since R5); **(9)** a snapshot task exists for every dataset holding customer data (SYS-14); **(10)** every `Proxy/` file on the NAS has an original at the sibling path, and vice versa for ticked projects - the cheapest detector of a half-completed reorganisation.
- **Effort:** L (S per invariant; add them one at a time)   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `_run_enforce`, `folder_tuning_drift`, CR-91a/B16, CR-52, R5

### SYS-10: The routine action with the widest silent blast radius is the only one with no confirmation
- **Lens:** user-error
- **Where:** `dashboard/templates/partials/admin_users.html:71,123,152,267` (excellent confirmations), `dashboard/templates/partials/project_detail.html:92`, versus the selection routes `PUT/DELETE /selection/{editor}/{slug}` and `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/assignments.py` (no confirmation at all)
- **Scenario:** the owner, on the `/admin/assignments` grid - which by design *"writes every editor's selection at once, one cell at a time"* - mis-clicks a cell on the row above. An editor mid-deadline is unticked from their project.
- **Today:** the *destructive-looking* actions are beautifully guarded: deleting a user spells out the consequence in two sentences and names what survives. The *routine* action is one click with no confirmation, no preview and no undo - and it is the one that reaches into an editor's machine: the enforce cycle unshares the Syncthing folder within `interval_enforce`, lane B stops, and the project file stops syncing. Under-sharing is the deliberately safe direction, which is right, but it is still an invisible, uncommunicated change to someone else's working day.
- **Proposed:** (a) an untick preview the dashboard can already compute from `editor_media_project` and Syncthing completion: *"This stops syncing 412 GB of proxies to Priya's Razer and stops the project file updating. Files already on that computer stay there. Priya is 6 % behind on this project right now."* (b) A **deferred apply with an undo window**: record the untick, show [ UNDO ] for 60 s, and have the enforce cycle skip selections younger than that - it runs on a timer anyway, so this costs one `WHERE changed_at < ?`. (c) The companion should *tell the editor*: an untick that removes a project they have open in Resolve deserves a tray line, not silence.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** the `hx-confirm` copy on the user/machine deletes is the model to copy; `docs/MULTI_MACHINE_PLAN.md`

### SYS-11: No audit trail: "who unticked this, and when" is unanswerable
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/db.py:2382,2398` (`DELETE FROM selections`, no history), grep for `audit` in `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/` returns nothing
- **Scenario:** an editor says a project stopped syncing on Tuesday. There are two admins. The dashboard cannot say what changed on Tuesday.
- **Today:** selections are deleted in place. The fleet halt, the lane B resume, pushed updates and file moves each keep *some* state, in four different shapes, and none of them is queryable as a timeline. The container log holds a few `log.warning` lines (a removal override, a file move) and rotates.
- **Proposed:** one append-only `fleet_audit(at, actor, action, subject, detail_json)` table, written from the handful of state-changing routes that already exist: tick/untick, halt set/clear, lane B resume, user/machine delete, package publish/make-current, pushed update, file move, site settings change, device approval. Render it as a fleet timeline and put the last seven days into SYS-8's weekly report. It is a dozen one-line inserts and it converts every future incident from inference into a lookup - which is exactly what CR-91a, CR-49 and CR-42 each cost a day of.
- **Effort:** S   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high

### SYS-12: Six command channels with six delivery semantics and no acknowledgement contract
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:5480-5600` (the whole reply builder)
- **Scenario:** an admin clears a machine's breaker. The reply carrying `resume_lane_b` is lost to a dropped connection.
- **Today:** the reply carries `halt` (always present, both states, because absence means "too old to have an opinion"), `upgrade` (standing until the machine reports a version at or past it), `resume_lane_b` (**one-shot: cleared the moment the reply goes out**, so a lost reply costs a click), `broll_ingest`/`music_ingest` cancels (best-effort, present only when non-empty), and `file_moves` (**re-sent until acked via `file_moves_applied`**). Every one of those choices is individually well-reasoned and documented in place - and the result is that a reader must know six rules, and each new command re-litigates them. `file_moves` is the only one with an acknowledgement, and it is clearly the right shape.
- **Proposed:** generalise `file_moves`' shape into one envelope rather than adding a seventh rule: `commands: [{id, kind, payload, delivery: "once"|"until_acked"|"state"}]`, with the companion returning `commands_applied: [{id, ok, detail}]` and the dashboard storing an outbox row per (machine, command). This makes every command idempotent by id - which is what stops a redelivered resume from clearing a *second*, later trip (the exact hazard `resume_lane_b` handles today with a `requested_at` stamp the companion must remember to compare) - gives the admin UI "delivered / applied / failed" per click instead of hope, and gives SYS-11 its rows for free. Migrate one command at a time; the old keys stay for older companions.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** CR-46 (a redelivered resume trashing 100 proxies per cycle), `docs/FILE_MOVES.md`

### SYS-13: Nothing enforces the deploy ordering that a dozen ledger entries were caused by
- **Lens:** pitfall / safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:3893-3936` (`_upgrade_info` knows nothing about the dashboard's own version), `PUT /api/v1/admin/packages/...`
- **Scenario:** the upload-only tick ships. A companion below 0.9.54 reads `sync_mode` it does not know; a dashboard below 0.7.14 serves a per-machine table to a person-level enforce cycle - which is the B16 unshare-the-fleet shape.
- **Today:** "deploy the dashboard before the companions" is a rule written in `CLAUDE.md`, in three docs and in the ledger, enforced by a human remembering it. It has been violated enough times to be its own failure class (CR-22, CR-27a, CR-49, CR-55, CR-83, CR-85, CR-87). The `min_version` field is a downgrade floor and says nothing about *peers*.
- **Proposed:** a machine-readable peer requirement in the package record - `requires_dashboard: "0.7.14"` on a companion build, `requires_companion: "0.9.54"` as a *warning* threshold on a dashboard build - set by `tools/release.ps1` from a constant in `config.py`, signed with the rest of the record. Then: the publish route **refuses MakeCurrent** on a companion whose `requires_dashboard` exceeds the running dashboard's `VERSION` (a 409 naming both numbers, `--allow-ordering-skew` to override, exactly as the other publish refusals are shaped), and the fleet grid chips every machine whose version is below the *live* dashboard's `requires_companion` as `[ NEEDS 0.9.54 ]`. This also feeds SYS-9's invariant 7 and answers the standing `SYNC_SAFETY.md` complaint that a safety guarantee's reach is *"a property of the companion build, not of the system"*.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** med
- **Related:** ledger class C; `docs/RELEASE.md` publish refusals (the pattern to copy); CR-52

### SYS-14: Nothing in the product says a safety mechanism is ABSENT
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/setup_engine.py` (the snapshots task reads state and *"neither task WRITES anything"*), `docs/SYNC_SAFETY.md` :287-290, `docs/BACKUP_RESTORE.md` :34-52
- **Scenario:** the live TrueNAS holds `dashboard.db`, `broll.db` and `music.db` under `/mnt/tank/apps`, which is a plain directory, not a dataset. It therefore has **no scheduled snapshot at all** - and `setup_snapshots.py --apply` has never been run on either NAS (CR-10, open).
- **Today:** every page renders green. `SYNC_SAFETY.md` states the gap in prose - *"there is no banner for 'this NAS has no snapshot schedule'. Until there is, it is a runbook item, not a system property"* - and `COMMERCIAL_READINESS.md` lists "whether ZFS snapshots exist out-of-band" under "not verified from the repo". The dashboard is one API call away from knowing.
- **Proposed:** invert the default: **a safety mechanism the dashboard cannot positively verify is reported as MISSING, not as silence.** A `protection` block on the admin health page and in SYS-8's weekly report, each line green only on positive evidence: snapshot task exists for the tree dataset / for the apps dataset (TrueNAS `pool.snapshottask` list, which `setup_engine.py` already has the client for; on DSM, where no scheduling API exists, the line reads "cannot verify - confirm in DSM" and stays amber forever, which is honest); the last snapshot is younger than 25 h; `DASH_RELEASE_PUBKEYS` is set; the release key has been backed up (an admin-set acknowledgement date); a restore drill has been run this year; `.ccsync-trash` and `.stversions` are within retention. The tri-state is the whole point - "could not ask" must never render as "fine", which is the exact mistake `folder_errors` and the healthcheck have each made before.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** CR-10 (open), WPK-6 (a pre-recreate snapshot failing silently on every deploy for weeks), `ZERO_TOUCH_PLAN.md` WP D/G

### SYS-15: The owner cannot recover from anything without a root shell
- **Lens:** user-error
- **Where:** `docs/BACKUP_RESTORE.md` §4a-4e; `docs/RESOLVE_EDIT_SAFETY.md` :77-90; `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/dashboard_update.py:408` (the one restore path that *is* automated)
- **Scenario:** the owner deletes a project folder on the NAS by hand on a Sunday.
- **Today:** every one of the five documented restore paths is a root SSH session requiring judgement a non-technical owner cannot supply: is `apps` a dataset or a directory (this decides which of two `cp` lines is correct, and whether a snapshot exists at all); which snapshot; is everything since it expendable; has the fleet stopped writing; and - platform-dependent and destructive if wrong - `chown` is required on TrueNAS and **deletes the share's ACL on DSM**. `zfs rollback -r` destroys later snapshots, guarded by a parenthetical. The single self-service path in the whole document is browsing `.stversions/` over SMB, which never covers video originals (they have no versioning at all, NAS-side). The Resolve `.drp` restore is a four-step GUI procedure ending in *"Compare, then keep whichever is right"*, and the undo is a tray click on the editor's machine with no admin route - unlike the breaker, which got `[ RESUME ]` in CR-45.
- **Proposed:** ZERO_TOUCH WP G, narrowed to what actually gets used, plus a rehearsal. (a) **Snapshot browse-and-restore in the dashboard** for the one case that recurs: pick a project, pick a snapshot, see a diff of what would be restored, restore into `<project>/.restored-<ts>/` **rather than over the live path** - quarantine-instead-of-overwrite, so the destructive judgement disappears. (b) An **admin-side Resolve undo**: `commands.resolve_undo` naming a journal id, replaying the same journal the tray already replays. (c) A **guided disaster runbook page** that is a wizard, not prose: it names what is protected *right now* (SYS-14's evidence), asks what went wrong, and either performs the recovery or prints the exact commands with the customer's real pool name, dataset-vs-directory answer and platform substituted in - so the shell path is generated from live facts rather than transcribed. (d) A **restore drill** the dashboard can run itself against a scratch path monthly, recorded as a date on the protection panel: *"a backup nobody has restored from is a hypothesis"*, and today nothing schedules or alarms on that.
- **Effort:** L   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `ZERO_TOUCH_PLAN.md` WP G (unbuilt), CR-10, CR-12

### SYS-16: A companion whose editor is not signed in reports nothing at all, so the machine simply vanishes
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/reporter.py:775-789` (`post_once` returns early when `get_editor_name()` is None), `companion/src/ccsync_companion/app.py:6420-6460` (`_identity_watch_loop`)
- **Scenario:** an editor's identity token lapses (pre-CR-86 tokens still do), or they sign out, or the EULA gate parks them, or a clock jump makes the token look expired.
- **Today:** the reporter *skips the cycle entirely* - by design, so nothing reports under an unverified identity, which is correct. The consequence is that the machine drops off the fleet grid with no trace, its `machine_state` row ages out after 30 days, and the state is indistinguishable from "that computer is switched off". CR-86 is exactly this: an editor lost two days of syncing, announced by *one transient tray balloon on a machine with nobody watching it*. CR-22/CR-27a is the same shape via the licence dialog.
- **Proposed:** a machine with no verified identity should not go silent - it should report **that**. Send a minimal unauthenticated beacon on the shared token to a `POST /api/v1/presence` route: `{machine, machine_id, platform, version, reason: "not_signed_in"|"eula_pending"|"token_expired"}` and nothing else - no editor data, so it authorises nothing it should not. The grid then shows `[ SIGNED OUT since Tue 14:02 ]` instead of an absence, and SYS-8 alerts on it. Where the machine is known (`machines.machine_id` survives a rename and is minted once), the row can even be attributed without any identity claim.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** CR-86 (fixed by removing the TTL, which does not help tokens minted earlier), CR-22, the `eula_accepted.json` park

### SYS-17: `proc.wait()` on rclone is unbounded, and lane B is joined without a timeout
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/sync/rclone_lane.py:3310` (`returncode = proc.wait()`), `rclone_lane.py:1303-1313` (`--max-duration ... SOFT`), `sequencer.py:1578` (`thread.join()`)
- **Scenario:** a Mac's external SSD stops answering (the MAC-12 condition, logged verbatim on the machine in CR-91b). rclone blocks in a kernel read on the *local* side.
- **Today:** the remote-side stalls are well covered - `--timeout 5m`, `--contimeout 60s`, `--retries-sleep 10s`, and a `--max-duration` derived from `project_rotation_seconds`. But `--timeout` governs *remote* IO, `--cutoff-mode SOFT` deliberately *"lets the current one land"* (right, because SFTP uploads do not resume), and none of it bounds a local read wedged in the kernel. `proc.wait()` then never returns, holding `_run_lock`; `_run_lanes_a_and_b` joins the lane B thread with no timeout for the stated reason that an un-joined lane B would write into a directory the next project's repath moves. Both are individually correct and jointly unbounded. A hand-edited `project_rotation_seconds = 0` removes `--max-duration` altogether.
- **Proposed:** a hard ceiling above the soft one: `proc.wait(timeout=max_duration * 2 + 300)`, then `proc.kill()`, `state=error`, `detail="rclone did not exit after Ns - killed"`. That single line converts SYS-1's silent stall into a reported error on the machine that has the evidence, and it is the companion-side half of SYS-1's server-side detector. Refuse `project_rotation_seconds <= 0` in config validation (`config.py` already collects `config_problems`) rather than silently dropping the budget. And bound the lane B join, killing the lane's process before proceeding rather than blocking the rotation forever.
- **Effort:** S   **Severity:** high   **Confidence:** med (the local-read hang is inferred from MAC-12's logged probe, per CR-91b's own "most likely cause, not confirmed")
- **Related:** CR-91b (open), MAC-12, AUDIT_2 L-12 (which added `--timeout` for the remote half of this)

### SYS-18: No fault injection anywhere in 13 test suites
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** no `chaos`/fault-injection harness exists in any suite (verified by grep); `companion/tests/conftest.py` (three excellent autouse isolation fixtures - the seam to hang this on)
- **Scenario:** every failure class above is a fault that a test could have induced in seconds and never did. The ledger agrees: 2 of ~120 entries were found by a test.
- **Today:** the suites are strong on *logic* and near-silent on *conditions*. The lane adapters, the reporter's `_http_post`, the selection client's opener, `popen_factory` and `subprocess.run` are all already injectable seams - the harness is 90 % built and unused for this purpose.
- **Proposed:** one `tests/chaos/` module per component, parameterised over a fault list, asserting the *observable* each fault should produce rather than an internal call. Cheap, high-yield injections, each tied to a real ledger entry: a `popen_factory` that never exits (SYS-17/CR-91b - assert the lane reports `error` within the budget); a clock 20 minutes slow (SYS-4 - assert a skew warning and that lane B is not silently empty); `disk_usage` returning 1 GB free (SYS-5 - assert lane B parks, not thrashes); a report POST that always 401s, then recovers (assert the cached selection is used *and* said so); `_run` raising on the third pass (SYS-2 - assert a restart is recorded and reported); killing the process between an atomic latch's tmp-write and replace (class G, paid for twice); a report carrying an undeclared section (SYS-3 - assert it is counted); a second hostname reporting an existing `machine_id` (the disk-clone case in SYS-9); a Syncthing that 200s but returns an empty folder list (the shape that trips the breaker benignly, CR-44/CR-47). Nine tests, each one closing a class rather than a bug.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** ledger section 4 ("an automated test or monitor: ~2 %")

### SYS-19: Blast radius is undocumented and two of the four single points of failure degrade badly
- **Lens:** safeguard
- **Where:** `companion/src/ccsync_companion/selection.py:72-108,239-300` (the on-disk selection cache), `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/app.py:474-478`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §2
- **Scenario:** each of: the dashboard container down; the NAS down; Syncthing down; the base rig off; one companion misbehaving.
- **Today, verified:** **Dashboard down** - degrades *well*: the selection client falls back to a tolerant on-disk cache written atomically, lanes keep running on the last known plan. But the fallback is silent and unbounded in age; a dashboard restored from an old backup silently reverts everyone's plan and the enforce cycle then unshares to match (the B16 direction). **NAS down** - total; every lane fails, and `_note_transport` correctly infers "offline" only when both rclone lanes fail on the same project. **Syncthing down on the NAS** - lane C stops fleet-wide; the per-machine supervisor covers only the *editor's* engine, and its report section is the one the dashboard drops (SYS-3). **Base rig off** - no releases, no indexing, no NAS install tooling; sync unaffected; correctly decoupled. **One companion misbehaving** - largely contained by `evict_extra_machines`, the report ceilings and `machine` being a bounded string; the uncontained case is a machine reporting a wrong-but-plausible identity or a duplicated `machine_id`.
- **Proposed:** (a) put the table above in `ARCHITECTURE.md` as a blast-radius section - it is the first thing an operator needs and it is currently only inferable from code. (b) Bound the silent cache fallback: after N consecutive failures the tray says *"Using the last plan I was given, from Tuesday - the dashboard has not answered for 3 hours"*, and the grid says so when the machine reappears. (c) Give the *dashboard* a Syncthing-down alarm on the same terms as the editor's supervisor, and read the supervisor section (SYS-3). (d) The dashboard's own `dashboard.db` restore should refuse to serve selections until an admin confirms the plan is current if its newest `selections.changed_at` is older than the newest `machine_state.received_at` - the "restored from an old backup" signature, which is cheap to detect and catastrophic to miss.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** high
- **Related:** `docs/GOTCHAS.md` :298-308 ("falls back silently to its cached selection"), B16

### SYS-20: `.ccsync-project` markers and per-machine state have no generation number, so a restore is indistinguishable from an edit
- **Lens:** pitfall
- **Where:** `dashboard/src/ccsync_dashboard/api.py:5406-5470` (absent field leaves the table untouched), `companion/src/ccsync_companion/reporter.py:603-650` (`_drop_unchanged_sections`, `SECTION_RESEND_SECONDS = 600`)
- **Scenario:** `dashboard.db` is restored from a snapshot. The companions do not know.
- **Today:** the section-suppression is correctly bounded - a forced resend every 600 s means presence data self-heals within ten minutes, and `_note_sections_sent` is careful to record only what the server actually accepted. That half is right. What is missing is the converse signal: the dashboard has no way to say *"I have lost state, tell me everything now"*, and no way for a companion to notice that the plan it just received is older than the one it was following.
- **Proposed:** a `server_epoch` (a uuid minted at first boot and re-minted whenever the DB is restored or migrated) plus a monotonic `selection_generation` per (editor, machine), both returned in the report reply and in `GET /selection`. The companion clears `_section_state` on an epoch change (an immediate full resend rather than a ten-minute wait) and **refuses to apply a selection whose generation is lower than the one it is running**, logging and reporting the refusal instead. That converts a silent, fleet-wide plan rollback - the most expensive plausible consequence of the one recovery operation the owner is most likely to perform - into a visible, self-announcing refusal.
- **Effort:** M   **Severity:** med   **Confidence:** med
- **Related:** `docs/BACKUP_RESTORE.md` :262-264 ("editor-project ticks are not re-discoverable"), SYS-19(d)
