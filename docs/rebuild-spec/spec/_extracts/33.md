# Extract of inventory section 33

## Items

### 33-001: The shelf panel shows cuts set aside from the cut, in two tabs: Staged and Sequences.
- Where: cards-editor / Notes and the staged shelf
- What: The shelf sits in the right-hand rail of the desk layout and in the shelf tab of the phone layout. It is drawn only while a cut list is open; with none open it is empty. Its bar holds two tabs, each with a count: **Staged** (the number of staged cuts across every shelf category) and **Sequences** (the number of saved sequences). The tab chosen is remembered per browser (default Staged) and is never written into the cut list, so two devices may look at different tabs of one shelf. A shelf that is hidden (for example the phone shelf tab while on the desk) is brought up to date when it shows.
- Exact: yes

### 33-002: On the desk, the shelf can take the rail's whole height or share it with the section list.
- Where: cards-editor / Notes and the staged shelf
- What: On the desk rail only, an icon button gives the shelf the whole rail ("Give the shelf the whole rail") or gives the section list its half back ("Show the sections"); it shows a pressed state while the shelf is tall. The choice is remembered per browser. With no choice saved, the shelf takes the whole rail by itself whenever the rail is shorter than 440 pixels. While the shelf is tall, the section list is folded to its title bar, and clicking that bar returns to the split. In the normal split the section list has 2 parts and the shelf 3 parts of the rail's height. In the rail a staged card's meta line is kept to one line.
- Exact: yes

### 33-003: Category chips filter the Staged tab.

### 33-007: A staged card can be listened to off the timeline, from its start or from any word.
- Where: cards-editor / Notes and the staged shelf
- What:
  - Clicking anywhere on a staged card other than its words and buttons plays the card from its in point to its out point, off the timeline. Clicking the card that is already playing (not on its words) stops it.
  - Clicking a **word** of a staged card plays from that word to the card's out point and marks that word in the card's line. If the clip's word timings have not arrived within 1.5 seconds, or the card's line and the timed words differ in length by more than 3 characters or 20 percent (whichever is larger), it plays from the card's start instead. A word that starts before the in point starts at the in point. A click between two words picks the nearer word. Works in every language, including ones written without spaces. Pressing on a card already asks for its word timings so the click has them.
  - Nothing plays while a card is lifted (phone) or while select mode is on.
  - Only one thing plays at a time: listening to a staged card pauses the timeline's playback, and starting timeline playback stops the staged card.
  - Escape stops it and clears the marked word. Opening another cut list stops it.
  - Warnings: "There is nothing to play there." (a zero-length cut), "Playback is not available here." (the browser cannot play audio), "Could not play it: <reason>".
- Exact: yes

### 33-079: Dragging the head or a trim edge while paused plays short snatches of sound.
- Where: cards-editor / Playback and audio
- What: While the head (or an F8 hold) or a trim edge is dragged and playback is paused, short 70 ms snatches of the clip under it are played at normal pitch, at most one every 22 ms, at that cut's level and fade; holding still is silent and a gap is silent. A dragged trim edge is heard at the clip's level without fades.

### 33-080: The first Play is never delayed by a sleeping sound device.
- Where: cards-editor / Playback and audio
- What: While the editor is visible, it keeps the computer's sound output awake with an inaudible signal, and wakes the sound device on the first click or key anywhere, so the first Play is not delayed. This stops while the page is hidden.

### 33-081: Automatic levelling brings every interviewee to the same speech level.
- Where: cards-editor / Playback and audio
- What: On by default (Same loudness). Each clip's speech level is measured from its waveform, ignoring parts more than 10 dB quieter than the average, so a door slam does not set it. The target is the middle (median) of the speech levels of the clips used in the cut; shelf clips and clips of other cut lists never count. Loud clips come down, quiet ones come up. Each cut is levelled by the speech within 2 minutes either side of its middle (in 5-second steps), never more than 18 dB from the clip's whole level; a clip with under 20 seconds of waveform uses its whole level. The automatic gain stays between 0.05x and 8x. Other players in the editor (the transcript) use the same level for any clip and moment so they sound like the lane; a clip not in the cut never moves the target.

### 33-082: Exports turn the cut into a timeline another program imports, frame-exact and the same every time.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports
- What: Only the cut is exported; the shelf and saved sequences are not.
  - Frame rate: the rate asked for, else the cut list's own. A rate within 0.01 of 23.976, 29.97, 47.952 or 59.94 is treated as the exact NTSC fraction (24000/1001 and so on); any other rate is read as the nearest fraction with a denominator of at most 1001.
  - Seconds become frames once, exactly, rounding to the nearest frame with ties to even (an option rounds ties up, matching the hand-over to Resolve frame for frame), so all three formats agree on every frame.
  - Drop-frame timecode only at 29.97 and 59.94; a cut list marked drop-frame at another rate is written non-drop, with a note.
  - Timeline start: the cut list's start timecode; unreadable means 00:00:00:00, with a note.
  - Source side: each cut's in and out in frames at the clip's own rate (unreadable rate: the timeline's, with a note), counted from the clip's start timecode (unreadable: from 00:00:00:00, with a note). Source drop-frame is used when the rate allows and the timeline is drop-frame or the clip's start timecode uses ";".
  - A cut shorter than one frame is exported as one frame, with a note.
  - Record side: cuts laid end to end after the black in front of them, so nothing drifts. A clip at another rate than the timeline has its record duration rescaled (it may differ by a frame), with a note per clip.
  - Gaps: every gap is exported as black at its own length, including gaps after the last cut; consecutive gaps are rounded as one run, so totals match the hand-over.
  - Fades in timeline frames, never longer than the cut.
  - Markers: section headings (Blue by default), note headings (Green by default; a heading's own colour wins) and note rows (Cream) become markers on whatever follows them.
  - Edges that fall between frames (more than 0.001 frame off) are reported as "falls between frames: rounded to the nearest frame (<rule>)".
  - Every export with a cut ends with the note "automatic levelling is the editor's playback only: exported audio plays at source level plus each cut's manual gain where the format carries it".
  - Each export lists "not carried" sentences naming what the format lost; nothing is lost silently. Cuts are named "cut N (<person or clip>)".
  - File names come from the timeline name (default the cut list's title, else its project name, else "Timeline Cards"), with characters Windows forbids replaced by "_" and trailing dots and spaces removed ("timeline" if empty). The same cut list always gives the same bytes.
  - Media paths, when known, are written as file addresses (Windows drive paths, network share paths and Mac paths converted).
- Exact: yes

### 33-083: The OpenTimelineIO export.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports
- What: Label "OpenTimelineIO (.otio)". OTIO 0.15 schema, one audio track "A1", global start = the timeline start. Each cut is a clip with its source range at the clip's rate starting at its source timecode; its media as an external file reference or, with no path, a missing reference ("no media path for <clips>: written as MissingReference (relink in the target tool)"). Each gap is a Gap. Markers sit at the start of the black before the cut they precede; Resolve marker colours are mapped to OTIO's set (Fuchsia to MAGENTA, Rose to PINK, Lavender to PURPLE, Sky to CYAN, Mint to GREEN, Lemon to YELLOW, Sand and Cocoa to ORANGE, Cream to WHITE, unknown to BLUE), with the exact colour kept in metadata. Gain, fades, clip colour, note and the cut's reference are kept only in each clip's `metadata.timeline_cards`, and reported as not carried ("gain … and fades … not carried by OTIO (kept in metadata.timeline_cards only)", "… OTIO 0.15 has no clip colour …"). Mixed rates: "N cut(s) from clips at another rate than the timeline: OTIO lays each at its source duration, so on this mixed-rate timeline later cuts may sit up to a frame off the timeline grid".
- Exact: yes

### 33-084: The Final Cut Pro XML export.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports
- What: Label "Final Cut Pro XML 1.10 (.fcpxml)". FCPXML 1.10, one library, event and project named after the timeline; a 1920x1080 format named for standard rates (23.98, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50, 59.94, 60) or a custom frame duration; stereo, 48 kHz; audio only. One asset per clip (duration the longer of the furthest out point used and the media's own length; channels and sample rate from the media when known, else 2 and 48000); with no media path, an offline placeholder and the note "no media path for <clips>: written as offline media (relink in the target tool)". Each gap is a `<gap>`; each cut an asset-clip named "<person>: <clip>" with the dialogue role. Carries per-cut gain (`adjust-volume`, e.g. "+3.0dB"), fades (ease in / ease out, in frames), the cut's note, and markers (on the first gap in front of the clip, else on the clip). Times are exact fractions on frame boundaries. Not carried: clip colour ("N cut(s) carry a clip colour: FCPXML has no clip colour"); markers after the last cut when there is no gap to hang them on. Text is escaped; characters the format cannot hold are removed.
- Exact: yes

### 33-085: The CMX3600 EDL export.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Exports
- What: Label "CMX3600 EDL (.edl)". UTF-8, CRLF line ends. "TITLE:" (first 70 characters, never splitting an emoji) and "FCM: DROP FRAME / NON-DROP FRAME". - Exact: yes
