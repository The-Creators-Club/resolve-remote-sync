# Extract of inventory section 43

## Items

### 43-001: Planning gives the owner every interview paragraph of an episode, sorted into themes, as the raw material of the film.
- Where: cards-board / What the board is
- What: Planning exists so that, before cutting, every paragraph of every interview of an episode sits in front of the owner sorted by theme (the "pool"), and from it he builds the film's order (the "cut") by hand. Each theme is a section of the film. Every paragraph is on the board exactly once; nothing is lost and nothing is doubled. A paragraph no theme claims is still on the board, in a final theme called "Uncategorised". The owner's hand order is the authority: the assistant reads it, judges it and proposes, but only he moves cards unless he explicitly asks for a full re-order, which produces a new version and leaves his own untouched.

### 43-002: The board holds three kinds of card: a paragraph, a note card, and a paragraph borrowed from another episode.
- Where: cards-board / Cards on the board
- What: A paragraph card holds one transcript paragraph: its original wording (for a Mandarin interview, the verbatim Chinese) and its English, and it always knows which interview and which paragraph it came from. A note card holds the owner's own direction with no paragraph behind it (for example "b-roll: harbour at dawn"); it is part of the order but is not a quote. A paragraph from another episode's interview (for example from an earlier season) can also be placed; it is played from its own interview and its speaker keeps the colour they have elsewhere. An interviewer's question is a paragraph card too, shown as a heading so it stands apart from answers.

### 43-003: The speaker of a card is the interviewee, not the transcript it came from.
- Where: cards-board / Cards on the board
- What: A card's speaker is the person interviewed, so two transcripts of the same person (two shoot days, a follow-up interview, an online call) are one speaker everywhere: one colour, one count in speaker balance, one self-introduction allowance.

### 43-004: Every interviewee has one colour, used for their cards, their multicam clips and their cuts.
- Where: cards-board / Cards on the board
- What: Each interviewee gets one colour, the same on the board, on their multicam clips in the editing program and on their cuts in a timeline. The palette is 16 colours in a fixed order: #1375a0, #ec4b93, #448f64, #00b5b8, #dbaf80, #966145, #f09ec1, #000035, #c8a9fe, #16552f, #b8ad4c, #4577a1, #7a3b8f, #2f6f5e, #a0522d, #5470c6. A person already given a colour keeps it for good, across episodes too. The owner can change a person's colour, and that person is recoloured everywhere from then on.

### 43-005: Decided for him: which colour a new interviewee gets.
- Where: cards-board / Cards on the board
- What: New interviewees are taken in alphabetical order (ignoring letter case) and each takes the first palette colour nobody in the episode has yet. With more than 16 people, colours repeat. A paragraph card is the speaker's colour throughout (text, background and border). Needs a manual override: the owner choosing a person's colour.

### 43-006: Decided for him: the first sort of paragraphs into themes.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The assistant proposes the themes and places every paragraph in one of them. Each paragraph lands in exactly one theme: if a paragraph is proposed for two, the first placement stands and the second is dropped, and the owner is told how many were dropped. Paragraphs no theme claims go into "Uncategorised". A theme that ends up with no paragraphs is still shown, empty. After the sort the owner is told how many themes there are, how many paragraphs were placed, how many went to "Uncategorised", and whether every paragraph was placed exactly once (a mismatch is called out). Needs a manual override: moving any card, renaming, adding or removing a theme.

### 43-007: Decided for him: the order of the themes on a new board.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Each theme has a role: personal stories, body or ending. A theme with no stated role is "personal" if it is called "Personal Stories", "ending" if it is called "Ending" (any letter case), and body otherwise. Themes are laid out personal stories first, then the body themes, then the ending, keeping the order proposed within each group (never sorted by size). "Uncategorised" is always last. The same rule can be applied to a board in use as a quick "arc": Personal Stories to the front, Ending to the back, everything else where it was, Uncategorised last. Needs a manual override: dragging themes into any order.
- Flag: differs: the quick arc on a board in use only recognised the two names with exact capitals, while a new board recognised them in any letter case.

### 43-008: Decided for him: the order of cards inside a theme on a new board.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Inside a theme, cards are grouped speaker by speaker, speakers in the order they first appear in the episode's interviews, and within one speaker in the order the sort listed them. Needs a manual override: dragging cards within a theme.

### 43-009: The order of the board is read the same way by everything that reads it, and every card has a number.
- Where: cards-board / What the board is
- What: Themes are read in order, and inside each theme its cards in order. Distance between cards means nothing; only order counts. Every card, note cards included, gets a number 1, 2, 3 ... in that order, and the assistant's reports, the owner's notes and any re-order plan refer to cards by these numbers together with each paragraph's own reference. Numbers shift as soon as a card is added or moved, so the assistant re-reads the board every time and never assumes a number from an earlier reading is still right. A card left outside every theme is read after all themes as "(ungrouped)" and the owner is warned how many there are; a theme with no name is called "(unnamed group)".

### 43-010: No change to the board, by hand or by the assistant, can lose a card.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: Every change to a board in use is checked before it is kept: every card must end up in exactly one theme, the count of cards before and after must match, and no card may be duplicated. A change that would break any of these is refused and the board is left as it was. A change that would leave cards stranded outside every theme is refused with the advice to tidy those first, so they are never silently left behind. The previous state of the board was always kept before a change.

### 43-011: A later interview can be added to a board already being arranged, without disturbing his arrangement.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: When a follow-up interview arrives, its paragraphs can be added either all into one chosen theme or spread over several themes paragraph by paragraph; paragraphs not chosen stay off the board entirely (they do not go to "Uncategorised"). Some paragraphs can be left out, or only some taken. A paragraph already on the board is skipped and listed, so adding again after the transcript grows brings in only the new paragraphs. Naming a theme that does not exist is refused with the list of themes that do. A preview shows what would be added to each theme before anything changes. The rest of the board keeps its arrangement.

### 43-012: Decided for him: where an added interview's cards go and what colour they take.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: New cards go at the end of their theme's order. Their colour is the colour that speaker already has on this board (so another transcript of the same person matches); a speaker not yet on the board takes the first palette colour no card on the board uses. The owner can give the new cards another colour when adding them, and is told whether the colour was reused or new.
- Flag: differs: an added interview took its colour from the cards already on the board, not from the episode's remembered colour for that person, so a person could end up a different colour on the board than on their clips and cuts.

### 43-013: A new theme can be carved out of chosen cards on a board in use.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: Chosen paragraphs are lifted out of whatever themes they sit in into a brand-new theme, placed at a chosen position, after a named theme, or at the end. The new theme's cards are in the order they were chosen. The themes they left close up in their remaining order. Refused when a theme of that name already exists, when the same paragraph is chosen twice, when chosen paragraphs are not on the board (the first few are named), or when the named neighbour does not exist. A preview shows how many cards came from each old theme and where the new theme will sit. Cards keep their text and colour.

### 43-014: Decided for him: a theme emptied by a change disappears.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: When carving out a new theme leaves an old theme with no cards, the old theme is removed and the owner is told "'<name>' is now empty - removing the group". Needs a manual override: keeping an empty theme as a placeholder.

### 43-015: Whole themes can be moved along the board, their cards riding with them.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: Themes can be put in a new order without any card changing theme or moving inside its theme. When the new order is given as a list it must name every theme exactly once (a theme left out is refused, "so nothing is re-ordered by accident"; a name that is not a theme, or a name given twice, is refused), and two themes with the same name make an order impossible to state. "Uncategorised" stays last whatever the order says. Asking for the order the board already has does nothing and says so. The result shows how far each theme moved, earlier or later.

### 43-016: A runtime is estimated for every card, section and cut, and real durations replace it once the cut has been timed.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Until a cut has been timed against the footage, each paragraph's length is estimated from its text: Chinese at 3.6 characters per second when it has any Chinese, otherwise English at 18.5 characters per second, to a tenth of a second. Note cards count as no time. Once the cut has been timed, the real duration of each card is used. Every estimate is marked as estimated, and every runtime report says whether its durations are real or estimated and what share were estimated. Runtimes are given per card, per section and for the whole cut.

### 43-017: Interviewer questions are banned from the cut; the owner is warned about any left in it.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: House style: the film is one conversation between the interviewees, with no voiceover and no interviewer. An interviewer's question in the cut is a defect: every one is flagged, and the owner is told how many there are and where, so he can move each to a staged group. Questions already sitting in a staged section are still marked but not counted in the warning. A question the owner's own notes name for a place in the film is exempt, and is reported as exempt rather than as a defect. In the list of unused material, questions are marked as context only and must never be proposed for the cut.
- Flag: differs: the story check's own advice on bridging a weak join ranked an interviewer question from the unused material as an acceptable bridge, while its rules said questions are banned and must never be proposed.

### 43-018: A paragraph plays once; a second play is flagged.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: The same paragraph appearing twice in the cut is flagged at its second appearance ("this already played") and the owner is warned with every duplicate and where it falls. When two of his notes want the same card, the assistant asks him rather than choosing.

### 43-019: The assistant can read the cut and say what story it currently tells, without touching it.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: When the owner asks whether the edit works, what the story is, how sections connect, or for bridges between sections, the assistant reads his ordered cut against the whole pool of paragraphs. It reads and reports only; it never changes his cut or the pool, and every suggestion is written so he can drag the named cards himself. It reads the whole cut end to end in order and then every unused paragraph end to end, with no skimming or sampling, using the original Mandarin where a card's meaning turns on it, and uses the strongest assistant available. If he names only one of the two (cut or pool), it asks which it is and looks for the other beside it. The check is re-run fresh every time.

### 43-020: The story check's judgement comes in five parts, every claim pinned to a card.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Every claim cites the card's number and paragraph reference.
  - A. The story as it plays now, beat by beat: where the film states its question and whether it answers it, who it is about, whether the argument escalates or flattens into a survey, and the act breaks that can actually be found.
  - B. Section by section: its job, how it opens, the state it leaves the viewer in, its runtime, and its faults: cards out of order, an answer before its setup, a term used before it is introduced, two cards making the same point, a section that peaks and then trails. Not a fault (house style): a speaker's self-introduction landing after their first substantive line, unless the delay runs to minutes or interrupts an argument.
  - C. The joins: for every boundary between two sections, the last card out and the first card in, with a verdict of "lands", "bumps" or "hard cut", and the reason in terms of what changes across the join (subject, speaker, register, scale, place, time, stance).
  - D. Against everything unused: themes with many unused paragraphs the cut never touches (checking specifically for personal stories, stakes and an ending), unused material that contradicts or answers a claim in the cut, stronger unused versions of a point made, and speaker balance (each speaker's paragraphs available against those used).
  - E. Mechanical faults: duplicate plays, note cards still unresolved, orphaned interviewer questions, cards that match nothing in the pool, cards outside every section.

### 43-021: For every weak join, the assistant proposes two or three ranked bridges from unused material.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Only joins graded "bumps" or "hard cut", and joins inside a section that need one, get proposals, and "leave it" is said where a join works. Preference order: a hinge quote naming both subjects; a widening beat; a cost or stakes beat; a human beat; an interviewer question from the unused material (see 43-017's flag); and only when nothing unused can do it, a non-quote beat (b-roll, archive, narration, location) specified exactly. Rules: every proposed paragraph must really be unused and is quoted word for word, never paraphrased; a speaker the viewer has already met is preferred; moving a card already in the cut is preferred over adding one (naming both places); each proposal says where it goes (closing the outgoing section, opening the incoming one, or both, "after card N"), names the hinge word or idea, gives its seconds and its cost.

### 43-022: The story check is delivered as a report beside the cut, with a short verdict in the conversation.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The report opens with the date, which cut and pool were read, the card count, the count of distinct quotes, the runtime and whether durations are real or estimated; then parts A to E, then the bridges. If an earlier report exists the assistant reads it first and notes what changed, and never overwrites one silently. In the conversation it gives only the total and per-section runtimes, a two or three sentence verdict, how many joins are weak, the headline structural finding and where the report is.

### 43-023: The unused material is shown to the owner theme by theme.
- Where: cards-board / What the board is
- What: Every paragraph of the pool not in the cut is listed under its theme, themes with the most unused paragraphs first, each paragraph with its speaker and its English (or its original wording when it has no English, or "(no text)"), questions marked as such. The owner is also told, per speaker, how many of their paragraphs exist and how many are used.

### 43-024: The owner's reading notes are kept as one tidy notes document per episode.
- Where: cards-board / Working the board by hand
- What: While reading his cut, the owner writes raw notes. The assistant turns them into the episode's structure notes, laid out as: a header (episode, which version of the cut, how far he has read, when last tidied, a statement that nothing has yet been done to the cut, a legend, and that lines marked as the assistant's are its own readings); the rules for the re-order (the standing rules below plus any rules for this episode); the shape (acts and numbered beats, each with what it does, what it opens on, what it is built from and what it lands on); the moves (tick boxes, grouped by the section the card sits in now, naming cards only); lines to build on; open questions; an empty inbox with its one-line format hint ("card number - speaker - paragraph - move to ... / good out for ... / cut"); and his raw notes exactly as written, dated. His wording and the strength of his imperatives are kept; notes he has not decided go to Open. Every card mentioned is a working link to that card, and the assistant checks that every linked card is still in the cut and lists any that are not.

### 43-025: Every card his notes mention is pinned down before anything is written.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The assistant resolves every reference in his notes to actual cards: a paragraph reference, a quote (found by searching the English or the Chinese), "X's part", "run from", "the end". It checks each quoted line really is on the card it points to. It can list each section with its card count and minutes, find cards by words in English or Chinese, show one section (optionally one speaker only), or show a run.

### 43-026: "Run from" means that speaker's stretch, not one card.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: A paragraph reference in his notes is a pointer, not a boundary. "Run" means that speaker's stretch from the named card onward, in the same section, until someone else speaks (shown up to 12 cards). A named card should not move alone by default when its neighbours belong with it; neither should neighbours that drift off-topic be dragged along. Whether a run continues past a change of topic is asked, not assumed.

### 43-027: New inbox notes are folded in, and every ambiguity is put to him as a question.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The assistant finds each inbox line's cards and decides where it lands in the shape, the moves and the lines to build on. It collects every ambiguity first: a quote or card not found; a card no longer in the cut; a vague destination; two notes wanting one card; a note contradicting an earlier decision or the shape; whether a run continues past a topic change; a card whose content does not fit the section it was filed under. It asks them all, as multiple-choice questions (up to four at a time, as many rounds as needed), each naming and quoting the cards, stating the consequence and offering two to four concrete options plus "keep it open" where fair. Answers are written in as his decisions; undecided ones go to Open. If he is mid-read, only what he has written is asked about and the unread sections are noted under Open. Processed inbox lines move to the raw notes with the date and the inbox is left empty. He is told what moved where, what is now open, and which readings were the assistant's own.

### 43-028: The standing editorial rules for any re-order of his cut.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: These hold in every phase:
  - Never cut down to a runtime. Remove only interviewer questions and chatter; deleting is his job.
  - Relevance decides where a card goes, for named and unnamed cards alike.
  - The order inside a section is whatever strengthens its argument, with no priority on the original order.
  - Strong transitions first; no voiceover.
  - Intercut the speakers as one conversation, each card a reply to the last; never keep a speaker's run together for its own sake.
  - Expect to splice a linking word (所以 / 然後 / 但是) between cards.
  - A specific note beats a blanket one.
  - A paragraph reference is a pointer, not a boundary (43-026).
  - A card plays once.
  - Ask, never guess. An interpretation written without asking is marked as the assistant's own.

### 43-029: Only when he asks, the assistant re-orders the whole cut from his notes into a new version.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The full re-order happens only on his explicit request ("do the re-order", "build V2"). It never changes his existing cut, the pool or any earlier version: it writes a new version (the next version number if one exists). First it asks, in one batch, whatever his notes do not settle (43-030), and says roughly what the job will cost. It turns the notes' shape into target sections, each with its purpose, what belongs in it and its fixed ways in and out; places every card; orders each section; makes the splices; and checks the result. A large episode takes hours (one real run: 16 interviewees, about 2,200 cards in 37 sections, about nine and a half hours, giving 26 sections with about 1,500 cards playing and 630 staged, and 146 splices).

### 43-030: Decided for him unless he says otherwise: the scope of a re-order.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The assistant asks these first, and uses these defaults where he does not answer:
  - Staged cards go into a staged group directly before the section they would belong to, named "Staged -- <section>".
  - Staging is light.
  - A cold open made of copied cards: the copies are dropped and the originals stay where they are.
  - Interviewer questions and chatter go to one staged bin, "Staged -- Interviewer questions and chatter".
  - Special bins (a jettisoned theme, personal stories) hold only staged cards.
  - Self-introductions are placed (43-032) unless he turns that off.
  - Whether some sections are still unread.
  Each is a manual override he can set.

### 43-031: Decided for him: which section each card goes to, and whether it plays or is staged.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: For every card the assistant judges by what the card says which target section it belongs in and whether it plays there or is staged there. Cards his notes name are fixed in the section they name, playing, and their neighbours are judged around them; a card named for two sections is put to him. Cards in a theme he sends wholesale to one section go there. Cards meant only for a staged bin are always staged. A staged card is still given the section it would have been in. Staging is light: only dead cards, unplayable fragments or clearly weaker repeats are staged, and no card is ever dropped. Every card of the old order ends up exactly once: playing, staged, or in the question bin. He gets a tally per section of cards playing, staged and minutes, and a section far over its share of the runtime is noted.

### 43-032: Decided for him: each speaker's self-introduction is placed just after their first stretch.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Each speaker keeps at most one self-introduction, their single best. It is placed right after the end of that speaker's first continuous stretch of cards in the film, so the viewer hears what they say before who they are. A speaker with more than one candidate is reported ("keep the best, restage the rest"). A speaker with no line in the film has the introduction staged. A speaker with no introduction at all is fine and only listed.

### 43-033: Decided for him: the order inside each section.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Each section is ordered to build its argument: speakers intercut as replies, his fixed ways in and out honoured, moving from setup to landing without giving away the next section early. Each section ends handing the viewer to the next section's fixed first card. The assistant then reads every join (the last two cards of a section and the first two of the next) and may fix a bad hand-off, saying so in its report.

### 43-034: Decided for him: when only part of a card plays, which words.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Where a join needs only part of a card, the part that plays is marked as highlighted in the card's English; several parts of one card may be marked. The original-language line is never changed. Only splices that the ordering asked for are made, plus optional ones only where the join depends on them; none are invented, and staged cards get none. A splice is always whole sentences or clauses, copied word for word from the card (a splice described in Chinese is mapped to the English), and must occur exactly once on the card; one that cannot be found exactly once is skipped and reported. A card he has already marked himself is left alone entirely: his splice wins.

### 43-035: Each section of a re-ordered cut opens with a flag card carrying the reasoning.
- Where: cards-board / Cards on the board
- What: The new version has one bright yellow "TRANSITION / FLAG" note card at the head of each section: the section's name in capitals, the short notes (up to 12 lines) on how that section was ordered and what it hands on, and a line saying it was re-ordered on that date from the structure notes. Card references in it point at paragraphs, never at numbers from the old order or at internal labels.

### 43-036: A re-ordered cut is checked before he gets it.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: Before handing over, the assistant checks that no card is duplicated; that there are no interviewer questions in the cut except one his notes name (listed in the report); that the count of unused paragraphs is the same as in the version it came from (nothing went missing); and that the flag cards carry no internal labels. A re-order whose card numbers no longer match the cut it was planned against is refused and started again.

### 43-037: After a re-order he is told what was done and every call he did not make himself.
- Where: cards-board / What the assistant may do
- What: The structure notes get a status block at the top: what was applied, the new version, and a map from each beat of the shape to its new section numbers; his move tick boxes are left as they were. The episode's planning record notes what was built, how, and which choices are not yet confirmed. He gets a report: a table of new sections by act, the size of each staged group, confirmation that every fixed way in or out and every named card was honoured, how many flags and splices were made, and under "Calls for you to check" every choice he had not made himself, every place his order was changed and why, splices that could not be made, and translation slips (fixed only with clear evidence from the Chinese, and listed).

### 43-038: Some sections are set aside from the cut: alternates and staged shelves.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: A section whose name starts with "Alternates" holds alternative takes that are not in the cut. A section whose name starts with "staged" (any letter case) is a shelf of cards set aside, named by whatever follows ("Staged-- China actually scared" is the shelf "China actually scared"; a bare "Staged" is the plain shelf). Two staged sections with the same name share one shelf. Every other section is in the cut.
- Flag: differs: going straight to a timeline left out any section whose name merely contained "Alternates" (and its cards could be brought in on request), while turning the plan into a cut list kept them, matched and ready, as an alternates section, but only when the name started with "Alternates".

### 43-039: The planned order becomes a cut in the editor, section by section.
- Where: cards-board / What the board is
- What: The planned order turns into a cut list the Timeline Cards editor opens, with no editing program needed. In-cut sections become the cut's sections, in order, each with its name and a colour from a fixed rotation by its position (Blue first). Alternates become an alternates section with their cards matched. Staged sections become staged shelf categories, after the cut. Every other section also gets an empty shelf category of the same name after the cut. Each paragraph card becomes a cut on its interview's clip with its speaker, colour and text; each note card becomes a note (an empty one reads "(empty card)"); a card whose words cannot be found becomes an "unmatched" note holding its text and a link to its paragraph. He is told how many matched, how many matched only loosely, how many did not match (each with its number, paragraph, speaker, first words and the reason), and how many notes, alternates and staged cards there are, and for each interview which clip it was cut from and how it was timed. A preview shows the beginning of the result first; an existing cut list is never replaced unless he asks.
- Flag: unreachable: the empty shelf category for every section could not be switched off by any person.

### 43-040: Decided for him: which clip each interview's cards are cut from.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: For each interview transcript the product picks its clip: first a choice the owner has written down (a transcript to clip pairing, or "skip this transcript", whose cards then report as skipped); then the subtitle export sitting beside the transcript, when exactly one fits (an online-call transcript only matches an online export, and the reverse); then, for a transcript carried over from another episode, the clip of the transcript it says it was taken from (followed at most two steps, a loop stops it); then the clip whose name best matches the transcript's name by words, accepted only at half the words or better, with online and in-person never mixed. A guessed clip that turns out to have no timing at all is withdrawn and the carried-over source is tried instead. With no clip, every card of that transcript is unmatched with "no matching clip". Needs a manual override: the written pairing.

### 43-041: Decided for him: where each clip's word timing comes from.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: Per clip, best first: the product's own word-timed speech-to-text; otherwise Resolve's own transcription of the clip; otherwise the clip's subtitle export, timed only to the subtitle lines. When the main source cannot find a card but the subtitle export can, and does better, the subtitle export is used for that card and the cut is marked as timed from subtitles. The result says, per clip, which was used. Without the editing program a cut can still be worked out from the subtitle exports alone, as a preview; building a timeline needs the editing program.

### 43-042: Decided for him: where in the clip a card's words are.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: A card is found by its last line (for a Mandarin interview, the verbatim Chinese). Punctuation, spacing, letter case, highlight marks and polished English punctuation are ignored, and traditional and simplified Chinese characters count as the same. The first exact occurrence in the clip wins, at full confidence. Otherwise the closest stretch is accepted if it is at least 80% alike (the earliest of equally good stretches), trimmed so a stray echo cannot pull the start early, and marked as a loose match with its confidence. Below that the card is unmatched: "text not found in <clip> transcript". The same paragraph placed twice is pointed out. Needs a manual override: the threshold, and placing a card by hand.

### 43-043: Decided for him: a cut's in and out points.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Frames, frame rates and timecode
- What: Each cut starts 12 frames before its first word and ends 12 frames after its last, but never reaches into the previous or next spoken word (pauses do not count as words), never before the clip's start and never past its end. A cut is always at least one frame. Clips of unknown rate are taken as 29.97 frames per second; timelines are 29.97 drop-frame by default, starting at 01:00:00:00. Rates within a hair of 23.976, 29.97, 59.94 or 47.952 are taken as exactly those broadcast rates. A start timecode that cannot be read is taken as 01:00:00:00. Each of these is an override he can set.

### 43-044: Decided for him: whether a plan is interviews or a montage.
- Where: cards-board / Montages
- What: A montage card already names its exact clip and its in and out (minutes and seconds), with its quote and speaker above, so it needs no finding. A plan is treated as a montage when more of its cards name a clip than point at an interview paragraph; otherwise as interviews. On a montage, a card with an in but no out, an interview card, or a borrowed paragraph each becomes an unmatched note and is listed with the reason ("no pinned span", "needs matching"); a card with no clip becomes a plain note. Montage clips start at 00:00:00:00 and take their rate from the product's own record of the clip. Needs a manual override: choosing montage or interviews.

### 43-045: The planned order can be built straight into a Resolve timeline, with markers for every section, note and failure.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Sending a cut to Resolve
- What: With Resolve open on the right project, the order becomes a new timeline, named "<plan name> Conform <date>" unless he names it. Nothing is built until he confirms; first he sees, per interview, the clip chosen, how it was timed and its colour, then a table of every card (number, section, paragraph, clip or note, in, out, seconds, confidence, status: cut, cut timed from subtitles, gap with marker, or unmatched with the reason) and a summary of cuts with minutes, gaps and unmatched. On building: each section starts with a marker named after it, section markers taking a rotating colour so neighbours differ (Blue, Sand, Purple, Mint, Rose, Sky, Lemon, Fuchsia, Cyan, Cocoa, Lavender, Yellow, Pink, Cream, then again), never Green or Red; a note card leaves a Green marker carrying its text and 5 seconds of empty timeline; an unmatched card leaves a Red "UNMATCHED" marker with the reason and the same gap; a cut that does not land exactly where expected and within 2 frames of its length is removed and replaced by a Red "FAILED" marker and a gap, and the rest carries on. Each cut is coloured with its speaker's colour unless he says not to. He is told how many cuts were placed, failed and tinted and how many markers were set. Refused with "no project is open in Resolve", "nothing to place -- no matched cuts", or "could not create timeline". Planning can also be narrowed to some interviews or the first N cards.
- Flag: differs: section marker colours here count every section, while the cut list's section colours count only in-cut sections, so the two disagree once a staged or alternates section sits mid-order.

### 43-046: Every timing of the cut is written down as a report he can read.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Sending a cut to Resolve
- What: Unless he turns it off, a report beside the plan lists the date and time, the timeline name, whether it was built or only previewed, and every card with its section, paragraph, clip or note, in, out, seconds, confidence and status; cuts that failed to land appear as unmatched. It is replaced on every run, previews included. The story check uses its real durations.

### 43-047: Interviewee colours are carried onto Resolve's clip colours.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Undo, markers, titles, fades and properties
- What: Each of the 16 palette colours is paired with one of Resolve's clip colours, in order: Blue, Violet, Green, Teal, Tan, Chocolate, Pink, Navy, Purple, Olive, Yellow, Blue, Purple, Green, Orange, Navy, so the first 8 speakers get 8 different clip colours and they repeat after that. A colour chosen by hand that is not in the palette is matched to the nearest Resolve clip colour. Resolve's Brown is never used anywhere. Any colour picker offers Resolve's 16 clip colours minus Brown, in Resolve's own menu order (Orange, Apricot, Yellow, Lime, Olive, Green, Teal, Navy, Blue, Purple, Violet, Pink, Tan, Beige, Chocolate).

### 43-048: The owner can colour an episode's multicam clips, and a timeline's cuts, by speaker in Resolve.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Undo, markers, titles, fades and properties
- What: For a chosen bin (default: the bin open in Resolve), each multicam clip is matched to its interviewee and shown as "already <colour>" or "would set <colour> (now <colour>)"; nothing changes until he confirms, then each shows "set" or "FAILED to set". A clip with no matching interviewee still gets a steady colour of its own, and he is told which clips had no interviewee. Optionally the cuts on the first video track of the current timeline are brought to their speaker's colour too, only those that differ, each change one undo step in Resolve. Without Resolve he can still see every interviewee's colour. Refused with "no project is open in Resolve", "no multicam clips in bin '<name>'", or "no timeline is open in Resolve".

### 43-049: Placement failures can be explained position by position for the people building it.
- Where: cards-to-resolve-and-exports / Sending a cut to Resolve
- What: A hidden switch makes the timeline build print, for every cut that fails to land, where it was expected and where it actually went.
- Flag: dev only
