# The capabilities and their sub-topics

The specification and the knowledge base are filed under the same 18 capabilities. Under each are the sub-topics the knowledge base already uses; an item of the specification is filed under one of them where it fits, or under a new plain heading where none does.

## `whole-product`: The whole product

Rules that hold on every screen and in every part: the look, wording, how errors and unknowns are shown, the assistant in general (keys, switching it off), the product's names and branding, behaviour common to all parts.

Sub-topics: Could not check, refusals and nothing silent; The rebuild itself; The look; Wording; Safety in what the product does; Windows, text and names; Requests, logs and outside programs; The assistant.

## `accounts-privacy-legal`: Accounts, privacy and the legal paperwork

Who may sign in and what each person may see and do; passwords, sign-in keys and sessions; what is recorded about people and computers and their say over it; the licence and legal paperwork a person meets.

Sub-topics: Who may sign in; How a computer proves who it belongs to; Suspending and removing a person; Passwords, sessions and failed sign-ins; What each person may see and reach; What the product records about a person; The licence a person accepts; Third-party licences and what customers receive; How secrets and keys are kept.

## `sync`: Sync

Getting footage between the server and each editor's computer: which projects a computer takes, originals going up, proxies and originals coming down, shared folders, wired or remote, upload only, the sync drive, disk space, pausing and stopping, the stops that protect footage, what an editor and an admin are told about progress.

Sub-topics: What syncs where; Uploads that cannot resume, shutdown and sleep; A plan belongs to a computer; Wired or remote; Upload only; Deletions and removing a project; Safety stops; Studio-wide stops and the editor's pause; Files that must never sync; Names; The sync drive; Disk space; Stuck, slow and fair transfers; Saying truthfully what the sync is doing; What is owed; Load on the NAS when nothing changes; Speed and routes; Clocks and file age; Computers joining and changing; Project folders on an editor's computer; Facts about the NAS and the operating systems.

## `proxies-and-file-moves`: Proxies and file moves

Making proxies (on the server or on editors' computers) and knowing which clips have them; moving, renaming and relinking files so that every computer follows; copy tools.

Sub-topics: Making proxies on editors' computers; What Resolve accepts as a proxy; What a proxy must be; Raw camera formats and Blackmagic Proxy Generator; Pairing proxies with their originals; Writing, copying and deleting files safely; Moving footage through the product; Moves that reach every computer; Following a move on an editor's computer; Relinking Resolve after a move; Moves made by hand on the NAS; Names: letter case and Unicode; Relinking and gathering missing clips.

## `fleet-jobs`: Work shared out across the computers

Heavy work shared out across the studio's computers: which computer does a piece of work, when, what an editor and an admin see, cancelling, per-computer and per-kind settings.

Sub-topics: When a computer counts as free and able; What a computer can honestly offer; Choosing who runs what; Load on the NAS and the computers; Progress; Stopping and cancelling; Outages and withdrawn access; Failures, retries and the queue; Results that are complete and current.

## `dashboard`: The dashboard

The web pages an admin and editors open: the overview, projects, transfers, the per-computer view, problems the server found, alerts and reports, settings (each by what it does), searching, help pages. NOT the pages of the switchable parts below.

Sub-topics: Overview, menus and finding things; Each computer's state; Ticking and unticking; Projects; Transfers and the queue; People and computers; Reports from computers; Problems the server finds; Alerts by mail; Settings and setup; Pages that refresh and answer clicks; Speed under load; Phones and the installed app; Security of pages; Stopping and resuming the fleet.

## `editor-app`: The app on each editor's computer

The program on each editor's computer as a person meets it: its tray icon and menu, windows, dialogs and notifications, its settings, starting and quitting, sleep and power, what it tells the editor is wrong, installing and first-run set-up on Windows and macOS.

Sub-topics: Installing and setting up; Security warnings, permissions and certificates; Drive letters and network drives; Starting, staying running and crashing; The tray or menu-bar icon and its menu; Windows, dialogs and notifications; Telling the editor what is wrong; Signing in, the computer's identity and the licence; Settings, records and background work; Uninstalling.

## `releases-and-updates`: Releases and updates

How a new version reaches the server and every editor's computer: publishing, trust and signing as a person meets them, automatic and pushed updates, rollback, version display, what the vendor does for customers.

Sub-topics: Installing an update on an editor's computer; Facts about Windows, macOS and the NAS that bite an update; Version numbers; Signing and trust; Making and publishing a release; Trying a build on one computer first; Offering an update to a computer; Pushing updates and seeing who has what; Rolling back and withdrawing a build; The server updating itself; Customer sites and the vendor's release feed.

## `server-install-and-running`: The server: installing it and keeping it running

Setting up a studio's server and keeping it running: installing, the set-up wizard, kinds of NAS, backups and snapshots, restoring, health, secrets an admin must enter, the studio's network and remote access.

Sub-topics: Installing on a NAS; Settings and secrets; Updates, restarts and rollbacks; Shares that go away, and checks that cannot run; Snapshots, restoring and the server's disk; Off-site backup; Health checks and alerts; Connections and credentials; NAS speed, load and clock.

## `resolve-on-the-editors-computer`: Resolve on the editor's computer

Everything the product does with DaVinci Resolve on an editor's computer OUTSIDE Timeline Cards: noticing missing or unsynced media, relinking and fixing, undo of those changes, bins and imports, shared LUTs and stills, proxies inside Resolve, set-up of Resolve itself. (A switchable part, on by default.)

Sub-topics: Multicams, frame rates and timelines; Clip paths and media outside the shared tree; Setting Resolve up: edition, scripting switch, preferences; Bins, clip sorting and imports; Connecting to Resolve's scripting: launch, stalls and death; Project libraries and projects; Knowing which project is open; Missing clips, relinking and refreshing; Proxies in Resolve; Colour look-up tables (LUTs); Fixing media: copying in, rehearsals and undo; Grading from the server's originals; Other givens on the editor's computer.

## `broll`: The b-roll archive

The b-roll archive: how it is laid out, bringing footage in, describing and indexing clips, search, the clip player and previews, sending a clip to the editing program, client folders and the links handed to clients. (A switchable part.)

Sub-topics: Givens: browsers, cameras, files and Resolve; How the archive is laid out; Describing and indexing clips; Search; The clip player; Previews made for the archive; Bringing new footage in; Sending a clip to Resolve; Previews standing in for originals; Client folders and links; Replacing the search index.

## `music`: Music

The music library: which files are music, analysis, search and similar tracks, playing, sending a track to the editing program. (A switchable part.)

Sub-topics: What the owner wants; Which files are music; Adding tracks to the library; Imports across computers; Keeping the library's data whole; Searching and filtering; Listening in the browser; Sending to Resolve; Keeping files safe.

## `youtube`: YouTube downloads

YouTube downloads: searching, picking, downloading on the server or on the editor's own computer, making clips ready to edit, where they are filed, what YouTube blocks. (A switchable part.)

Sub-topics: Searching and reviewing results; How YouTube throttles and refuses; Signing in to YouTube; Files Resolve can decode; File names and what a file carries; Where a download runs, and saying so; One job, one computer; Choosing the destination and checking space; Jobs on the page: queue, retry, cancel; Progress; Reaching the editor and Resolve; Keeping the downloader working; The assistant behind search; Rights and terms.

## `footage-sorter-and-camera-cards`: The Footage Sorter and camera cards

Getting footage off camera cards and recorder cards and into the studio's folders: recognising cards, copying and verifying, sorting by shoot, day and camera, naming, duplicates, when a card may be wiped, tracking where every copy of every clip is, field backup, the Sorter's pages. (A switchable part.)

Sub-topics: Recognising cards and drives; What cameras and recorders write; Damaged and unfinished recordings; Clocks and file dates; Telling recordings apart; Copying a card; Checking a copy; Drives, speed and interruptions; Backed up and safe to format; The record of every copy; Sorting into shoots, people and cameras; Moving footage on the NAS; Placing sound recordings; Thumbnails and hover filmstrips; The sorter page; Archive search; Downloading originals and sharing folders.

## `cards-footage-in`: Timeline Cards: footage in, ready to cut

Timeline Cards, step 0: clips dropped in and made ready to cut with no further step: which clips are one interview, lining up angles by sound, the multicam, transcript, translation, word timing, every automatic decision and its manual override. (Timeline Cards is one switchable part.)

Sub-topics: Cameras, sound recorders and their clocks; Frame rates; Facts about Resolve: rates, multicams, timecode; Grouping clips and building the multicam; Facts about Resolve's own transcription; Speech-to-text: rulings; Speech-to-text: facts and situations; Chinese script; The transcript and its English; Word links between the original and the English; Running on its own, with a hand on every decision; Footage files and files made from them; Bringing the studio's existing transcripts in once; Montages.

## `cards-board`: Timeline Cards: the board

Timeline Cards, step 1: the board for planning the film: theme columns, transcript paragraphs as cards, notes, the assistant proposing themes and updating the board when called.

Sub-topics: What the board is; Cards on the board; Working the board by hand; What the assistant may do; The owner's editorial rules; Montages.

## `cards-editor`: Timeline Cards: the editor

Timeline Cards, step 2: the editor where the film is cut: every screen, panel, key, controller button and gesture; the lane, cards, sections, playback and audio, transcript panel and search, shortening and the edit chat, the English a cut shows, the shelf and notes, offline use, several devices, opening episodes, the cut-list file.

Sub-topics: Chinese text and matching; Search and the transcript panel; The English a cut shows; Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant; Selecting, deleting, inserting and keys; Notes and the staged shelf; Editing, undo and the cut list; Speed and smoothness; Playback and audio; The lane: clicking, trimming, zooming and dragging; Phone and game controller; The page: panels, header, warnings and redrawing; Opening episodes and keeping the editor running; Working offline, connections and several devices; Sections and the overview; Colours; Media the browser gets; The lane: how clips look and read; Resolve is optional.

## `cards-to-resolve-and-exports`: Timeline Cards: the hand-over to Resolve, and exports

Timeline Cards, step 3: handing the cut to the editing program (multicams and the cut as a timeline, fades, gain, markers, frame rates) and the exports and imports (XML, OTIO, EDL, script).

Sub-topics: Frames, frame rates and timecode; Placing, deleting and linking clips in Resolve; Undo, markers, titles, fades and properties; Talking to Resolve: restarts, dialogs and speed; Sending a cut to Resolve; Edits sent live and staged edits; Bringing Resolve's changes back into the cut; Which computer is driving Resolve; Exports; Renders from Resolve; Titles and compound clips; Subtitles and delivery.
