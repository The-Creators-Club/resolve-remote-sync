### 28-001: The editor's keys work on any keyboard layout, and stay out of the way while the editor is typing.
- Where: cards-editor / Selecting, deleting, inserting and keys
- What: Terms used by every key item below: the cut list is the document being edited; its timeline is an ordered run of items, which are cuts (one piece of an interview clip, with a source in point and out point in seconds of that clip) and gaps (a stretch of silent timeline time with a length). Gaps behave like clips everywhere: they are selected, moved, deleted and resized like one. The shelf holds staged cuts, kept in the document but not on the timeline. The head is the playhead. The lane is the horizontal timeline strip at the bottom; "the strip" means the lane's track area itself holding the keyboard (clicked or tabbed to). The keyboard is in one of three places: the lane (the default: the page body, the lane, the transport buttons), the cards list, or the transcript panel. A frame is 1 / the cut list's frame rate. The shortest cut and the shortest gap are both 1/24 s.
  - Keys are matched by physical key position, so every binding holds on any keyboard layout. Ctrl and Cmd are the same modifier. A chord is written modifiers first, in the order Ctrl, Alt, Shift.
  - A key is ignored entirely (it goes to the page) when the focus is in a text box, text area, select list, anything editable, or an input of any kind other than button, checkbox, radio, submit, reset, range, colour, file or image; while an input-method composition (for example typing Chinese) is in progress; and when another part of the page has already handled the key.
  - A key can be limited to some keyboard places. When a key has a meaning for the current place and one for everywhere, the place's own meaning wins. A key marked "strip only" works only when the lane's track area has the keyboard; elsewhere that key is not taken at all.
  - Holding a key down: only the keys listed as repeating fire again on the operating system's key repeat. A held key that is the editor's but does not repeat is still swallowed, so the page does not scroll (for example a held Space).
  - A key that is the editor's but means nothing in the current situation (for example Escape with nothing selected or armed) is left for the rest of the page; its repeats are still swallowed.
- Exact: yes

### 28-002: Space and Alt never trigger the browser or the last clicked button by accident, and a lost window focus ends every held key cleanly.
- Where: cards-editor / Selecting, deleting, inserting and keys
- What: When the editor takes Space while focus is on a button, link or other control, the browser's own "click on key release" is suppressed, and any keyboard-made click on that same control within 600 ms after the Space is swallowed. So Space never re-presses the last clicked button (for example the settings menu). A lone Alt press (down or up), outside a text box, is cancelled, and on Alt release the window takes the focus back: on Windows, Chrome otherwise moves focus to its own menu button after an Alt+drag, Alt+click or Alt+wheel, and the next Space opens Chrome's menu. When the window loses focus, a Space held for its "hold" meaning ends (if the hold had not yet reached 280 ms it counts as a tap and toggles play), and an F held for its hold meaning is abandoned without sending its tap. When the editor's keys are taken away (the editor closes), any held Space ends without doing anything, a held F is abandoned, and a pending Delete arm is dropped.
- Exact: yes

### 28-003: Space plays and pauses with a tap, and plays at double speed while held.

### 28-005: The frame and second keys move the head, or trim an armed edge.

- What: An armed edge is one edge (in or out) of one item, chosen by clicking that edge on the lane; while one is armed, these keys trim it instead of moving the head. All work everywhere and repeat while held.

Cuts are staged on the shelf under a shelf named after the last section heading above each cut (a heading longer than 60 characters, containing comment markers, or starting with # is not used). Gaps are removed outright (undo puts them back). The info notice joins its parts with "; ": "1 cut staged under ## <shelf>" or "<N> cuts staged under ## <shelf>, ## <shelf>", and "gap taken out" or "<N> gaps taken out". The selection then moves to the next surviving timeline item after the last removed one, else the one before the first removed, else nothing.
- Exact: yes

### 28-073: Staging without deleting, and putting shelf items back on the timeline.
- Where: cards-editor / Notes and the staged shelf
- What: Staging moves the given (else selected, else focused) timeline items to the shelf, with no notice; refused "select a cut first" when none is on the timeline; the selection moves on as after a delete. Putting shelf items back places them after the item under the head by default, else at the end; refused "select a cut first" with none; they become the selection.

### 28-074: Inserting and resizing gaps.
- Where: cards-editor / Editing, undo and the cut list
- What: A new gap is refused "a gap shorter than a frame is no gap" below 1/24 s. By default it goes at the nearer boundary of the item under the head (before it when the head is in its first half, after it otherwise), else at the end. The new gap is selected, so its edges are the ones trimmed next. The head keeps its timeline time. Resizing a gap: refused "that gap is no longer in the cut list" or "a gap is a number of seconds"; below 1/24 s, a gap on the timeline is taken out with the info notice "gap taken out" and the selection moves on; elsewhere refused "a gap shorter than a frame is no gap -- delete it instead". The head rule applies.
- Exact: yes

### 28-075: Fades, gain, clip colour, notes and markers are edits like any other.

- What: Setting a cut's audio fades takes the given lengths. Setting gain or clip colour acts on the given, else selected, else focused items; refused "select a cut first". ### 28-076: Undo and redo take back only this editor's own edits, and refuse when someone else has since changed the same thing.
- Where: cards-editor / Editing, undo and the cut list
- What:
  - Undo is per browser: only this editor's own edits are undone, never anybody else's.
  - Refused (info) "nothing to undo" / "nothing to redo"; "no cut list is open".
  - Refused when somebody else's edit has since changed what the step touches: "cannot undo the <label>: <what> was changed since by another edit" (or "cannot redo ..."), where <what> is "cut <N>", "the order of the cut list", or "that part of the cut list". The step is then thrown away. Undoing one's own later steps first never trips this. The server checks the same thing, so an undo crossed by another edit on its way is refused with "cannot undo the <label>: <reason>" and thrown away.
  - Labels: trim, split, move, delete, stage, placing, shelf move, discard, insert, paste, new gap, gap resize, fade, gain change, colour change, note, highlight, words, gap note, section summary, marker, marker edit, marker move, marker removal, section move, new category, category removal, category rename, saved sequence, sequence restore, sequence rename, sequence removal, settings change, edit (several changes together, or anything else), undo.
  - Any new edit clears redo. At most 200 steps are kept each way (oldest dropped). When the server refuses one of this editor's edits, every undo / redo step naming it is forgotten.
  - The history survives a reload, per cut list, on this device; on opening a cut list it is back before any edit can be made; damaged entries are dropped, the newest 200 kept. If the device refuses to store it, undo still works until the page closes. Opening another cut list starts with that list's own saved history (or empty).
- Exact: yes

### 28-077: Opening a cut list shows this device's copy at once, offline or not.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: Opening a cut list clears the selection, the in / out range, the playhead (to 0, stopped) and the notices, loads that list's undo history, and shows the connection as "connecting". The editor shows what this device has (the last saved copy plus every unsent edit) at once, before the server answers. With no saved copy, the editor opens it from the server, and from the device's offline copy of the page when offline. Every edit applies on screen at once and is sent only once it is saved on this device; edits reach the server in the order they were made, and everything not yet confirmed is sent again after a reconnect, never applied twice. Closing a cut list (or opening another) saves everything and stops playback.
- Exact: yes

### 28-078: The editor shows its connection state, and reconnects by itself.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: States shown: offline, connecting, syncing (connected with edits waiting), live (connected, nothing waiting), error, with the count of waiting edits and the last error sentence. After any drop the editor reconnects by itself, waiting a random time that grows up to 10 s; at once when the browser says it is online again. The browser saying it is offline shows "offline" unless the connection is live. A connection that has heard nothing for 45 s is treated as dead and reconnected at once. If after connecting this device's copy disagrees with the server's, the warning "this editor's copy did not match the server's, so it was reloaded from the server" and the server's copy is loaded. When the server's changes cannot be followed, the editor quietly starts over from the server.
- Exact: yes

### 28-079: What the editor says when the server refuses an edit, a connection fails for good, or the sign-in has expired.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What:
  - The server refuses one of this editor's edits: the warning "your <label> of cut <N> was refused: <reason>" (or "your <label> was refused: ..."), the edit disappears from the screen, and its undo steps are forgotten.
  - An edit made here, not yet sent, that no longer applies after other people's edits arrive is dropped with the warning "your <label> of cut <N> no longer applies: <reason>". An edit that has (or may have) reached the server is never dropped on a guess; it waits for the server's verdict.
  - Messages the server sends as notices are shown as notices.
  - A protocol mismatch, a cut list not found, or a bad key is final: the state shows "error" with the server's sentence as an error notice, and nothing is retried until "Retry now". Any other server error (for example overload) shows "connecting" with the sentence and retries every 10 s or so.
  - A connection refused before it ever opened, while online, checks the sign-in. If it has expired: "Your sign-in has expired: sign in again to send your edits", and no retries until asked. Otherwise the normal retry.
- Exact: yes

### 28-080: Edits are kept on this device until the server has them, and the editor says when the device will not keep them.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: The device's copy of the cut list is saved within half a second of the last change, at once when the page is hidden, and when the cut list closes. If what the device saved cannot be read: the warning "could not read what this device saved (<reason>); loading from the server". If the device refuses to save (storage full, private mode): the error notice, once, "this device could not save your edits (<reason>): they are kept only until they reach the server"; edits are still sent. In a browser that offers no storage (for example a private window), nothing survives a reload.
- Exact: yes

### 28-081: Two tabs on one cut list never lose each other's edits.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: Unsent edits are kept per browser tab, so two tabs on one cut list never overwrite each other's. Opening a cut list takes up every tab's unsent edits, each once, so a closed tab's edits are sent by the next tab to open it. An edit held by two tabs is applied once.
- Exact: yes

### 28-082: Which cut list opens on start, and the address that reopens it.

- What: The cut list named in the address opens; else the last one this device opened; else the most recently changed one on the server. The address is updated to name it, so a reload, online or not, opens the same one. The editor asks for the screen to be kept awake. A clip kept on this device plays from the device copy, online or not. In a browser that cannot play sound, editing still works without playback. Everything is saved to the device when the page is hidden. The editor page itself opens offline.
- Exact: yes
