### 40-001: The editor offers a choice of three strengths of assistant, each with a short note.
- Where: whole-product / The assistant
- What: Wherever Timeline Cards lets a person choose the assistant's strength (the transcript search panel and the edit chat), three choices are offered: one described as "fastest", one as "stronger" and one as "most capable". While the product is on the versions it knows, each note also carries its price per million units of text read and written (the cheapest about 2 and 10 dollars, the middle about 4 and 20, the strongest about 10 and 50). The panel says which choice is that panel's default: transcript search defaults to the fastest, the edit chat to the most capable. Some other assistant jobs in the engine always use a fixed strength the person cannot change (summaries, the montage builder and the search inside one transcript use the fastest).

### 40-002: The assistant choices move to newer versions by themselves, and a bad choice never fails.
- Where: whole-product / The assistant
- What: When the provider makes a newer version of one of the three strengths available, the choice moves to it by itself, never to anything older than the version the product shipped with. Its label then shows the new version and its note loses the price (just "fastest", "stronger" or "most capable"), because no price is known for it. The product checks for newer versions at most every 6 hours (10 minutes after a failed check), never making a person wait for the check; until a check has answered, or if checks fail, it keeps what it last knew. A request that names a strength the product does not recognise (empty, misspelt) is quietly given that feature's default; nothing is ever refused for a bad choice.

### 40-003: Each strength has its own conversation, so switching strength starts over at that strength's cost.
- Where: whole-product / The assistant
- What: A conversation the assistant holds (a search scope, an edit chat) belongs to the strength it was opened with. Switching the picker opens a separate conversation, which first re-reads the material (slow, and paid again at that strength's price), rather than continuing the one on screen. When a strength moves to a newer version (40-002), an open conversation simply continues under the new version.

### 40-004: When the assistant cannot be reached, the buttons that need it are dimmed with the reason, and failures are explained in words.
- Where: whole-product / The assistant
- What: Whether the assistant is available on this server is worked out once and shown on the page, so every button that needs it is dimmed with a one-sentence reason. Checking availability never stops the page from updating. When a request to the assistant fails, the person is told in words: the provider could not be asked, the provider failed (with its own message), the provider answered with something that was not a result, or "the AI provider refused, and said why in no words at all". The name of the provider that answered is remembered and shown. When cards are selected in the editor, the selection always travels with the question as text.

### 40-005: A conversation with the assistant is remembered per cut list and survives reloads and restarts.
- Where: whole-product / The assistant
- What: Each cut list remembers the conversation the assistant holds about it, saved after every completed turn, so reloading the page or restarting the server continues the conversation instead of making the assistant read all the material again. Starting over (the "new conversation" or "start over" buttons) gives a fresh conversation. A cut list restored from its history keeps talking to the same conversation. A conversation with no cut list open is kept only until the server restarts. If the remembered conversation record is missing or damaged, the next turn simply opens a new one; if the episode folder cannot be written, the conversation is simply not remembered. Conversations are held by the provider for one server only: a conversation opened on one server cannot be continued on another, and a rebuilt server loses them. When the provider no longer knows a conversation, the feature re-opens it once (re-reading its material) and says so on its status line; it never retries silently.

### 40-010: A card's words come from the best transcription available for its clip.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: The product decides, per clip, which transcription a card's words come from, best first: the product's own word-timed speech-to-text for that clip (with who-is-speaking per word when known); otherwise the transcription Resolve stored for the clip; otherwise a subtitle file exported for the clip. ### 40-011: The product decides by itself which transcript document belongs to each clip.
- Where: cards-footage-in / Running on its own, with a hand on every decision
- What: To link a clip to its bilingual transcript, the product looks first among the episode's clip (montage source) transcripts by the clip's media name; otherwise it guesses the interviewee from the clip name against the episode's interviewee names and looks in that person's transcripts. Each candidate transcript is scored by how many of its paragraphs are found in the clip's words; a transcript needs at least 3 matching paragraphs to be accepted, so one interviewee's different shoot days are not mixed up, and the transcript with the most wins. A montage source folder holding exactly one transcript is accepted without a vote. Where a subtitle file of the same stretch of time exists, it is used to place paragraphs (faster and finds more), while card timing still uses the word timing. If no transcript wins, every other word source already on disk for that clip is tried and the one that places the most paragraphs wins; no new transcription is run for this. Each clip is classed as "montage", "interview" or cannot tell. A person is not told which source linked a clip.

### 40-024: What a person types into a search matches Chinese and English forgivingly, and pinyin works.
- Where: cards-editor / Chinese text and matching
- What: 
  - Searches ignore letter case, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese characters count as the same.
  - A query in quotes (straight or curly, at least 3 characters with the quotes) matches exact letter case, without folding Chinese, and as whole words where the query's edge is a letter or digit (so "I" in quotes does not match the I of "In").
  - Pinyin: a query mixing Chinese characters and Latin letters treats each run of letters as the sound of the characters in that position, each character contributing the start of one of its readings (so 我dh finds 我的話, and 我d finds 我的, 我得, 我德).
  - A query of letters only (optionally with tone digits 1 to 5, spaces or apostrophes, at least 2 letters), such as chu1, chuqu or guo2wang2, is searched both as plain English text and as pinyin in the Chinese, where each character must take a whole syllable (except the last letters, which may still be being typed); results of both are merged.
  - A tone digit after a syllable must match the character's tone (5 is the neutral tone); no digit matches any tone. ü is typed as v. Every reading of a character with several readings counts. Longest matches are preferred.
- Exact: yes

### 40-025: A highlighted stretch of transcript becomes a card of exactly those words.
- Where: cards-editor / Search and the transcript panel
- What: When an editor highlights text in a transcript and inserts it:
  - The selection is first looked for inside the stretch of the clip covered by the highlighted paragraphs (with 8 characters of slack), then in the whole clip, accepting a close match (about 75% alike).
  - If that fails and the selection is the English of a Mandarin interview, every paragraph whose English contains the selection (or is contained in it, both at least 8 characters) contributes its whole Mandarin span.
  - If the text match failed, or landed more than 2 seconds outside the highlighted paragraphs, the highlighted paragraphs' own span is used instead.
  - In those last two cases the card is flagged "whole paragraph" with its length in seconds and the page warns.
  - 12 frames of handle are added either side, never reaching into the neighbouring words and never before the clip's start; a card is at least one frame.
  - Refused with "couldn't find that text in the clip's transcript -- try selecting the Mandarin line, or a longer stretch", or "no transcript for that clip".
  - A span the page timed itself from a played preview gets the same handles; refused with "empty span" or "no transcript for that clip". A span given in frames is refused with "that clip is not in the bin scan" or "empty span".
- Exact: yes

Refused unless the cut list is still as the proposal left it: "the cut list has been edited since then, so undoing that would take the later work with it. Use undo on the cards instead." Also "none of those suggestions has been applied, so there is nothing to undo", "that one has been undone already", "that one changed nothing, so there is nothing to undo".
- Exact: yes

### 40-073: The chat refuses a message plainly, and says why a turn was slow.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Refusals: "type what you want changed"; "the edit chat needs claude: <why>"; "the cut list changed under you - the page has reloaded, ask again"; "Claude is still working on the last message" (one message at a time); "Claude answered with <type> instead of an instruction, so nothing was changed"; "the Claude session was lost twice running: give it a moment and retry"; "the Claude session could not be opened: the provider refused the id twice running". A slow turn is explained as "opened a new conversation: " followed by one of: "the first message of the session reads the transcripts and the cut list"; "the transcripts changed since the last message, so they and the cut list were read again"; "the house style changed since the last message, so it and the cut list were read again"; "the conversation had gone cold (over an hour), so the cut list was read again"; "the conversation could not be dated, so the cut list was read again"; "the Claude session was lost (new machine or a rebuilt container), so the cut list went in again".
- Exact: yes

### 40-074: The chat panel shows what it is working with.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: The panel shows whether a cut list is open (and why not), whether the assistant is available, the cut list's name, the strength and the three choices, the revision, the conversation (turns, warm or not, seconds since the last message, provider), the material (documents, interviewees, clips, size, whether an episode house style is in use, whether this conversation holds the current material) and the history.
- Exact: yes

### 40-075: Shortening removes material to a target length and never rewrites, reorders or deletes.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: A shortening is a set of removals only: it never reorders, never inserts and never changes a word. Every removal, once accepted, moves onto the shelf under the shortening's own category, named "shortened <target> <date>" (for example "shortened 12:00 2026-09-11"), so it stays in the file, playable and restorable. Refusals common to every step: "open a cut list first - the chat edits a .cut.md, not a live timeline"; "this cut list is open read only, so nothing can be changed in it"; "shortening needs claude: <why>". Every step answers with a sentence rather than failing.
- Exact: yes

### 40-076: The editor says how long the cut should be, as a length or a percentage.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: A target is a length: 720, 12:00, 1:02:30, 12m, 12 min, 12 minutes, 720s (a bare number is seconds; zero or less is not a target); or a percentage of the current length to keep. Lengths are shown as m:ss, or h:mm:ss past an hour. Refusals: "say how long it should be: a length like 12:00, or a percentage of what it runs now"; "this cut already runs <now>, which is inside <target> - there is nothing to shorten"; "this cut list has no section headings, so there is no structure to budget. Add headings first, or ask the chat to cut it down in words."
- Exact: yes

### 40-077: The editor can limit shortening to some sections, steer it in words, and pin sections to keep or drop.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Choosing sections makes the target and current length refer to those sections only; the others are outside the plan, and the turn reads "shorten to <t> (N of M sections)". If none of the chosen sections is left: "none of the chosen sections is in this cut list any more - reload the page and choose again". Free text from the editor is passed on as "THE EDITOR ALSO SAYS: ...". Each section can be pinned keep, drop or free; a pin always overrides the plan (the row's reason becomes "pinned KEEP by the editor" / "pinned DROP by the editor"), and removing a pin restores the plan's own choice for that section.
- Exact: yes

### 40-078: Shortening first plans per section, and the editor can edit that plan.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: The assistant is given the target, the current length, how much must go, the rubric (40-082) and each section's card count and length, and answers per section keep, thin (to about N seconds) or drop, each with a reason, plus a two-or-three-sentence note to the editor. A section it left out is kept whole ("Claude said nothing about this section, so it is kept whole"); a thin with no length, or a length not below the section's own, becomes keep ("the thinning target did not make sense"). Where the plan lands is always the product's own sum (dropped sections count 0, thinned ones their target, kept ones their length), never the assistant's arithmetic. An answer that is not a plan: "Claude answered with <type> instead of a budget, so nothing was planned". The plan is recorded in the edit chat as a turn "shorten to <t>". The editor can edit the plan table; edited rows are taken as written and re-summed, and pins sent with the table win. Planning gives up after 10 minutes.
- Exact: yes

### 40-079: Shortening then chooses, section by section, which cards and which sentences go.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Sections planned keep stay whole ("kept whole"); sections with no cards are left. A dropped section loses every card except those carrying a note, and its heading stays, empty ("section dropped; its heading stays, empty; N card(s) kept for its note"). A thinned section is shown to the assistant one sentence per line, with every word numbered; a sentence ends at . ? ! … or 。？！, or at a silence of over 0.7 seconds between words. A card with a note is marked protected; highlighted words are listed; the plan's reason for this section is given. Cards without word timing are left whole ("no word timing in this section, so nothing was cut in it" when none have it). The assistant answers, per card, drop the whole card or keep these word ranges, each removal with a reason and a kind (restate, detail, setup, scene, second example, fluff). Each section is a separate question that sees only that section's cards; the page has up to 4 sections in flight; each gives up after 10 minutes.
- Exact: yes

### 40-080: Rules every shortening removal must pass, whatever the assistant says.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: A refused removal costs only itself; the section's note says "N op(s) refused: ..." with the first three reasons.
  - Only drop or keep; the card must be in the section; one per card.
  - A card with a note is never removed: "[uid] carries a note, and a card the editor has written on is never removed".
  - Every removal needs a reason: "... would be removed with no reason, and a removal without a reason is refused".
  - A section's last card is never dropped unless the whole section is: "... a section keeps its last card unless the whole section is dropped (the cliffhanger rule)".
  - A keep needs ranges: "... is a keep with no ranges. To remove the whole card, say {"op": "drop"}."; whole word positions inside the card, with the first and last two or three words copied (at most 6 each).
  - The copied words must spell the card's own words at that end (ignoring case, spacing and punctuation). If they match exactly one position up to 3 words either way, that end is moved to match; otherwise: "the index and the text disagree: head says "...", but words a to b of that card are "...". Nothing is guessed - say which one you meant."
  - A kept range starts at a sentence start and ends at a sentence end, unless it is marked as a deliberate mid-sentence splice with a reason (a mark with no reason is refused).
  - Ranges are in order and do not overlap; a keep that keeps the whole card is not a removal.
- Exact: yes

### 40-081: Each section's splices are read back and fixed or undone before they are offered.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Every card cut inside is shown to the assistant as its kept words with a mark at each seam, and it answers per card "ok" or "fix" with new ranges. A fix passes the same rules (40-080); a fix that fails keeps the card whole ("kept whole: the splice did not read"). If the read-back cannot be done, the cuts stand and are marked "unchecked".
- Exact: yes

### 40-082: Shortening follows the studio's rubric of what to keep and what to cut first, and an episode can have its own.
- Where: cards-board / The owner's editorial rules
- What: Keep, in order of stubbornness: the sentence a paragraph walks towards, concrete details, turns, first mentions later cards depend on, spans the editor highlighted, a section's summary card and its last card. Cut first: restatement, scene-setting the picture already covers, the second of two examples, throat-clearing, and cards in a "maybe" colour. Never remove a noted card, a section's last card, or a card another kept card refers back to. Every removal has a one-clause reason in the editor's terms. An episode can carry its own rubric in place of this (empty or unreadable falls back); changing it starts a new chat conversation.

### 40-083: Every removal is its own suggestion, which the editor accepts, dismisses or undoes one at a time.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Nothing is removed by the shortening itself. Each removed card or piece becomes a suggestion with a title ("drop: <reason>", "splice: <reason>" or "drop the section: <reason>"), the reason, the kind, the seconds it saves, the words, the person, the section, where in the clip it came from and which card it follows. Each suggestion is matched against the cut as it stands every time, so accepting one does not make the others stale. One that can no longer be made is "invalid" with its reason, for example 'the words "..." are not one card in this cut any more, so this suggestion cannot be made. Dismiss it, or ask for the pass again.', "that removal does not say where in the clip it came from, so it cannot be made now", "[uid] has no word timing any more, so there is nothing in it to cut on", 'the words "..." are not in [uid] any more'. States: pending, accepted, dismissed, invalid. Accepting one applies it as its own change with its own undo: a whole card goes onto the shelf; a piece is split out and goes onto the shelf. Refusals: "that suggestion is not part of this shortening any more", "that one has been accepted already", "that one was dismissed: put it back on the table before accepting it". Dismissing keeps it on the record, dimmed; dismissing again restores it; an accepted one cannot be dismissed ("that one has been accepted already: undo it to put the words back in the cut"). Undoing an accepted one makes it pending again.
- Exact: yes

### 40-084: The editor can take every remaining suggestion at once, as one change with one undo.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: All pending suggestions are applied together, grouped per card (several pieces of one card become one cut keeping what all of them leave). Refusals: "there is no suggestion left to take: every one has been accepted or dismissed"; "none of those suggestions can be made to this cut list any more".
- Exact: yes

### 40-085: Shortening shows what the cut runs now and where it would land.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Shown: the length now, where it lands with the accepted suggestions only, and where it would land if every pending one were taken. The summary reads "N suggestion(s), <length> if you take them all", plus ", asked for <target>" when that is more than 10% (and at least 1 second) away from the target.
- Exact: yes

### 40-086: A shortening review is the same in every window and on the phone.
- Where: cards-editor / Working offline, connections and several devices
- What: The whole review is kept with the cut list's chat history, so a reload, a second window or a phone sees the same suggestions in the same states, and changes from two places at once are taken one after the other.

### 40-087: The cut can be given scores once, after which a target length becomes an instant slider.
- Where: cards-editor / Shortening, the edit chat and the assistant
- What: Once per cut list, the assistant scores every card from 0 ("nobody would miss it") to 1 ("the cut is not this cut without it"), with a kind, the cards it depends on and a reason (giving up after 15 minutes). Refusals: "there are no cards to score"; "the scores could not be saved: <message>". Given a target, the slider at once (no waiting on the assistant) proposes dropping the lowest-scored cards, longest first among equal scores, until the target is reached; never a card with a note, a section's last card, or a card a remaining card depends on; a card unscored or whose words changed since scoring stays. It applies nothing, and shows a keep score per remaining card as a heat display. Refusals: "say the length to land on, like 12:00"; "this cut list has no scores yet - build them once and the target becomes a slider".
- Flag: undetermined: whether the present editor offers the slider is not known from this section.
- Exact: yes
